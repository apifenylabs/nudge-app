### Seoul with Kids: The Complete Family Travel Guide

Seoul is one of Asia's most underrated family destinations. It blends ancient palaces with futuristic tech, offers incredibly safe public spaces, and — perhaps most importantly — Korean kids are welcome everywhere. From **toddlers to teenagers**, Seoul delivers a travel experience that keeps everyone entertained without breaking the bank.

This **5-day Seoul family itinerary** covers the must-see sights, kid-approved activities, and local food experiences that make South Korea's capital a fantastic choice for your next Asian family adventure.

---

### Day 1: Palaces, Hanbok, and Insadong Culture

Start your Seoul family trip with a deep dive into Korean history — made fun for kids.

**Gyeongbokgung Palace** is the largest and most impressive of Seoul's five grand palaces. Arrive early (opens 9:00 AM) to beat the crowds and catch the **changing of the royal guard ceremony** at the main gate (10:00 AM and 2:00 PM). Kids love the colourful costumes and drumming.

**The Hanbok Experience:** Rent traditional Korean hanbok from one of the dozens of shops near the palace entrance. Hanbok rental costs around **₩15,000–25,000 ($11–18 USD)** per person for 2–4 hours, and children's sizes are readily available. Best of all: wearing hanbok grants **free admission** to all five Seoul palaces. It's a brilliant money-saving tip and makes for unforgettable family photos.

**Kid-Friendly Palace Tips:**
- **Strollers:** Gyeongbokgung has paved pathways but some gravel sections. Lightweight strollers are fine.
- **Restrooms:** Clean restrooms are located near each main hall and at the ticket office.
- **Age Range:** All ages. Toddlers love running in the open courtyards; older kids enjoy the history museum inside.
- **Food:** Pack snacks. The palace has limited cafe options, but the **Samcheong-dong** neighbourhood just east is full of kid-friendly cafes.

**Afternoon in Insadong:** Walk from the palace to Insadong, Seoul's traditional culture street. Visit **Ssamziegil**, a unique multi-level shopping complex with a spiral ramp (stroller-friendly!) full of quirky craft shops. Kids enjoy the calligraphy souvenir stalls and the **ice cream in a cup shaped like poop** — a silly Korean novelty that children find hilarious.

**Dinner:**
- **Gwangjang Market** (10 mins by taxi) — one of Seoul's oldest traditional markets. Try **mayak gimbap** (addictive mini seaweed rice rolls), **bindaetteok** (mung bean pancakes), and **tteokbokki** (spicy rice cakes — order "mild" for kids). Budget: **₩10,000–15,000 ($7–11 USD)** per person.

### Day 2: Kid-Friendly Museums and N Seoul Tower

**Morning — National Museum of Korea:** This world-class museum is surprisingly fantastic for children. The **Children's Museum** (separate wing) is an interactive wonderland with hands-on exhibits about Korean history, archaeology, and traditional arts. Admission is **free** (reserve timed tickets online in advance). Plan 2–3 hours.

**Stroller Access:** Excellent — fully wheelchair and stroller accessible. Free stroller rental available at the information desk.

**Lunch at Yongsan Family Park:** Pack a picnic or eat at the museum cafeteria (decent kids' menu with bibimbap and noodles). The adjacent park has playground equipment and shaded benches.

**Afternoon — N Seoul Tower:** Head to Namsan Mountain for panoramic views of Seoul. Take the **Namsan Cable Car** from Myeongdong (₩14,000 / $10 USD round trip adult, ₩10,000 / $7 USD child). The cable car is a thrill for kids — glass cabins offering sweeping views as you ascend.

**Tip:** Visit 1 hour before sunset to see Seoul in daylight and watch the city lights flicker on. The **Locks of Love** area (thousands of padlocks left by couples) is entertaining for older kids and teens.

**Dinner in Myeongdong:** Myeongdong's street food stalls are legendary. Let kids try:
- **Egg bread (gyeran-ppang)** — a whole egg baked into sweet bread
- **Korean corn dogs** — mozzarella-filled, coated in sugar
- **Hotteok** — sweet Korean pancakes filled with cinnamon and honey
- **Budget:** Street snacks run ₩2,000–5,000 ($1.50–$4 USD) each

### Day 3: COEX Aquarium, K-Pop, and Gangnam

**Morning — COEX Aquarium:** Seoul's largest aquarium houses over 650 species across 14 themed zones. The 85-metre underwater tunnel is mesmerising for all ages. Kids can touch starfish and sea cucumbers at the interactive pool. **Admission:** ₩32,000 adult / ₩26,000 child ($23 / $19 USD). Book online for 15% discount.

**Stroller Access:** Excellent. The route is one-way with ramps throughout.

**Lunch at COEX Mall:** Eat at **Food Empire** (food court with Korean, Japanese, Western options) or **Sarabeth's** for reliable kid-friendly brunch.

**Afternoon — K-Pop Experience at SMTOWN:** For families with **music-loving tweens and teens**, the SM Entertainment complex in Gangnam is a must. Visit **SMTOWN@coex Artium** where you can:
- Record a virtual music video with K-pop idols
- Try the hologram concert experience
- Shop official merchandise
- Visit the K-pop photo zone

If K-pop isn't your family's thing, **Bongeunsa Temple** (just across from COEX) offers a serene escape — a 1,200-year-old Buddhist temple surrounded by skyscrapers.

**Late Afternoon — Han River Bike Ride:** Rent family bikes at **Yeouido Hangang Park** (₩3,000–10,000 / $2–7 USD per hour). The riverside paths are flat, wide, and perfect for kids. In summer, rent a **banana boat** or pedalo.

**Dinner:** **Mapo Jeong Daepo** (Gangnam) for all-you-can-eat Korean BBQ. Kids love grilling at the table. Look for the **kids' bulgogi set** — sweet marinated beef that even picky eaters enjoy. Budget: **₩25,000–35,000 ($18–25 USD)** per adult for unlimited BBQ.

### Day 4: Lotte World — Seoul's Ultimate Theme Park

Devote an entire day to **Lotte World**, one of the world's largest indoor theme parks. It's divided into two zones: **Lotte World Adventure** (indoor, year-round) and **Magic Island** (outdoor, seasonal).

**Why It Works for Families:**
- **Indoor zone** means rain is never a problem
- **Under-7 section** (Kidstoria) with gentle rides like balloon rides and carousels
- **Teen-friendly thrills:** Gyro Drop, Atlantis Adventure roller coaster, and the Comet roller coaster
- **Strollers:** Available for rent (₩5,000 / $4 USD); all indoor areas are stroller accessible
- **Nursing room** and multiple family restrooms inside

**Admission:** ₩62,000 adult / ₩54,000 child ($45 / $39 USD). **Save 30–40%** by booking combo tickets that include the **Seoul Sky Observatory** in Lotte World Tower (same complex). Afternoon Magic Pass (from 4 PM) costs just ₩36,000 ($26 USD).

**Dinner in Lotte World Mall:** Endless dining options on the lower floors — try **Paulin Pan** (French-Japanese bakery), **Myth Jokbal** (Korean braised pork trotters, surprisingly loved by kids), or **Cheese Room** for pizza and pasta.

### Day 5: Bukchon Hanok Village and Departure

**Morning — Bukchon Hanok Village:** Wander through this picturesque neighbourhood of traditional Korean houses (hanok) nestled between Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung palaces. The sloping alleyways with tiled rooftops offer some of Seoul's most photogenic views.

**Kid Tips:**
- Visit before 10 AM when it's quiet (residents still live here)
- Stop at **Bukchon Traditional Culture Centre** for free craft activities (paper fan making, traditional knotting)
- Watch for **hanok cafes** — many serve traditional rice punch (sikhye) and sweet rice cakes that kids enjoy

**Lunch — Samgyetang (Ginseng Chicken Soup):** A nourishing Korean classic that's gentle on young stomachs. Try **Tosokchon Samgyetang** near Gyeongbokgung — they stuff a whole chicken with rice, ginseng, and jujubes. Kids portion available. Budget: ₩18,000 ($13 USD) per bowl.

**Departure:** Seoul's Incheon Airport is 60–90 minutes from central Seoul. Take the **AREX Express Train** from Seoul Station (₩9,500 / $7 USD, 43 minutes) — it's faster and cheaper than taxis (₩80,000 / $58 USD).

### Getting Around Seoul with Kids

Seoul's subway system is excellent for families:
- **T-money card** — buy at any convenience store (₩2,500 / $2 USD), tap for all buses and subways
- **Priority seats for families** — look for pink seats marked for pregnant women and families with young children
- **Elevators** — every station has at least one; look for the elevator symbol on station maps
- **Subway apps** — download **KakaoMap** or **Naver Map** (Google Maps is unreliable for walking in Seoul)
- **Taxis** — orange ("general") and black ("deluxe") taxis. General is fine for families. Flagfall starts at ₩3,800 ($3 USD).

### When to Visit Seoul with Kids

- **Spring (March–May):** Cherry blossoms in April are stunning. Moderate temperatures (15–22°C). Peak season — book early.
- **Autumn (September–November):** Crisp air, golden foliage. Best overall season. September still warm enough for outdoor pools.
- **Summer (June–August):** Hot and humid (28–35°C) with monsoon rains in July. Lotte World indoor zone is a lifesaver.
- **Winter (November–February):** Cold (-5 to 5°C) but crowds are thin. Christmas decorations are lovely, and indoor attractions are perfect.

### Final Tips for Seoul with Kids

- **Toilets:** Seoul has excellent public restrooms — in subway stations, parks, and convenience stores. Carry your own toilet paper and hand sanitiser just in case.
- **WiFi:** Free public WiFi is everywhere. Rent a **portable WiFi egg** at Incheon Airport (₩5,000–8,000 / $4–6 USD per day) or get a local SIM.
- **Napping toddlers:** Many Korean jjimjilbangs (bathhouses) — like **Dragon Hill Spa** — have sleeping areas, family rooms, and heated floors. A unique cultural experience while your little one rests.
- **Picky eaters:** Korean chain restaurants like **BHC Chicken** (fried chicken), **School Food** (Korean-style pasta and rice balls), and **Paris Baguette** (pastries and sandwiches) are safe bets.

Seoul might not be the first city that comes to mind for a family trip to Asia, but it absolutely should be. Safe, affordable, endlessly entertaining, and welcoming to children — Seoul is a hidden gem waiting to be discovered by your family.
