# Hanoi with Kids — Best Things to Do in Vietnam's Capital

Hanoi is a city of contrasts. Ancient temples sit next to French colonial buildings. Street food stalls thrive on sidewalks inches away from motorbikes. The Old Quarter is a maze of narrow streets where the action never stops.

It's also a fantastic, chaotic, unforgettable city for families. Hanoi is safe, cheap, and full of experiences that kids find magical — water puppet shows, egg coffee, cycled tours through tiny alleys, and boat rides on the Red River Delta.

The key to Hanoi with kids is pace. Don't try to cover too much. The heat, the noise, and the traffic can be overwhelming. Plan for 2-3 activities a day with plenty of rest and hydration breaks.

## Top Family Activities in Hanoi

### 1. Water Puppet Theatre (Thang Long Theatre)

**Best for:** All ages
**Price:** ~$5-8/adult, ~$3-5/kid
**Time needed:** 50 minutes (one show)

Hanoi's most unique cultural experience. Water puppetry is a 1,000-year-old Vietnamese art form performed in a pool of water. Wooden puppets controlled by hidden puppeteers act out folk tales. The live traditional music accompaniment is beautiful.

Showtimes: Daily at 3:00 PM, 4:00 PM, 5:00 PM, 6:30 PM, and 8:00 PM.

**Family Tip:** Book tickets in advance (especially for evening shows) — they sell out. Arrive 15 minutes early for good seats. The narration is in English and Vietnamese alternating stretches. There's a nice toy shop and ice cream stand across the street.

### 2. Hoan Kiem Lake & Ngoc Son Temple

**Best for:** All ages
**Price:** FREE (walking around), ~$1/adult (temple entrance)
**Time needed:** 1-2 hours

The heart of Hanoi. A peaceful lake in the middle of the chaotic city. Walk the perimeter (about 1.7 km), watch locals doing morning tai chi, and cross the iconic red Huc Bridge to Ngoc Son Temple on a small island.

**Family Tip:** The lake is most magical at sunrise (5:30-6:30 AM) when locals exercise. For normal hours, go in late afternoon (4-6 PM) when it's cooler and the setting sun creates beautiful reflections. The streets around the lake are closed to traffic on weekends (Friday 7 PM to Sunday midnight) — a massive street party takes over.

### 3. Hanoi Old Quarter Walking Tour

**Best for:** Ages 5+
**Price:** FREE (self-guided), ~$15-20/person (guided tour)
**Time needed:** 2-3 hours

The Old Quarter is a maze of 36 narrow streets, each historically named after the trade that took place there (Silk Street, Paper Street, Fish Street). Today they're filled with shops, cafes, street food, and motorbikes.

**Kid-friendly route:**
- Start at **Hoan Kiem Lake** (north side)
- Walk through **Hang Gai Street** (silk and tailoring)
- Turn onto **Hang Bac Street** (silversmiths — kids enjoy watching artisans)
- Stop for egg coffee at **Giang Café** (famous, very kid-friendly)
- Continue to **Dong Xuan Market** (massive covered market, toys and snacks)

**Family Tip:** Use a luggage carrier stroller instead of a lightweight umbrella stroller — the uneven sidewalks and curbs are brutal on tiny wheels. Cross streets slowly and steadily — motorbikes flow around you, not through you.

### 4. Temple of Literature (Van Mieu-Quoc Tu Giam)

**Best for:** Ages 6+
**Price:** ~$2/adult, ~$0.50/kid
**Time needed:** 1-1.5 hours

Vietnam's first national university, founded in 1076. A beautiful complex of courtyards, ponds, pavilions, and ancient stone stelae mounted on tortoises — each one containing the name of a doctoral graduate. The gardens are peaceful and well-shaded.

**Family Tip:** The turtle stelae are a highlight — kids can touch them for good luck. The courtyards are open and perfect for running. Go early (opens 8 AM) to beat the heat and crowds.

### 5. Vietnamese Cooking Class (Kid-Friendly)

**Best for:** Ages 6+
**Price:** $25-35/person (half day including market tour)
**Time needed:** 3-4 hours

Hanoi has excellent family-friendly cooking classes. Most include a visit to a local market where kids can see (and smell) exotic ingredients, then a hands-on session making spring rolls, pho, or banh xeo (Vietnamese crepes).

**Recommended schools:**
- **Hanoi Cooking Centre** — Excellent family classes, English-speaking chefs, gorgeous colonial setting
- **Giang Café Cooking Class** — Smaller, more intimate, includes the famous egg coffee recipe
- **Apron Up Cooking Class** — Very kid-friendly, supportive staff

**Family Tip:** Morning classes (8:30 AM) include the market tour while it's still cool. Most schools let children as young as 6 participate with supervision.

### 6. Train Street (Train Alley)

**Best for:** Ages 5+
**Price:** FREE (but cafes charge for a seat)
**Time needed:** 30-45 minutes

One of Hanoi's most Instagrammable spots — a narrow alley where a train passes within inches of the houses on both sides. Locals have set up cafe tables right next to the tracks.

**The train schedule (approximate — check on arrival):**
- 3:30 PM (weekdays)
- 7:00 PM (weekdays)
- 8:00 AM, 11:30 AM, 3:30 PM, 7:30 PM (weekends)

**Family Tip:** The train passes VERY close. Hold kids firmly and stay behind the white line. The most famous section is at Le Duan Street entrance (near the Hanoi Railway Station). Police occasionally close the track to tourists — ask your hotel for the latest situation.

### 7. Bat Trang Pottery Village

**Best for:** Ages 4+
**Price:** FREE to visit; pottery making ~$3-5/person
**Time needed:** 2-3 hours (including transport)

A traditional pottery village about 15 km from central Hanoi (30-40 minute drive). Kids can try making their own pottery on a wheel, paint pre-made pieces, and walk through showrooms of beautiful ceramics.

**Family Tip:** Take a Grab car (~$10-15 round trip). The village is tourist-friendly and doesn't pressure you into buying. The pottery-making experience is the best activity — messy but fun.

## Best Family Hotels in Hanoi

### Luxury Choice
**Sofitel Legend Metropole Hanoi** — $200-400/night. Historic colonial hotel with a pool, kids' club, and excellent family service. The bunker tour from the Vietnam War era is fascinating (free for hotel guests).

### Mid-Range Excellence
**Hanoi La Siesta Hotel Trendy** — $50-90/night, Old Quarter. Excellent value. Family rooms sleep 4, rooftop pool, complimentary afternoon tea, and walking distance to Hoan Kiem Lake. Our top mid-range pick.

**The Oriental Jade Hotel** — $60-100/night, near Hoan Kiem Lake. Modern rooms, rooftop infinity pool with lake views, spa, and excellent family-friendly service.

### Budget-Friendly
**Adonis Hanoi Hotel** — $25-40/night, Old Quarter. Clean and central. Basic but comfortable rooms. Breakfast included. Best for families on a shoe-string.

**Golden Lotus Hotel** — $35-50/night, near Old Quarter. Good location, helpful staff, reasonable rooms with AC. A solid budget option.

## Practical Tips for Hanoi with Kids

- **Traffic is intense** — Cross streets by walking slowly and steadily. Motorbikes flow around you. Hold kids' hands at all times on streets
- **Heat is serious** — Hanoi hits 35-40°C from June to August. Plan activities in 2-hour blocks with AC breaks
- **Air quality varies** — Winter (Nov-Feb) can have poor air quality. Check AQI and pack masks if needed
- **Tap water is not drinkable** — Buy bottled water everywhere ($0.20-0.50)
- **Street food is safe** — Eat at busy stalls with high turnover. Avoid raw vegetables if concerned
- **Dengue mosquitoes exist** — Use DEET repellent, especially in the Old Quarter and near lakes
- **Negotiate taxi prices** — Use Grab for fixed pricing. Taxis from the airport should be $10-12
- **Book Halong Bay tours carefully** — 2-day/1-night cruises are better with kids than rushed day trips

## Plan Your Trip

For a family of four, budget **$100-180/day** for a very comfortable Hanoi trip (mid-range hotel, restaurant meals, 1 paid activity, Grab transport). It's one of the cheapest capital cities in Asia.

**Best time to visit:** September-November or March-April — mild temperatures (22-30°C), low humidity, blue skies. Avoid June-August (sweltering) and December-February (damp, chilly, poor air quality).

**Sample 3-night itinerary:**
- Day 1: Arrive, Hoan Kiem Lake walk, water puppet show, dinner in Old Quarter
- Day 2: Temple of Literature (morning), cooking class (afternoon), Train Street (evening)
- Day 3: Bat Trang pottery village (morning), egg coffee at Giang Café, depart or continue to Halong Bay
