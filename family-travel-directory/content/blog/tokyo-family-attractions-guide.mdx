# Tokyo Family Attractions Guide — Best Things to Do with Kids in Tokyo 2026

Tokyo is a dream destination for families. It's impossibly clean, hyper-efficient, incredibly safe, and full of experiences that delight kids and adults alike. From towering robot-filled arcades to serene temples and neon-lit districts, Tokyo offers sensory overload in the best possible way.

The catch? It can be overwhelming. The sheer size of the city, the crowded trains during rush hour, and the language barrier can feel daunting. But with a solid itinerary and a few insider tips, Tokyo is one of the most rewarding family destinations in Asia.

---

## Top Family Attractions in Tokyo

### 1. Tokyo Disneyland & Tokyo DisneySea

**Best for:** Ages 3+
**Price:** ~$55-65/adult, ~$40-50/kid (advance booking required)
**Time needed:** Full day each

Two world-class parks, but **DisneySea** is the standout — unique to Japan and widely considered the most beautiful Disney park on earth. It has a more adventurous, immersive feel with seven ports of call themed around ocean exploration.

Disneyland is more classic: Cinderella's Castle, Pooh's Hunny Hunt (best Winnie the Pooh ride anywhere), and the nostalgic World Bazaar.

**Family Tip:** Book tickets on the official site or Klook at least 2-4 weeks ahead — same-day tickets sell out. Go on a weekday. Download the Tokyo Disney Resort app for ride wait times and standby passes.

### 2. teamLab Planets & teamLab Borderless

**Best for:** Ages 4+
**Price:** ~$25-30/adult, ~$15-20/kid (ages 4-12)
**Time needed:** 1.5-2 hours

Immersive digital art installations that feel like walking through a dream. teamLab Planets (Toyosu) involves walking through water, climbing over soft hills, and standing in a room full of floating flowers. Borderless (Azabudai Hills) is a massive, interconnected digital universe.

**Family Tip:** Planets is more linear and easier with smaller kids (sit on the floor, touch the projections). Kids under 4 are free at Borderless. Book time-slot tickets in advance.

### 3. Ueno Zoo & Ueno Park

**Best for:** All ages
**Price:** ~$3/adult, FREE for kids 12 and under
**Time needed:** 2-3 hours

Japan's oldest zoo, home to giant pandas, Sumatran tigers, and a five-story pagoda. It's compact and manageable for little legs. Ueno Park itself has playgrounds, pedal boats on Shinobazu Pond, street musicians, and food stalls.

**Family Tip:** Go early (opens 9:30 AM) when animals are active. Combine with the National Museum of Nature and Science right next door — the dinosaur skeletons are a huge hit.

### 4. Sanrio Puroland (Hello Kitty Theme Park)

**Best for:** Ages 2-8 (especially Hello Kitty fans)
**Price:** ~$30-35/adult, ~$25-30/kid
**Time needed:** 3-4 hours

An indoor theme park in Tama Center (30 min from Shinjuku) dedicated to Hello Kitty, Gudetama, Pompompurin, and other Sanrio characters. Indoor parade, gentle rides, character meet-and-greets, and a Hello Kitty-themed cafe.

**Family Tip:** The ticket includes unlimited rides. Check the character greeting schedule when you arrive. Thursdays and Fridays are quietest.

### 5. Odaiba — Family Entertainment Island

**Best for:** All ages
**Price:** Varies by attraction (many free)
**Time needed:** Half day to full day

A man-made island in Tokyo Bay packed with family-friendly attractions:
- **Joypolis** ($35/adult) — Sega indoor theme park with virtual reality rides
- **TeamLab Borderless** — See above
- **Palette Town Ferris Wheel** — Great city views
- **LEGOLAND Discovery Center** ($20/kid) — Perfect for ages 3-10
- **Odaiba Beach** — Man-made beach, shallow swimming area

**Family Tip:** Take the Yurikamome line from Shimbashi — it's an elevated monorail with spectacular bay views. Kids love riding in the front car.

### 6. Ghibli Museum

**Best for:** Ages 5+ (fans of Studio Ghibli films)
**Price:** ~$10/adult (ages 19+), ~$5/kid (ages 4-18). Booking is essential.
**Time needed:** 2-3 hours

A whimsical museum in Mitaka dedicated to Hayao Miyazaki's Studio Ghibli. No "rides" — it's more like stepping into a Ghibli film. A spiral staircase inside a giant robot, a life-sized Catbus (kids only), and original sketches from My Neighbor Totoro and Spirited Away.

**Family Tip:** Tickets sell out months in advance. Book through the official Lawson site or Viator at least 2-3 months ahead. Inside photography is not allowed — enjoy the moment.

## Best Family Hotels in Tokyo

### Luxury Choice
**Park Hyatt Tokyo** — $500-900/night, Shinjuku. Iconic hotel from Lost in Translation. Spacious rooms by Tokyo standards, indoor pool, stunning city views. Best for older kids who can appreciate the design.

### Mid-Range Excellence
**Keio Plaza Hotel Tokyo** — $180-350/night, Shinjuku. Family rooms sleep 3-4, free shuttle to Disneyland, Hello Kitty-themed rooms available. Our top mid-range family pick.

**Mitsui Garden Hotel Ginza Premier** — $150-300/night, Ginza. Compact but elegant rooms, great location near shopping and Tsukiji Outer Market. Rooftop bar has incredible views.

### Budget-Friendly
**APA Hotel Shinjuku Gyoenmae** — $60-100/night. Small rooms but clean and well-located. Best for families who just need a bed and shower.

**Tokyu Stay Shibuya** — $100-160/night. Slightly larger rooms than average, some with washer/dryers. Great for longer stays.

## Practical Tips for Tokyo with Kids

- **Rush hour is real** — Avoid trains 7:30-9 AM and 5:30-7 PM. Strollers are difficult during these times
- **Bring a collapsible stroller** — Many subway stations have limited elevators. A light umbrella stroller works best
- **Convenience stores are your friend** — 7-Eleven, Lawson, and Family Mart have great onigiri, sandwiches, and drinks for quick kid-friendly meals
- **Pokémon Centers are everywhere** — There's one in Shibuya, Ikebukuro, Tokyo Station, and Skytree. Free entry, great for a 20-minute break
- **Get an IC card** — Suica or Pasmo (load at any station machine). Tap for trains and convenience stores. Kids 6-11 get a 50% discount
- **Pack comfortable shoes** — You'll walk 10,000-20,000 steps a day in Tokyo
- **Google Maps works flawlessly** — For trains, buses, and walking directions
- **Book everything in advance** — Disney, Ghibli, teamLab, and popular restaurants book out weeks ahead

## Plan Your Trip

For a family of four, budget $300-500/day (mid-range hotel, convenience store breakfasts, casual restaurants, 1-2 attractions). A 5-day Tokyo trip with a mid-range hotel and basic attractions runs about $1,500-2,500 per person including flights from nearby Asian hubs.

**Best time to visit:** March-May (cherry blossom) or October-November (autumn foliage). Avoid August (brutal heat and humidity) and Golden Week (late April-early May, everything is packed).

Book hotels on [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com) and attraction tickets on [Klook](https://www.klook.com) for the best advance prices. Get your IC card at the airport before heading into the city.

Tokyo is overwhelming, exhausting, and absolutely magical for families. Go with low expectations about covering everything — pick two or three districts per day and let the kids set the pace.
