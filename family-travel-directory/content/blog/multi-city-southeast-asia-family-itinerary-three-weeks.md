### The Ultimate 3-Week Southeast Asia Family Itinerary

You want to show your family Southeast Asia — the golden temples, floating markets, ancient temples, and lush rice terraces. But where do you start? How many destinations can you realistically visit in three weeks without burning everyone out?

This itinerary has been crafted for **families who want to see the highlights without the headaches**. It covers three countries (Thailand, Laos, and Vietnam — with Cambodia as a bonus), using a logical north-south loop that minimises backtracking. We've factored in **travel days, rest days, and buffer time** for when things don't go perfectly (and with kids, they won't).

This is the Classic Southeast Asia Family Loop — tested by dozens of travelling families and refined for maximum joy, minimum meltdowns.

---

### The Route at a Glance

**Bangkok (3 nights) → Chiang Mai (3 nights) → Luang Prabang, Laos (3 nights) → Hanoi (2 nights) → Halong Bay (2 nights / 1 day cruise) → Hoi An (3 nights) → Siem Reap, Cambodia (3 nights) → Depart**

**Total:** 21 days | **Countries:** Thailand, Laos, Vietnam, Cambodia | **Best for:** Kids aged 5–15

**When to Go:** **November–February** (dry season across all four countries). March–April is hotter but still workable. Avoid June–October (monsoon in Laos, Vietnam, and Cambodia).

### Week 1: Thailand (Bangkok + Chiang Mai)

#### Days 1–3: Bangkok

**Where to Stay:** **Sukhumvit area** (best for families — central, connected by BTS Skytrain, endless dining options). Recommended: **Adelphi Suites** (serviced apartments with kitchenettes, pool, from $65/night) or **Centre Point Sukhumvit 10** (family rooms, pool, from $55/night).

**Day 1: Arrival and Orientation**

- Arrive at Suvarnabhumi Airport. **Grab a taxi from the official counter** (Level 1, Gate 7) — pay 1,000–1,500 THB ($28–42 USD) flat rate to Sukhumvit. Avoid meterless taxis.
- **Afternoon:** Visit **Siam Paragon** — not just shopping. The basement houses **Sea Life Bangkok Ocean World**, an excellent aquarium that kids love. **Admission:** 1,190 THB ($33 USD) adult, 890 THB ($25 USD) child.
- **Evening stroll:** **Asiatique Night Market** (riverside, airy, stroller-friendly). Dinner at the food court — try pad thai, mango sticky rice, and fresh fruit shakes.

**Day 2: Temples + Floating Market**

- **Morning:** **Grand Palace + Wat Phra Kaew** — the most iconic Bangkok sight. Arrive at 8:30 AM (opens 8:30). Strict dress code (cover shoulders and knees). Young kids can get hot — bring a portable fan. **Admission:** 500 THB ($14 USD) adult — **kids under 120cm free**.
- **Lunch:** **Tha Maharaj** riverside plaza (5 minutes from Grand Palace) — air-conditioned food court with family-friendly options.
- **Afternoon (choose one):**
  - **Wat Pho** (Reclining Buddha) — 5-minute walk from Grand Palace. Impressive 46-metre Buddha, less crowded than the Grand Palace.
  - **Damnoen Saduak Floating Market** (1.5 hours from Bangkok, half-day tour). Early start (6 AM pickup). Kids love the boat ride and watching vendors sell from sampans. Tour cost: 600–1,000 THB ($17–28 USD) per person including hotel pickup.

**Day 3: Kid-Focused Bangkok**

- **Morning:** **KidZania Bangkok** (Siam Paragon) — an interactive mini-city where kids role-play as doctors, pilots, firefighters, and chefs. 3-hour session. Ages 4–14. **Admission:** 590–990 THB ($16–27 USD) depending on age.
- **Alternative:** **Chatuchak Weekend Market** (if it's Saturday or Sunday) — the world's largest weekend market. Entertaining chaos for older kids. Stop at the toy section (Section 5) and the pet section (Section 13, where kids can hold rabbits).
- **Evening:** Take the **Chao Phraya Tourist Boat** (blue flag) for a cheap river cruise. **Cost:** 40 THB ($1.10 USD) per person for a day pass.

#### Days 4–6: Chiang Mai

**Travel:** 1-hour flight from Bangkok. **Nok Air**, **Air Asia**, and **Thai Lion** fly frequently. Book 4–6 weeks ahead for fares around 1,000–1,500 THB ($28–42 USD) per person.

**Where to Stay:** Old City (inside the moat) is most convenient for temples and night markets. **Baan Orapin** (charming boutique hotel, pool, from $45/night) or **The Inside House** (pool villas, from $80/night).

**Day 4: Arrival + Night Market**

- **Morning:** Arrive Chiang Mai, check in, lunch at **Khao Soi Khun Yai** (famous khao soi — curry noodle soup, mild enough for kids).
- **Afternoon:** Visit **Wat Chedi Luang** (the giant ruined chedi in the Old City). Kids love the "monkey school" demo at the nearby temple.
- **Evening:** **Sunday Night Market** (if Sunday) or **Night Bazaar** (daily). Let kids try **roti** (Thai pancake with banana and Nutella) and pick up cheap souvenirs.

**Day 5: Elephant Sanctuary (Full Day)**

This is the highlight for most families. Choose an **ethical sanctuary** — these are non-riding, non-bathing, observation-only experiences that prioritise elephant welfare.

**Top Pick:** **Elephant Nature Park** (the gold standard, founded by Lek Chailert). Day visit costs 2,500 THB ($70 USD) per adult, 1,500 THB ($42 USD) per child, including hotel transfer, lunch, and guide. **Minimum age:** 4 years old for full-day, no minimum for half-day.

**What You'll Do:**
- Feed elephants (bananas and watermelon)
- Observe them in their natural herd
- Learn about elephant conservation
- River viewing from a distance
- Volunteer in the afternoon (sugar cane cutting, corn husking)

**What NOT to do:** Avoid sanctuaries that offer elephant rides, elephant painting, or bathing — these involve coercion and often cruel training methods.

**Day 6: Doi Suthep + Thai Cooking Class**

- **Morning:** **Doi Suthep Temple** — the golden temple atop a mountain. Take the **red songthaew** (shared pickup truck) from the Old City for 60 THB ($1.70 USD) per person. The 306-step naga staircase is a challenge for little legs — take the tram (20 THB / $0.55 USD) up instead.
- **Afternoon:** **Family Thai cooking class** — many Chiang Mai cooking schools welcome kids. **Thai Farm Cooking School** offers a family-friendly half-day class including a market visit to buy ingredients. Kids punch basil in a mortar, stir noodles, and taste-test everything. **Cost:** 1,200 THB ($34 USD) per adult, 800 THB ($22 USD) per child.

### Week 2: Laos (Luang Prabang) + Vietnam (Hanoi + Halong Bay)

#### Days 7–9: Luang Prabang, Laos

**Travel:** Fly from Chiang Mai to Luang Prabang (1 hour, Lao Airlines). Alternatively, take the **slow boat from Huay Xai (2 days)** — adventurous but demanding with young kids. We recommend flying.

**Why Luang Prabang:** This UNESCO-listed town is Southeast Asia's most charming destination — a laid-back blend of French colonial architecture, golden temples, and the Mekong River. It's safe, small, and walkable — perfect for families.

**Where to Stay:** **Madilay Garden Hotel** (family rooms, pool, central, from $40/night) or **My Dream Boutique Resort** (pool villas, from $55/night).

**Must-Do with Kids:**
- **Kuang Si Waterfalls** (45-min drive) — turquoise multi-tiered waterfall with natural pools for swimming. The lower pools are perfect for kids. Entrance: 25,000 LAK ($1.20 USD). Sun Bear Sanctuary at the entrance is free.
- **Alms Giving Ceremony** (dawn, 5:30–6:30 AM) — locals give sticky rice to Buddhist monks. Observe respectfully from a distance. If you want to participate, buy sticky rice from a vendor the night before (20,000 LAK / $1 USD).
- **Mekong River Cruise** — 2-hour sunset cruise on the Mekong. Many operators offer kid-friendly trips with stops at **Pak Ou Caves** (filled with thousands of Buddha statues). Private boat for a family: 500,000–700,000 LAK ($24–34 USD).
- **Night Market** — the Hmong night market sells handmade textiles, lanterns, and local snacks. Let kids try **sai oua** (Lao sausage, mild) and **khao lam** (sticky rice in bamboo).

**Food:** Luang Prabang has excellent French-influenced cuisine. **Tamarind Restaurant** serves Lao tapas (small portions, perfect for kids to try). **Bouang** is a great bakery for breakfast (pain au chocolat, banana bread — a lifesaver for picky eaters).

#### Days 10–11: Hanoi, Vietnam

**Travel:** Fly Luang Prabang to Hanoi (1 hour, Vietnam Airlines or Lao Airlines). Only one direct flight daily — book in advance.

**Where to Stay:** **Old Quarter** (walking distance to most sights). **Hanoi E Central Hotel** (family suites, from $50/night) or **La Siesta Classic Ma May** (boutique, from $60/night).

**Day 10: Hanoi Old Quarter**

- **Morning:** **Hoan Kiem Lake** — cross the iconic **The Huc Bridge** to Ngoc Son Temple. Kids love the giant preserved turtle (a 100-year-old Hoan Kiem turtle) on display at the temple.
- **Afternoon:** **Water Puppet Theatre** (Thang Long Theatre) — a uniquely Vietnamese art form. Puppets dance on water accompanied by live traditional music. Shows at 3:00 PM and 5:00 PM. **Admission:** 100,000 VND ($4 USD) per person. **Duration:** 50 minutes — just right for kids.
- **Evening:** **Bia Hoi Junction** (Luong Ngoc Quyen Street) — sit on tiny plastic stools and drink fresh draught beer (5,000 VND / $0.20 USD per glass). Kids can have fresh sugar cane juice and watch the chaotic street life.

**Day 11: Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum + Train Street**

- **Morning:** **Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum** (free, closed Mon–Fri afternoons and Oct–Nov for maintenance). The imposing building is impressive. **Strollers allowed** in the grounds, not inside the mausoleum (baby carriers okay).
- **Late Morning:** **Train Street** — narrow alley where a train passes within inches of houses. **Check the train schedule** (trains pass roughly at 7:00 AM, 11:30 AM, 3:00 PM, and 7:30 PM). A cafe on Train Street with front-row seats charges 50,000 VND ($2 USD) per drink.
- **Afternoon:** **Temple of Literature** — Vietnam's first university (1076 AD). Beautiful pavilions and gardens. Kids enjoy the stone turtles and the reflecting pond.

**Food for Kids in Hanoi:**
- **Bun cha** (grilled pork with noodles and herbs) — famously eaten by Obama, a kid-friendly dish
- **Pho bo** (beef noodle soup) — mild, warm, and comforting
- **Banh mi** (Vietnamese baguette sandwiches) — available everywhere for 20,000 VND ($0.85 USD)
- **Egg coffee** (for parents) — Hanoi's famous creamy coffee drink

#### Days 12–13: Halong Bay (Overnight Cruise)

**Travel:** 2.5–3 hour bus from Hanoi (booked as a transfer with your cruise operator). Most cruises include pick-up from Hanoi hotels.

**Cruise Selection Tips:**
- **Choose 2-day/1-night cruises** — 3-day/2-night is too long with kids
- **Look for "family-friendly" cruises** that provide kid's meals, life jackets in child sizes, and flexible itineraries
- **Top recommendations:** [Book Halong Bay cruises on Klook](https://www.klook.com/search/?keyword=Halong+Bay+cruise+family&aid=38VWJMX) or [browse options on Viator](https://www.viator.com/search/Halong%20Bay?pid=455806&mcid=42383)
- **Cost:** $150–300 USD per adult, $75–150 USD per child (all-inclusive: meals, kayaking, cave visits, cooking class)

**What to Expect:**
- Board around 12:00 PM, cruise through limestone karsts
- Visit **Sung Sot Cave** (Surprise Cave) — massive chambers with incredible stalactites
- **Kayaking** at a floating village — kids 6+ can ride with parents in 2-person kayaks
- **Squid fishing** in the evening (novelty activity, rarely catch anything)
- **Sunrise tai chi** on the deck (kids enjoy mimicking the movements)
- Return to Hanoi port by 11:00 AM on Day 13

### Week 3: Vietnam (Hoi An) + Cambodia (Siem Reap)

#### Days 14–16: Hoi An, Vietnam

**Travel:** Fly Hanoi to Da Nang (1 hour, Vietnam Airlines or Bamboo Airways from $25 USD), then 40-minute taxi to Hoi An (250,000 VND / $10 USD).

**Where to Stay:** **Ancient House Resort** (family-friendly, two pools, walking distance to Old Town, from $35/night) or **La Siesta Hoi An Resort & Spa** (luxury, by the river, from $80/night).

**Day 14: Hoi An Old Town**

- **Lantern-lit evenings** are the highlight. Hoi An's Old Town is a UNESCO World Heritage site with no cars allowed in the central area.
- **Visit the Japanese Covered Bridge** (built 1593), **Phuc Kien Assembly Hall**, and **Tan Ky Old House**.
- **At night:** Release **floating lanterns** on the river (20,000 VND / $0.85 USD each) — a magical experience for kids.
- **Get custom clothing made** — Hoi An is famous for tailoring. **Bazin Tailor** can make matching family outfits in 24 hours (from $15 per piece).

**Day 15: Cooking Class + Beach Day**

- **Morning:** **Hoi An Cooking Class** — many include a trip to the **Tra Que Vegetable Village** where kids can help plant herbs. The morning market visit is fascinating for kids (exotic fruits, live crabs, and fish). **Cost:** $25–35 USD per adult, $15–20 per child for a 4-hour class.
- **Afternoon:** **An Bang Beach** (15-minute bike ride from town). Gentle waves, soft sand, cheap beach chairs (50,000 VND / $2 USD), and fresh coconut water for 20,000 VND ($0.85 USD).

**Day 16: My Son Sanctuary + Bicycle Tour**

- **Half-day tour to My Son Sanctuary** — the "Angkor Wat of Vietnam," a cluster of Hindu temple ruins built by the Champa Kingdom (4th–14th century). 1-hour drive from Hoi An. **Admission:** 150,000 VND ($6.50 USD) adult, kids under 120cm free.
- **Afternoon:** **Hoi An Countryside Bike Tour** — flat roads through rice paddies and villages. Many tour operators rent bikes with child seats and trailers. **Cost:** 250,000 VND ($11 USD) for a guided 2-hour village tour.

#### Days 17–19: Siem Reap, Cambodia

**Travel:** Fly Da Nang to Siem Reap (1.5 hours, direct flight on Cambodia Angkor Air or Vietnam Airlines from $70 USD per person).

**Where to Stay:** See our Cambodia guide, but family picks are **Templation Hotel** ($80–150/night, giant pool, free kids club) or **Sarai Resort & Spa** ($50–80/night).

**Full Angkor Experience (3 days):** See our dedicated **[Cambodia with Kids: Angkor Wat & Siem Reap Guide](/cambodia-with-kids-angkor-wat-siem-reap-guide)** for the detailed 4-day itinerary. Recommended schedule:

- **Day 17:** Arrive, visit **Angkor National Museum** (air-conditioned primer), evening at **Pub Street**
- **Day 18:** **Angkor Wat** at 7:30 AM + **Ta Prohm** at 10:00 AM. Pool break at noon. **Preah Khan** at 3:30 PM.
- **Day 19:** **Bayon Temple** (dawn) + **Banteay Srei** (morning). Afternoon: **Phare Circus** at 6:00 PM (book ahead).

**Budget Alert:** 3-day Angkor Pass = $62 USD per adult. Kids under 12 enter FREE (passport required for age verification at ticket office).

#### Day 20: Departure

- **Morning:** Last-minute souvenir shopping at **Artisans Angkor** or **Siem Reap Art Center Night Market** (open morning for last calls).
- **Afternoon:** Siem Reap airport is 15 minutes from town. International flights connect directly to Bangkok, Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, and many Asian hubs.

### Pro Tips for a Smooth Multi-City Family Trip

1. **Don't change hotels daily.** Two-night minimum per city. Three nights is ideal.
2. **Plan a rest day every 5–7 days.** Our itinerary has built-in pool/beach days. Use them.
3. **Book flights with carry-on only** if possible. Laundry services ($1–3/kg) are available at all destinations.
4. **Use local SIMs** — buy one at each airport for navigation and translation. Total cost per country: $5–10 USD.
5. **Travel insurance is non-negotiable.** Southeast Asia has excellent hospitals in cities, but evacuation from remote areas isn't cheap.
6. **Download offline maps** (Google Maps offline mode works well). You won't always have data.
7. **Pack half the clothes you think you need.** Laundry is cheap and fast everywhere ($1–3 per kg).
8. **Tuk-tuk negotiation** — agree on the fare before you get in. Use **Grab** (Southeast Asia's Uber) for metered pricing and no haggling.
