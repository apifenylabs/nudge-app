# Ultimate Tokyo Family Itinerary: 7 Days of Fun for Kids & Parents in 2026

Tokyo is one of the most kid-friendly cities on the planet — it's clean, safe, punctual, and bursting with experiences that delight children and adults alike. From hyper-kinetic arcades to serene temple gardens, from robot restaurants to cat cafés, Tokyo delivers a sensory overload that families find absolutely unforgettable.

This **7-day Tokyo family itinerary** is designed for families with kids aged 4-14. It balances big attractions with downtime, indoor activities with outdoor exploring, and iconic sights with hidden gems.

## Before You Go: Essential Planning Tips

**Best time to visit:** March-May (cherry blossoms) or October-November (autumn colors). Avoid August (extreme heat + humidity).

**Getting around:** Get a **Suica or Pasmo card** for each family member (kids 6-11 get half-price child cards). Trains are stroller-friendly but avoid rush hour (7:30-9am and 5-7pm).

**Where to stay:** Stay near **Shinjuku** (best hubbing), **Tokyo Station/Ginza** (luxury), **Ueno** (budget-friendly), or **Odaiba** (Disney proximity).

**Packing essentials:** Comfortable walking shoes, portable snacks (convenience stores are everywhere), a small towel for public restrooms, and a **stroller** even if your kids can usually walk — Tokyo involves *a lot* of walking.

## Day 1: Arrival & Shibuya

**Morning:** Arrive at Narita or Haneda Airport. Take the Narita Express (N'EX) or Keisei Skyliner into the city, depending on where you're staying. A family from Narita to Shinjuku costs about ¥12,000 with reserved seats.

**Afternoon:** Check into your hotel and grab lunch at a nearby convenience store (konbini) — kids love the onigiri, egg sandwiches, and fruit pouches. Take a slow walk to **Shibuya Crossing** to watch the iconic scramble crossing from the second-floor Starbucks (TSUTAYA).

**Evening:** Visit **Hachiko Statue** (the loyal dog monument) and head up to **Shibuya Sky** observation deck for sunset views. The glass-walled corners are a big hit with kids. Have dinner at Shibuya Parco's food court — lots of family-friendly options.

**🍜 Dinner recommendation:** Uobei Shibuya Dogenzaka — conveyor belt sushi where you order via tablet. Fun, fast, and affordable (¥100-300 per plate).

## Day 2: Culture & Nature in Asakusa & Ueno

**Morning:** Head to **Asakusa** and visit **Senso-ji Temple**, Tokyo's oldest temple. Walk through the **Kaminarimon Gate** and down **Nakamise-dori** shopping street — kids love the traditional snacks like ningyo-yaki (little bean pastries) and senbei (rice crackers).

**Midday:** Walk 15 minutes to **Ueno Park**. Visit **Ueno Zoo** (¥600 adults, free for kids under 12) — the giant pandas are the main draw. The zoo is compact and manageable in 2 hours.

**Afternoon:** Explore **Kappabashi Kitchen Town** (near Asakusa), a street lined with shops selling kitchenware, plastic food displays, and chef knives. Let the kids pick out a cute plastic food keychain — a perfect souvenir.

**Evening:** Enjoy dinner near Asakusa. Try **Imahan** for sukiyaki (kid-friendly, they'll love cooking at the table) or grab yakitori from one of the many izakaya.

**💡 Pro tip:** If day two falls on a Sunday, visit the **Ueno Flea Market** — kids love hunting for vintage toys.

---

## Day 3: High-Tech Tokyo — teamLab & Odaiba

**Morning:** Visit **teamLab Borderless** (now relocated to Azabudai Hills as of 2026). This immersive digital art museum is mind-blowing for all ages. The "Floating Flower Garden" and "Sketch Aquarium" (where kids can color fish and see them projected onto walls) are highlights.

**Booking:** Reserve tickets online at least 2 weeks in advance. Morning slots (9:30-10am) are least crowded. Allow 2-3 hours.

**Afternoon:** Take the Yurikamome Line to **Odaiba Island** — the driverless train ride over Rainbow Bridge is an attraction in itself. Visit **DiverCity Tokyo Plaza** to see the life-sized Unicorn Gundam statue (it transforms several times a day).

**Late afternoon:** Choose from:
- **Joypolis** — Indoor Sega theme park with virtual reality rides
- **Legoland Discovery Center** — Indoor Lego playground (great for ages 3-10)
- **Tokyo Trick Art Museum** — Fun optical illusions and photo ops

**Evening:** Dinner at **Aqua City Odaiba** with views of Rainbow Bridge. The food court has everything from ramen to pizza.

## Day 4: Tokyo Disney Resort

Dedicate a full day to **Tokyo Disney Resort**. Choose based on your kids' ages:

**Tokyo Disneyland (best for ages 3-10):**
- Classic fairy-tale atmosphere
- **Pooh's Honey Hunt** — Unique ride not found in other Disney parks
- **Beauty and the Beast** — New in 2020, still a must-do
- **It's a Small World** and **Peter Pan's Flight** — Timeless family favorites
- Prep your **Priority Pass** via the Tokyo Disney Resort App

**Tokyo DisneySea (best for ages 8+):**
- More thrilling rides (Indiana Jones, Tower of Terror, Journey to the Center of the Earth)
- Themed ports of call — Mediterranean Harbor, Arabian Coast, Mermaid Lagoon
- Unique to Tokyo — you won't find anything quite like it anywhere else
- **Fantasy Springs** — The newest expansion area (opened 2024) featuring Peter Pan, Frozen, and Tangled

**🎟️ Ticket tip:** Buy tickets online from the official site. A 1-day pass is ¥7,900-10,900/adult. Kids under 4 are free.

**🍿 Snack hack:** DisneySea has the most creative popcorn flavors in the world — curry, soy sauce, honey, milk tea, and strawberry. Get a souvenir popcorn bucket and let the kids collect different flavors throughout the day.

## Day 5: Anime, Akihabara & Ghibli Museum

**Morning:** Visit the **Ghibli Museum** in Mitaka. This enchanting museum dedicated to Studio Ghibli's animated films is a dream come true for kids and parents alike. Highlights: the life-sized Catbus (kids can climb in), the rooftop garden with a giant Laputa robot, and exclusive short films.

**⚠️ Crucial:** Tickets go on sale on the 10th of each month for the following month and sell out within hours. Use a ticket-buying service (like Viator) if you can't secure them yourself. [Find Ghibli Museum tickets on Viator](https://www.viator.com/search/Ghibli%20Museum?pid=455806&mcid=42383).

**Afternoon:** Head to **Akihabara Electric Town**. Even if you're not into anime, the sheer spectacle of multi-story arcades, electronics shops, and maid cafes is unforgettable. Visit **Yodobashi Camera** for toys and electronics, and **Akihabara Radio Kaikan** for anime figurines.

**Teen must-see:** The **Animate** flagship store — 8 floors of anime, manga, and merch. **Mandarake Complex** for vintage toys.

**Optional:** Visit the **Pokemon Center Mega Tokyo** in Ikebukuro (Sunshine City) if your kids are obsessed — it's the largest Pokemon store in Japan.

**Evening:** Have dinner at a **Character-themed cafe** — the Pokemon Cafe (Nihonbashi) and Kirby Cafe (Tokyo Skytree Town) require reservations months in advance, but there are often same-day walk-up options.

## Day 6: Hakone Day Trip

Escape the city for a day in **Hakone National Park**. Mt. Fuji views on clear days, a ropeway over volcanic valleys, and a pirate ship across Lake Ashi — it's a nature-filled break from Tokyo's neon intensity.

**The classic Hakone Loop:**
1. Take the **Odakyu Romancecar** train from Shinjuku to Hakone-Yumoto (85 minutes, ¥2,300)
2. Switch to the **Hakone Tozan Railway** — switchback train through the mountains
3. **Hakone Tozan Cable Car** up the mountain
4. **Hakone Ropeway** over Owakudani Valley (smoking volcanic vents — try the black eggs cooked in the hot springs!)
5. **Pirate Ship** across Lake Ashi (kids love this)
6. Bus back to Hakone-Yumoto and return to Tokyo

**With more time:** Consider staying overnight at a **ryokan** with onsen. The Hakone Highland Hotel and Hyatt Regency Hakone both have family-friendly rooms.

**🎒 Pack for:** Temperature drops at altitude. Bring layers even in summer.

---

## Day 7: Final Morning & Departure

**Morning:** Visit **Tsukiji Outer Market** (the inner market moved to Toyosu, but the outer market remains a lively food destination). Let the kids try tamagoyaki (sweet egg roll), mini tuna bowls, and grilled scallops on skewers.

**Alternative for early flights:** If your flight departs before noon, just pick up a bento box at the airport — both Narita and Haneda have excellent food options.

**Departure:** Narita Airport has a **Japan Traditional Crafts Center** and a **Pokemon Store** near the gates. Haneda has an observation deck where kids can watch planes take off.

## FAQ

### Is Tokyo stroller-friendly?
Mostly yes. Major train stations have elevators, sidewalks are smooth, and attractions are accessible. However, some smaller shrines and restaurants up narrow stairs can be challenging. A lightweight umbrella stroller is ideal.

### Can my kids eat in Tokyo?
Absolutely. Japanese kids eat ramen, fried rice, curry, katsu (breaded cutlets), and gyoza (dumplings). Convenience stores are excellent for picky eaters. Halal and vegetarian options are limited but available in major areas like Shinjuku and Shibuya.

### What about bathroom breaks?
Tokyo's public toilets are clean (heated seats included). Major train stations, department stores, and attractions all have facilities. Carry a small hand towel as many don't have hand dryers.

### Do I need cash?
Yes — many smaller restaurants, temples, and market stalls are cash-only. ATMs at 7-Eleven and Japan Post accept international cards.

### Is it safe?
Extremely. Tokyo is one of the safest cities in the world. Lost items are usually returned. Kids can use public restrooms alone with minimal concern.
