### Philippines Family Island Hopping: The Ultimate Guide

With over 7,600 islands, the Philippines offers some of Asia's most spectacular beaches, warmest hospitality, and most family-friendly island experiences. Compared to Thailand or Bali, the Philippines remains **less crowded and more affordable** — a winning combination for families seeking genuine tropical adventure.

This guide covers the best **family-friendly islands**, kid-approved activities, inter-island logistics, and honest budgeting for an unforgettable Philippines island-hopping trip with children.

---

### Why the Philippines Works for Families

The Philippines is made for family travel for several key reasons:

- **Filipinos love children** — expect strangers to smile, wave, and offer help. Kids are welcomed everywhere, from street food stalls to fine dining.
- **English is widely spoken** — the Philippines is the third-largest English-speaking country in the world. No language barrier for ordering food, asking directions, or reading signs.
- **Shorter flight distances** — islands are relatively close to each other; inter-island flights are rarely longer than 1–1.5 hours.
- **Budget-friendly** — meals, accommodation, and Activities cost significantly less than in Japan, Singapore, or even Thailand.
- **Adventure for all ages** — calm lagoons for toddlers, snorkelling for school-age kids, and diving for teens.

### Best Islands for Families with Kids

#### 1. Palawan (El Nido & Puerto Princesa)

**Best for:** Families wanting iconic limestone karst landscapes and lagoon tours.

Palawan consistently ranks among the world's best islands, and for good reason. The **Bacuit Bay** in El Nido is a Jurassic Park-esque seascape of towering limestone cliffs, hidden lagoons, and turquoise waters.

**Family-Friendly Tour:**
- **Tour A (Lagoon Tour):** The gentlest option. Visit the **Big Lagoon** and **Small Lagoon** — calm, shallow waters ideal for toddlers. [Book El Nido tours on Klook](https://www.klook.com/search/?keyword=El+Nido+Palawan+tour&aid=38VWJMX). You'll ride an outrigger bangka boat (traditional Philippine boat). Kids can sit comfortably on the boat's bamboo deck.
- **Duration:** 4–5 hours, half-day
- **Cost:** ₱1,200–1,500 ($21–26 USD) per adult, half price for children under 10
- **Age Range:** All ages. Life jackets are provided for everyone.
- **Stroller Access:** Not applicable (boat-based tour). Use a baby carrier instead.

**Hotel Tip:** [Check Palawan family hotels on Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Palawan+Philippines&aid=2875669). Stay at **Lagen Island Resort** (family-friendly with a pool and kid's activities) or **El Nido Resorts Miniloc** (overwater bungalows, though pricier). For budget, **Spin Designer Hostel** in El Nido town has family rooms.

#### 2. Cebu (Moalboal & Kawasan Falls)

**Best for:** Older kids and teens seeking adventure.

Cebu offers a mix of marine life and waterfalls. **Moalboal's Pescador Island** is famous for its sardine run — millions of sardines swirling in a massive bait ball, viewable just 10 metres from shore.

**Family-Friendly Snorkelling:**
- **Sardine Run:** Snorkel with millions of sardines in crystal-clear water. Even hesitant swimmers can do this from the beach at **Panagsama Beach**.
- **Turtle Point:** Green sea turtles are almost guaranteed. Calm waters, 2–3 metre depth.
- **Kawasan Falls:** Mult-tiered turquoise waterfalls with natural pools. The main falls (first level) is easy to access — just a 10-minute walk from the parking lot. Kids can swim in the cool, shallow pool. Canyoneering (jumping from rock ledges) is for ages 10+ only.

**Age Range:** Sardine Run — 4+ comfortable snorkellers. Kawasan Falls main pool — all ages.

#### 3. Bohol (Chocolate Hills & Tarsiers)

**Best for:** Mixed-interest families (beach + nature + culture).

Bohol is the perfect "land and sea" island. It combines beautiful beaches with unique inland attractions.

**Must-Do with Kids:**
- **Chocolate Hills:** Over 1,200 perfectly symmetrical grass-covered hills that turn brown in the dry season, resembling chocolate kisses. The viewing platform has stairs but is manageable with a carrier. Admission: ₱50 ($1 USD) per person.
- **Philippine Tarsier Sanctuary:** Meet one of the world's smallest primates (fist-sized, huge eyes). The sanctuary is a protected reserve, not a zoo. The tarsiers are in their natural habitat, visible along a boardwalk path — **stroller accessible**. Kids adore their giant eyes and tiny bodies. Admission: ₱100 ($2 USD).
- **Loboc River Cruise:** A floating restaurant buffet cruise along the Loboc River. Calm, scenic, and kids can feed fish from the boat. Lunch cruise costs ₱600–800 ($10–14 USD) per adult, half price for children.

#### 4. Siargao

**Best for:** Active families with older kids/teens who love surfing.

Siargao is the Philippines' surf capital, but it's also home to quiet beaches, tidal pools, and island hopping. The famous **Cloud 9** surf break has gentle beginner waves on smaller days. Surf lessons cost just **₱500–700 ($9–12 USD)** for a 2-hour session including board rental.

**Non-Surfing Activities for Kids:**
- **Sugba Lagoon:** A stunning turquoise lagoon with floating platforms, paddleboarding, and kayaking. Calm, protected waters. Accessible by 30-minute bangka boat from Del Carmen.
- **Magpupungko Rock Pools:** Natural tidal pools that form at low tide — crystal-clear, warm, and full of small fish. Kids can spend hours splashing around. Admission: ₱50 ($1 USD).

### Inter-Island Travel with Kids

**Flights:** The easiest way between major islands. **Cebu Pacific** and **Philippine Airlines** operate frequent flights. Book in advance (2–3 months for best prices). Children under 2 fly free (lap child). Toddlers 2–11 get discounted fares.

**Ferries:** Between nearby islands (Cebu–Bohol, Bohol–Siquijor), ferries are comfortable with air-conditioned cabins. **OceanJet** is the fastest and most reliable. Book at the pier or via **12Go.asia**. Travel time: 1.5–2 hours. Motion sickness medication recommended for kids.

**Tricycles (Tuk-tuks):** The local transport. A family of 4 can fit in one (2 adults + 2 small kids). Negotiate the fare before getting in. Short trips run ₱20–50 ($0.35–$1 USD).

### Food for Kids in the Philippines

Filipino food is approachable for most children:

- **Chicken adobo** — soy-vinegar braised chicken, mild and savoury, a guaranteed kid-pleaser
- **Lumpiang Shanghai** — Filipino spring rolls (crispy, bite-sized, fried), perfect finger food
- **Pancit** — stir-fried noodles, available everywhere
- **Lechon manok** — rotisserie chicken, found at roadside stalls, whole chicken just ₱350 ($6 USD)
- **Halo-halo** — shaved ice dessert with sweet beans, fruit, and ice cream — kids love the rainbow presentation
- **Fresh fruit:** Mangoes (some of the world's best), bananas, dragon fruit, and coconut water sold everywhere

**Picky Eater Safety Net:** Every town has a **Jollibee** (Filipino fast-food chain loved by locals and kids) serving fried chicken, spaghetti, and burgers for ₱100–200 ($2–4 USD) per meal.

### Safety & Practical Tips

- **Weather:** Dry season is **November–May**. Avoid July–October (typhoon season). The best months for island hopping are **March–May** (calm seas, minimal rain).
- **Sun protection:** The tropical sun is intense. Bring reef-safe sunscreen, rash guards for kids, and wide-brimmed hats.
- **Life jackets:** Always provided on bangka boats, but bring your own **infant/child life jacket** from home for the best fit.
- **Mosquitoes:** Dengue is present. Use DEET-based repellent, and consider sleeping under mosquito nets in budget accommodation. **Best protection:** long-sleeved clothing after dusk.
- **Medical:** Major cities (Cebu, Puerto Princesa) have good hospitals. Remote islands like El Nido have clinics but for serious issues, evacuation is required. Pack a **comprehensive family first-aid kit** including rehydration salts.
- **Cash:** Many islands are still cash-based. ATMs exist in town centres but can run out on weekends. Bring enough Philippine pesos for 3–4 days at a time.
- **SIM Cards:** Buy a **Globe** or **Smart** tourist SIM at the airport (₱500 / $9 USD for 7 days of unlimited data). Coverage is good in towns, patchy in remote islands.
