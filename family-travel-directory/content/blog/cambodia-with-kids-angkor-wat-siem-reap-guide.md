### Cambodia with Kids: A Family Guide to Angkor Wat & Siem Reap

Angkor Wat is one of the world's great wonders — a sprawling ancient temple complex that rivals Machu Picchu and the Pyramids in historical significance. But can you visit it with young children? **Absolutely yes** — with the right strategy.

Siem Reap, the gateway to Angkor, is surprisingly family-friendly. It offers affordable accommodation, delicious kid-approved food, and a small-town feel that's easy to navigate. This guide covers everything you need for an unforgettable family trip to Cambodia's crown jewel.

---

### Is Angkor Wat Suitable for Children?

The short answer: yes, but you need to pace yourself. The Angkor Archaeological Park spans **400 square kilometres** with dozens of major temples. Attempting to "see it all" in one day is a recipe for meltdowns — regardless of age.

**Age-by-Age Advice:**

- **Babies (0–2 years):** Doable with a baby carrier (strollers don't work on temple stairs). Early mornings are hot and humid — bring a portable fan, plenty of water, and a sun hat. Limit temple visits to 2–3 per day.
- **Toddlers (2–4 years):** Focus on ground-level temples (Ta Prohm, Angkor Wat's lower level). Toddlers enjoy running in open courtyards and climbing low walls. The **entrance to Angkor Wat main tower** has extremely steep stairs — skip this with toddlers.
- **School-age (5–10 years):** This is a sweet spot. Kids can climb many temple structures (with supervision), love the "tomb raider" vibe of Ta Prohm's tree roots, and are fascinated by the temple carvings of elephants, monkeys, and warriors.
- **Teens (11+):** Ideal age. Teens can appreciate the history, climb the steeper temples (Bakheng, Pre Rup), and enjoy the sunset viewpoints. Consider hiring a guide who can engage them with stories.

### The Best Temples for Kids

Not all temples are equal when visiting with children. Here's our curated list ranked by kid-friendliness:

#### 1. Ta Prohm — The "Tomb Raider" Temple

**Why Kids Love It:** This is the most Instagram-famous temple in Angkor — massive strangler fig trees have grown over and through the stone ruins, creating a jungle-meets-ancient-city atmosphere that feels like a movie set (because it is — Angelina Jolie's *Lara Croft: Tomb Raider* was filmed here).

**Kid Tips:**
- **Flat terrain** for the main section — easy walking for little legs
- **Tree roots** create natural slides and climbing spots (supervise carefully)
- **Photo ops everywhere** — kids posing inside giant tree roots are magical
- **Best time:** 8:00–9:30 AM (before crowds and heat)
- **Watch for:** Loose stones and uneven pathways — hold toddlers' hands

#### 2. Angkor Wat (The Main Temple)

**The Strategy:** Don't try to "do" all of Angkor Wat in one visit. Focus on the **lower level** with kids. The bas-relief galleries that wrap around the temple tell epic Hindu stories carved in stone — kids can spot elephants, multi-headed serpents, and battle scenes.

**Pro Tip:** The famous **library buildings** on either side of the main causeway are less crowded and have shallow, safe platforms where kids can explore.

**What to Skip:**
- The central tower climb (near-vertical stairs, not suitable for most kids under 10)
- The upper level terrace (narrow walkways, no railings)
- The sunrise view (3:30 AM wake-up is cruel for everyone)

#### 3. Bayon Temple (Angkor Thom)

**Why Kids Love It:** Bayon is famous for its **216 massive stone faces** carved into the temple towers, smiling serenely in all directions. Kids are mesmerised by the faces and love trying to count them.

**Kid Tips:**
- **Two levels** — the lower level has narrow galleries (fun for hide-and-seek), the upper level has closer access to the faces
- Steps are moderate but manageable for agile kids aged 5+
- Visit **late afternoon (3:30–4:30 PM)** when the faces are beautifully lit by golden light
- **Best game:** "How many faces can you spot?" — keep a running tally

#### 4. Banteay Srei — "The Citadel of Women"

**Why Kids Love It:** This smaller temple (40 minutes from Siem Reap) features the finest, most intricate carvings in all of Angkor — so detailed they look like they're made of lace. The reddish-pink sandstone makes it visually stunning.

**Kid Tips:**
- **Small and compact** — easy to see in 30 minutes
- **Shallow pools** at the entrance where kids can cool their feet
- Bring a **handheld magnifying glass** — kids love examining the miniature carvings
- Best as a **morning trip** before the heat intensifies

### Our Recommended 4-Day Siem Reap Family Itinerary

**Day 1: Arrival and Orientation**

- Arrive at Siem Reap International Airport (direct flights from Bangkok, Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, and many Asian hubs)
- **Check into** a family-friendly hotel (see recommendations below)
- **Afternoon:** Visit the **Angkor National Museum** (air-conditioned!) — this is the perfect primer. The museum uses multimedia exhibits and is engaging for kids. **Admission:** $12 adult / $6 child ($20 family ticket available)
- **Evening:** Dinner at **Pub Street** — pedestrianised in the evening, full of restaurants with kid's menus. Try **Khmer Kitchen Restaurant** for mild amok (Coconut fish curry) and fresh spring rolls.

**Day 2: Angkor Wat + Ta Prohm**

- **7:30 AM:** Start at **Angkor Wat** (enter through the west gate). Spend 1.5 hours exploring the lower level and grounds.
- **10:00 AM:** Transfer to **Ta Prohm** (15-minute drive). 1 hour of jungle temple exploration. Enjoy the shade from the trees.
- **11:30 AM:** Return to hotel for pool time and lunch. The midday heat (35°C+) is brutal — don't push through.
- **3:00 PM:** **Preah Khan temple** — similar to Ta Prohm but less crowded. Sprawling, partially overgrown, and full of photo-worthy corridors.
- **6:00 PM:** Dinner at **Viroth's Restaurant** (high-quality Khmer cuisine, elegant garden setting, kid-friendly).

**Day 3: Bayon + Banteay Srei + Floating Village**

- **7:00 AM:** Enter Angkor Thom through the **South Gate** (the causeway lined with statues of gods and demons carrying a naga serpent — kids love hearing the mythological story). Visit **Bayon Temple** for 45 minutes.
- **8:30 AM:** Quick walk through the **Terrace of the Elephants** and **Terrace of the Leper King** (open-air, quick stops).
- **9:30 AM:** Drive to **Banteay Srei** (40 minutes). Spend 30–45 minutes exploring.
- **11:00 AM:** Visit **Banteay Samre** (nearby, very quiet, kids can explore freely).
- **Afternoon (2:00 PM):** Visit **Kampong Phluk** floating village on Tonle Sap Lake. This is a genuine cultural experience — stilted houses, floating schools, and boat rides through flooded forests. Kids are fascinated. **Cost:** $25–30 USD per person (boat tour included).
- **Evening:** **Phare, the Cambodian Circus** — a world-class performance by local artists combining acrobatics, theatre, and music. Kids of all ages love it, and it supports a social enterprise. **Tickets:** $18–38 USD. [Book on Klook](https://www.klook.com/search/?keyword=Phare+Cambodian+Circus+Siem+Reap&aid=38VWJMX).

**Day 4: Last Temple + Departure**

- **7:30 AM:** One last temple — choose **Bakong** (the largest in the Roluos Group, pyramid-style, gentle climb) or simply revisit a favourite.
- **10:30 AM:** Shopping at **Artisans Angkor** — a fair-trade workshop where you can watch artisans carving stone and wood, lacquering, and silk weaving. Free admission. Kids can try simple crafting activities.
- **12:00 PM:** Lunch at **Cuisine Wat Damnak** (Cambodia's top-rated restaurant, Michelin Guide listed — surprisingly welcoming to families at lunch).
- **Departure:** Siem Reap Airport is only 15 minutes from town.

### Food in Cambodia: What Kids Will Eat

Khmer cuisine is milder and less spicy than Thai food — great news for families.

**Kid-Approved Dishes:**
- **Amok** — coconut milk fish curry steamed in banana leaves (mildly spiced, creamy, served with rice)
- **Lok lak** — stir-fried beef served with rice and a lime-pepper dipping sauce
- **Num pang** — Cambodian baguette sandwiches (legacy of French colonialism), filled with pâté, pork, and pickled vegetables
- **Fish with tamarind sauce** — sweet and sour, familiar to most kids
- **Fresh spring rolls** — clear rice paper rolls with shrimp, herbs, and vermicelli
- **Fried rice (bai cha)** — available everywhere, with egg, chicken, or vegetables
- **Fried bananas (chék chien)** — street food favourite, crispy outside, soft inside

**Drink:** Fresh sugar cane juice ($0.50) and young coconut water ($1.00) are sold everywhere and perfect for rehydration.

### Practical Tips for Angkor with Kids

- **Start early, finish by noon.** The heat from 11 AM to 3 PM is intense. Return to your hotel pool, rest, and go out again at 3:30 PM.
- **Sun protection:** Sunscreen, wide-brimmed hats, and lightweight long-sleeved clothing are essential. The sun reflects off the stone temples.
- **Hydration:** Bring 2 litres of water per person. Most temples have vendors selling cold water ($0.50 per bottle).
- **Footwear:** Closed-toe shoes with good grip. Temple steps can be steep and uneven.
- **Toilet breaks:** Not all temples have restrooms. Use facilities before entering each site. The Angkor Wat complex has clean restrooms near the entrance.
- **Baby carrier over stroller:** Most temples have uneven stone pathways, steep steps, and narrow corridors. A good baby carrier (like Ergobaby or Tula) is essential for babies and toddlers.
- **Bribes for motivation:** Seriously — pack a bag of small treats (lollipops, stickers) to encourage tired legs. "Let's see five more faces at Bayon, then you get a sticker!"

### When to Visit Siem Reap with Kids

- **Best:** **November–February** — cool, dry, and pleasant (25–30°C days). Peak season, book accommodation 2–3 months ahead.
- **Shoulder:** **March–May** — hotter (35–38°C) but fewer crowds. Start your days at 6:30 AM if possible.
- **Wet Season:** **June–October** — afternoon downpours, but Angkor is quieter and the temples look stunning against dramatic skies. Mornings are usually clear. Expect very lush greenery.
