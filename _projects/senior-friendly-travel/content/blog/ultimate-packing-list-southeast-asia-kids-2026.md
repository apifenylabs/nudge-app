# Ultimate Packing List for Southeast Asia with Kids (2026)

Packing for a family trip to Southeast Asia is stressful. Overpack and you're dragging heavy suitcases through Bangkok's BTS stations and Chiang Mai's cobblestone streets. Underpack and you're paying a premium for suncream in Phuket and hunting for baby wipes at 10 PM.

After two years of covering family travel across Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, and Cambodia, we've built the definitive packing list for families visiting Southeast Asia in 2026.

**The golden rule:** Pack for 3-4 days and do laundry. Almost every hotel in Southeast Asia offers laundry service for $2-5 per kilo. You do not need a different outfit for every day.

---

## Before You Go: The 2026 Pre-Trip Checklist

| Task | Details |
|------|---------|
| Check passport validity | Must have 6+ months remaining from return date |
| Apply for visas | Check if your country needs eVisas for Vietnam, Cambodia, Myanmar |
| Download offline maps | Google Maps offline + Maps.me for remote areas |
| Download Grab/Gojek | Southeast Asia's Uber equivalents |
| Set up eSIM | Airalo, Holafly, or local SIM at the airport — don't pay roaming |
| Health check | Visit travel clinic 4-6 weeks before departure |
| Copy documents | Email copies of passports, visas, insurance to yourself |
| Check weather | Rainy season varies by country — pack accordingly |

## The Master Packing List

### 👕 Clothing — Every Family Member

Southeast Asia is hot and humid year-round. Natural fabrics, light colours, and layers (for air conditioning) are the strategy.

| Item | Per Person | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| Lightweight t-shirts/tops | 5-6 | Cotton or quick-dry fabrics |
| Shorts | 3-4 | Denim is too hot — go with cotton or linen |
| Long pants/trousers | 1-2 | For temples, evenings, and air-conditioned buses |
| Light dress (girls/women) | 2-3 | Maxi dresses work brilliantly |
| Long-sleeve shirt (light) | 1 | Sun protection + mosquito defence |
| Swimsuit | 2 per person | Rotate — one dries while you wear the other |
| Light cardigan/hoodie | 1 | Air-conditioned planes, buses, and malls get cold |
| Pyjamas | 2 sets | Lightweight cotton |
| Socks | 5 pairs | Thin athletic socks |
| Underwear | 7 pairs | Yes, bring a week's worth — hardest to replace |
| Comfortable walking shoes | 1 pair | Broken in, not brand new |
| Sandals/flip-flops | 1 pair | For beaches, pools, and hotel rooms |
| Rain jacket or poncho | 1 per person | Especially June–October |

**For temples** (applicable in Thailand, Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, and Bali):
Women/girls need shoulders and knees covered. A lightweight sarong or scarf works as both a cover-up and a temple wrap. Men/boys need long trousers and covered shoulders. Many temples offer free sarongs for rent, but having your own is easier.

### 👶 Babies & Toddlers (0–3 years)

If you're travelling with a baby or toddler, the following checklist is critical:

| Item | Bring or Buy? | Notes |
|------|--------------|-------|
| Baby formula | **Bring enough for the trip** | Brands differ between countries — stick with what baby knows |
| Nappies (diapers) | Bring 3-day supply | Buy Pampers or Huggies at supermarkets in Bali, Bangkok, Singapore |
| Baby wipes | Bring 2 packs | Buy more locally — they're everywhere |
| Nappy cream | **Bring** | Your specific brand likely isn't available |
| Baby sunscreen (SPF 50+) | **Bring** | Quality sunscreen is expensive and limited in SE Asia |
| Insect repellent (baby-safe) | **Bring** | DEET-free options are hard to find |
| Baby carrier (not stroller) | **Bring** | Streets in Bangkok, Chiang Mai, and Ubud are NOT stroller-friendly |
| Light stroller (umbrella style) | **Bring** | Useful at airports, malls, and smooth paths — skip for island travel |
| Swim nappies | 10-15 disposables | Or 3 reusable — buy more if needed |
| Changing mat | **Bring** | Public changing tables are rare outside major malls |
| Bottles & sterilising tablets | **Bring** | Cold-water sterilising tablets are life-savers |
| Baby food pouches | Bring to start | Buy more at supermarkets — increasing availability |
| Portable high chair strap | **Bring** | Straps onto regular chairs — many restaurants lack high chairs |
| First-aid kit (baby specific) | **Bring** | Calpol, teething gel, thermometer |

### 👧👦 Kids (4–12 years)

School-aged kids are the sweet spot for Southeast Asia travel. They can walk, they're curious, and they'll eat most things (especially if it's on a stick).

| Item | Bring or Buy? | Notes |
|------|--------------|-------|
| Swimsuit | **Bring** 2 | Rotating is essential in humid climates |
| Wide-brim sun hat | **Bring** | Chin straps prevent losing it in the wind |
| Water shoes | **Bring** | For rocky beaches, coral, and hotel pools |
| Rash guard / UV shirt | **Bring** | Best sun protection — they'll wear it all day |
| Kids' sunscreen (SPF 50+) | **Bring** | Reef-safe, kid-formulated — not always available locally |
| Insect repellent | **Bring** | Picaridin-based is best for kids (less smelly than DEET) |
| Reusable water bottle | **Bring** | Fill at hotels — most have filtered water stations |
| Small backpack for day trips | **Bring** | Let kids carry their own snacks and water |
| Wet wipes (small pack) | **Bring** | For sticky hands after street food |
| Entertainment for flights | **Bring** | Tablet, headphones, colouring, sticker books |
| Kids' snorkel mask | **Bring** | Full-face masks work well for younger kids |
| Refillable snack containers | **Bring** | Great for market snacks and hotel breakfasts |
| Kids' vitamins | **Bring** | Maintain routines even on holiday |

**The one thing NOT to bring:** A full toy box. Kids in Southeast Asia are easily entertained by street dogs, geckos on walls, and running around hotel pools. Bring one comfort item (small stuffed animal, favourite book) and buy a cheap toy at the night market as a souvenir.

### 🧑 Teenagers (13–17 years)

Teens have specific needs — tech, independence, and the right fashion:

| Item | Why |
|------|-----|
| Power bank (20,000 mAh+) | Phones die fast with GPS, photos, and social media |
| Universal travel adapter | Southeast Asia uses Type A, B, C, F, and G plugs — get a universal one |
| Good headphones | For flights, bus rides, and teen sanity breaks |
| Cross-body phone pouch | Anti-theft and leaves hands free for food |
| Modest temple outfit | Teens often push back — buy a cheap pair of elephant pants at the market |
| Decent trainers/hiking sandals | Teens walk more than they expect |
| Quick-dry travel towel | Some budget hotels and hostels don't provide towels |
| Small notebook | For journaling, sketching, or collecting stamps at temples |

**Parent tip:** Let teens pack their own bag. At 14+, they can manage their own carry-on with entertainment, snacks, and a change of clothes. Natural consequences teach faster than nagging.

## 🧴 The Health & Toiletries Kit

This is the one area where you should never compromise. Medical and toiletry items are available in cities but may be different brands, different formulations, or simply not available on smaller islands.

| Item | Notes |
|------|-------|
| Prescription medications | **Bring enough for the entire trip + 3 extra days** |
| Anti-diarrhoea medication | Imodium or similar — street food is worth it but sometimes risky |
| Oral rehydration salts | Life-saving for kids with stomach bugs |
| Antihistamines | For allergic reactions — bites, food, pollen |
| Motion sickness tablets | For ferries, long bus rides, and winding mountain roads |
| Paracetamol / ibuprofen | Kids' and adults' versions |
| Antiseptic cream | Small cuts and scrapes happen daily |
| Plasters/Band-Aids | Blister plasters are worth their weight in gold |
| Thermometer | Digital, quick-read |
| Insect repellent (Picaridin 20%) | Best balance of effectiveness and skin-friendliness |
| Sunscreen (SPF 50, reef-safe) | Bring from home — quality varies in SE Asia |
| After-sun / aloe vera gel | For the inevitable missed spot |
| Hand sanitiser | Small bottle for every bag |
| First-aid kit (pre-made) | Add any family-specific items |

## 🛍️ What NOT to Bring (Buy It There)

Southeast Asia has incredible shopping. Pack light and buy what you need:

| Item | Where to Buy | Approx Price |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| Sarong / temple wrap | Every market in Thailand, Bali, Cambodia | $2-5 |
| Elephant pants | Chiang Mai, Bangkok, Ubud | $3-6 |
| Flip-flops | 7-Elevens and street stalls everywhere | $2-5 |
| Nappies | Supermarkets in all cities and tourist towns | $5-15 for a pack |
| Baby wipes | Every supermarket, 7-Eleven, pharmacy | $1-3 |
| Sun hat | Night markets — huge selection | $2-5 |
| Cheap toys & souvenirs | Night markets everywhere | $1-10 |
| Swimwear | Markets in beach towns | $5-15 |

**The exception:** Sunscreen. Quality sunscreen that won't irritate kids' skin is genuinely hard to find in many parts of Southeast Asia. Bring enough for the trip from home.

## 🎒 Day Pack Essentials (What to Carry Daily)

Every day in Southeast Asia, this day pack should be ready:

1. **Water bottles** (one per person, refill at hotel)
2. **Sunscreen** (apply before leaving, reapply at lunch)
3. **Insect repellent** (apply after sunscreen)
4. **Wet wipes** (for hands, faces, surfaces)
5. **Snacks** (biscuits, nuts, dried fruit — granola bars melt in the heat)
6. **Rain poncho** (especially June–October)
7. **Extra shirt** (for kids — sweat happens)
8. **Power bank + charging cable**
9. **Small first-aid kit** (plasters, antiseptic, diarrhoea meds)
10. **Sarong** (temple admission, sun shade, picnic blanket, cover-up)

## 🧳 Luggage Recommendations by Family Size

| Family Size | Recommended Setup | Why |
|-------------|------------------|-----|
| 2 adults + 1 baby | 1 large suitcase (check) + 1 carry-on + baby bag | Baby gear takes space |
| 2 adults + 1 kid (4-10) | 1 large suitcase + 1 medium suitcase + 2 backpacks | Kid carries their own backpack |
| 2 adults + 2 kids | 2 large suitcases + kids' backpacks + 1 daypack | Never more than 4 bags total |
| 2 adults + 2 teens | 4 carry-on sized (kids pack their own!) | Teens manage their own luggage |

**Hard limit:** If you can't carry all your bags between two adults in one trip from taxi to hotel lobby, you have too much.

## 🌦️ Seasonal Packing Notes

### November–February (Cool/Dry Season)
Best time for comfortable travel. Pack the standard list above but add a light jacket for evenings in northern Thailand, especially Chiang Mai and Pai where temperatures can drop to 15°C at night.

### March–May (Hot Season)
Scorching. No jeans. Double the sunscreen. Add a handheld fan, cooling towels, and a bigger water bottle. Plan for siestas between 12-3 PM.

### June–October (Rainy Season)
This is when most of Southeast Asia gets afternoon downpours. Pack a proper rain jacket or poncho for everyone, waterproof sandals, and a waterproof phone pouch. The upside? Lush green landscapes, fewer crowds, and cheaper prices. Our [summer family itineraries](/blog/asia-summer-2026-family-itineraries) cover this season in detail.

## ✈️ Airport & Flight Survival Kit

Long-haul flights to Southeast Asia are 10-18 hours depending on your origin. Survive them with:

| Item | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| Change of clothes for each person | For freshening up before landing |
| Empty water bottle | Fill after security — plane air is dehydrating |
| Snacks that don't melt | Pretzels, crackers, dried fruit |
| Tablet loaded with shows/movies | Don't rely on airline entertainment systems |
| Kids' headphones (volume-limited) | Protect little ears |
| Wet wipes | Freshen faces during the flight |
| Toothbrush + toothpaste | Arriving fresh makes a huge difference |
| Neck pillows (inflatable) | Takes less space than foam pillows |
| Melatonin (parents) | For adjusting time zones — check with your doctor |
| Empty plastic bags | For dirty clothes, wet swimsuits, or unexpected messes |
