# Taiwan Family Travel Guide — Best Attractions, Hotels & Tips for Families

Taiwan is one of Asia's most underrated family destinations. It's safe (one of the safest countries in Asia), incredibly clean, has excellent public transport, and offers a remarkable mix of urban attractions and natural beauty — all at a fraction of the cost of Japan or Singapore.

The best part? Taiwanese people genuinely love children. You'll find strollers welcomed everywhere, helpful locals offering directions, and a food scene that caters brilliantly to kids. English signage is good in major cities, and the MRT system is world-class.

---

## Top Family Attractions in Taiwan

### 1. Taipei Zoo & Maokong Gondola

**Best for:** All ages
**Price:** ~$3/adult, ~$1/kid (zoo). ~$5/adult round-trip (gondola)
**Time needed:** 3-4 hours

One of the best zoos in Asia for the price. The panda exhibit (Yuan Yuan and Tuan Tuan) is the main draw, but the Formosan animal area (endemic species) and the children's zoo petting area are also highlights. The zoo is massive and well-designed.

After the zoo, take the **Maokong Gondola** from the zoo station up into the hills. The glass-bottomed "Crystal Cabin" gondolas are thrilling for kids. At the top, Maokong village has tea houses with stunning views over Taipei.

**Family Tip:** Go on a weekday. Start at the zoo at 9:30 AM (opening), spend 2-3 hours, then take the gondola up. Have lunch at a tea house in Maokong, then ride the gondola back. Buy an EasyCard for easy tap-in at both the zoo and gondola.

### 2. Taipei 101 Observatory

**Best for:** All ages
**Price:** ~$15/adult, ~$12/kid
**Time needed:** 1-2 hours

Once the world's tallest building (now 11th), Taipei 101's observation deck on the 89th floor offers breathtaking 360° views. The high-speed elevator (37 seconds to 89th floor, fastest in the world) impresses kids with the pressure change.

The wind damper (a giant gold ball) in the upper floors is visible from viewing platforms — it's a 660-ton pendulum that stabilizes the building during typhoons and earthquakes.

**Family Tip:** Go on a clear day — check the visibility before buying tickets. Late afternoon (4-5 PM) is ideal for daytime views + sunset + evening lights. The ground floor mall (Taipei 101 Mall) has excellent restaurants and a Din Tai Fung.

### 3. National Palace Museum

**Best for:** Ages 7+
**Price:** ~$8/adult, FREE for kids under 18
**Time needed:** 2-3 hours

One of the world's greatest museums, housing over 700,000 pieces of ancient Chinese imperial art and artifacts. The "jade cabbage" and "meat-shaped stone" are the most famous pieces — exquisitely carved miniature sculptures that kids find fascinating.

**Family Tip:** Skip the audioguide with young kids — they'll lose interest quickly. Focus on the top 10 highlights (marked on the map). The museum has a good cafe. The outdoor Zhishan Garden is beautiful for a post-museum run-around.

### 4. Ximending (Teenage Shopping District)

**Best for:** Ages 8+ (especially teens)
**Price:** FREE (budget $10-30 for shopping)
**Time needed:** 2-3 hours

Taipei's answer to Shibuya or Harajuku — a massive pedestrian shopping district filled with street performers, anime shops, cosplay boutiques, toy stores, arcades, and crazy street food. It's the epicenter of Taiwanese youth culture.

**Kid-friendly highlights:**
- **Eslite Spectrum** — A massive creative department store with a huge stationery and toy floor
- **Tong Hua Street Night Market** — Food stalls with stinky tofu, bubble tea, fried chicken cutlets
- **Retro Studio** — Old-school arcade, pinball machines, nostalgic games
- **Cosplay alley** — Kids love seeing people dressed as anime characters on weekends

**Family Tip:** Go in the evening (5-9 PM) when it's fully alive. The area is pedestrian-only and very safe. Great for letting older kids explore independently.

### 5. Beitou Hot Springs

**Best for:** All ages
**Price:** $5-15/person (public pools), $20-50/person (private resort pools)
**Time needed:** 2-4 hours

Taipei's hot spring district, reachable via MRT. The public **Beitou Hot Spring Public Bath** is cheap and family-friendly. For a nicer experience, book a day pass at a hot spring hotel (many allow non-guests to use their pools).

**Family Tip:** Go in cooler weather (October-March). The **Beitou Geothermal Valley** (aka "Hell Valley") is a dramatic steam-emitting pool of 100°C water — impressive but keep kids away from the edge. Wear swimsuits for the pools.

### 6. Yehliu Geopark

**Best for:** Ages 5+
**Price:** ~$4/adult, ~$2/kid
**Time needed:** 1.5-2 hours (plus 45-min drive from Taipei)

A surreal coastal landscape of wind-and-water-eroded rock formations. The "Queen's Head" is the most famous — a mushroom-shaped rock resembling a queen's profile. Other formations look like mushrooms, honeycombs, and ginger.

**Family Tip:** Go early (opens 8 AM) to beat the crowds and heat. The park is exposed to the sun — wear hats and sunscreen. Combine with a visit to **Keelung Night Market** (Taiwan's largest night market) on the way back.

### 7. Taroko Gorge (Day Trip from Taipei or Hualien)

**Best for:** Ages 6+
**Price:** FREE (park), ~$30-50/person (tour)
**Time needed:** Full day

One of Asia's most spectacular natural wonders — a marble-walled canyon carved by the Liwu River. The Shakadang Trail is easy and perfect for kids (paved, flat, 30-minute walk along a turquoise river). The Eternal Spring Shrine is a beautiful temple built into the cliff.

**Family Tip:** Take the high-speed TRA train from Taipei to Hualien (2 hours, beautiful coastal views) and join a guided tour. The **Eternal Spring Shrine** walk is safe for kids but hold hands. Avoid after heavy rain — trails can close.

## Best Family Hotels in Taiwan

### Luxury Choice
**Grand Hyatt Taipei** — $180-350/night, Xinyi District. Massive pool with water slides, kids' club, spacious rooms, connected to Taipei 101 via walkway. Best luxury family option.

### Mid-Range Excellence
**Caesar Park Taipei** — $100-180/night, near Taipei Main Station. Great location, family rooms sleep 4, indoor pool. Walking distance to good restaurants and transport hubs.

**City Suites NX** — $60-100/night, near Taipei Main Station. Apartment-style rooms with kitchenettes. Connected to a large shopping mall. Excellent value for families.

### Budget-Friendly
**Chamcharm Taipei** — $40-70/night, Ximending. Fun, colorful hotel with themed rooms. Great location in the middle of Ximending. Clean and modern.

## Taiwan Night Markets (A Family Food Adventure)

Taiwan's night markets are legendary and surprisingly kid-friendly. Here are the best ones:

| Night Market | Best For | Must-Try Food | Location |
|-------------|----------|---------------|----------|
| Shilin | Everything (largest) | Fried chicken cutlet, stinky tofu | Taipei (MRT Jiantan) |
| Raohe | Smaller, cozier | Pepper pork buns, grilled squid | Taipei (MRT Songshan) |
| Ningxia | Food-only, no games | Oyster omelette, fried taro balls | Taipei (MRT Zhongshan) |
| Tonghua (Linjiang) | Less touristy | Taiwanese sausage, shaved ice | Taipei (MRT Liuzhangli) |
| Fengjia (Taichung) | Largest in Taiwan (weekend only) | Bubble tea, sun cakes | Taichung |

**Family Tip:** Night markets are safe, cheap, and fun. Give kids a $10 budget and let them point and choose. The fried chicken cutlet (jipai) is the size of a dinner plate for $3. Always carry wet wipes.

## Getting Around Taiwan with Kids

- **MRT** — Taipei's metro is clean, cheap, stroller-friendly, and signed in English/Chinese. Get an **EasyCard** at any station
- **High-speed rail** — Connects Taipei, Taichung, Tainan, and Kaohsiung in 90-120 minutes. Spacious seats, family areas. Book at the station
- **Taxis** — $5-10 per trip in Taipei. Honest drivers, no haggling needed
- **Grab/Uber** — Available in Taipei. More expensive than taxis but convenient
- **Buses** — Good but harder with strollers. Stick to MRT in Taipei

## Practical Tips for Taiwan with Kids

- **EasyCard is essential** — Taps for MRT, buses, convenience stores, and some attractions. Kids 6-12 get discounted fares
- **Convenience stores are amazing** — 7-Eleven and Family Mart are everywhere. They sell fresh milk, yogurt, sandwiches, hot meals, and have clean bathrooms
- **Tap water is NOT drinkable** — Boil or buy bottled. Many hotels provide water coolers
- **Typhoon season (June-October)** — Check forecasts. Most attractions continue running in light rain
- **Earthquake safety** — Taiwan has frequent minor earthquakes. Know the evacuation procedures at your hotel
- **No tipping** — Tipping is not expected in Taiwan. Just pay the listed price
- **Bathroom culture** — Many public bathrooms have squat toilets. Family restrooms are common in MRT stations
- **English level** — Good in Taipei and tourist spots. Learn "xie xie" (thank you) and "ni hao" (hello)

## Plan Your Trip

For a family of four, budget **$200-350/day** for a comfortable Taiwan trip (mid-range hotel, restaurants, 1-2 paid activities, MRT/taxi transport).

**Best time to visit:** October-December (best weather) or March-May (spring flowers). Avoid June-September (typhoon and heat).

**Sample 5-day itinerary:**
- Day 1: Arrive, Ximending evening
- Day 2: Taipei Zoo + Maokong Gondola (morning), Taipei 101 (late afternoon)
- Day 3: National Palace Museum (morning), Beitou Hot Springs (afternoon), Shilin Night Market (evening)
- Day 4: Day trip to Yehliu + Jiufen (coastal scenery + old mountain town)
- Day 5: Taroko Gorge day trip or depart
