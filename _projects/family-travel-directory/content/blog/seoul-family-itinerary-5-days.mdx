# Seoul with Kids — 5-Day Family-Friendly Itinerary

Seoul is a fantastic family destination that doesn't get enough attention from Asia travelers. It's safe (one of the safest cities in the world), incredibly clean, has amazing public transport, and offers a fascinating blend of ancient palaces and futuristic technology.

Korean culture is also deeply family-oriented. Koreans love children — you'll find kids eating in restaurants at 10 PM, strollers everywhere, and helpful locals going out of their way to assist. The main challenges are the language barrier (less English than Singapore or Hong Kong) and the cold winter, but neither is a dealbreaker.

---

## 5-Day Seoul Family Itinerary

### Day 1: Palaces & Traditional Seoul

**Morning: Gyeongbokgung Palace**
- **Best for:** Ages 5+
- **Price:** ~$3/adult (kids under 18 FREE with Korean traditional wear)
- **Time needed:** 1.5-2 hours

The largest and most impressive of Seoul's five grand palaces. The changing of the guard ceremony (10 AM and 2 PM) is a highlight. Rent hanbok (traditional Korean clothing) for the kids — it's fun, and they get free palace entry.

**Family Tip:** Hanbok rental shops are everywhere near the palace. Rent for ~$15-20/per person for 4 hours. It's worth it for the photos alone.

**Lunch: Insadong**
- Traditional Korean street food district. Try hotteok (sweet pancakes), tteokbokki (spicy rice cakes), and mandu (dumplings). Plenty of kid-friendly options.

**Afternoon: Bukchon Hanok Village**
- **Best for:** All ages
- **Price:** FREE
- **Time needed:** 1-1.5 hours

A preserved neighborhood of traditional Korean houses (hanoks) on a hillside. The alleyways are beautiful, and there are small museums, craft shops, and tea houses. It's a quiet, walkable area.

**Family Tip:** Keep voices low — people still live here. Stick to the main paths with younger kids (stairs are steep).

**Evening: Myeongdong Night Market**
- Shopping, street food, and entertainment. Kids love the beauty product samples, the food stalls, and the buskers. Myeongdong Kyoja serves famous kalguksu (knife-cut noodle soup) that kids love.

### Day 2: Kid-Friendly Museums & Theme Parks

**Morning: National Museum of Korea**
- **Best for:** Ages 6+
- **Price:** FREE (special exhibitions ~$10/adult)
- **Time needed:** 2-3 hours

One of the world's great museums. The children's museum section is excellent — hands-on exhibits where kids can try traditional Korean crafts, dress in hanbok, and explore a replica archaeological dig.

**Family Tip:** The museum's outdoor grounds have a pond, sculptures, and plenty of space to run. The cafe is good and reasonably priced.

**Lunch: Yongsan Family Food Court near the museum**

**Afternoon: Lotte World**
- **Best for:** Ages 3+
- **Price:** ~$35/adult, ~$28/kid (Magic Pass includes most rides)
- **Time needed:** 4+ hours

Korea's answer to Disneyland — a massive indoor theme park (Lotte World Adventure) plus an outdoor area (Magic Island). The indoor section is perfect for hot or cold weather. Rides range from gentle (Gyro Drop, carousel) to thrilling (Atlantis Adventure, French Revolution roller coaster).

**Family Tip:** Go on a weekday. Buy the Magic Pass on Klook for unlimited ride access. The indoor park is open until 9 PM, so you could arrive around 2 PM and stay until closing.

**Evening: Lotte World Tower (Seoul Sky)**
- **Price:** ~$25/adult, ~$18/kid
- **Time needed:** 1-2 hours

The tallest building in Korea and the 6th tallest in the world. The glass-floor observation deck at 486 meters is thrilling. Kids love walking on the glass panels and seeing the city spread out below.

### Day 3: N Seoul Tower & Nature

**Morning: N Seoul Tower**
- **Best for:** All ages
- **Price:** ~$10/adult, ~$7/kid
- **Time needed:** 2 hours (including cable car)

Take the Namsan Cable Car up Namsan Mountain to the iconic N Seoul Tower. The views are spectacular. The "Locks of Love" area has thousands of padlocks left by couples. There's a fluffy teddy bear museum inside.

**Family Tip:** Go in the morning to avoid cable car queues (30-60 min at peak). The walk down the mountain is steep but scenic.

**Lunch: Namsan Park area — try Namsan Pattibang (steamed buns)**

**Afternoon: Namsan Park & Children's Grand Park**
- **Best for:** All ages
- **Price:** FREE
- **Time needed:** 2-3 hours

Namsan Park is beautiful and has playgrounds. If the kids have energy, head to Seoul Children's Grand Park in Neung-dong — it has a small zoo, a playground, a roller skating rink, and pedal boats on the lake.

**Evening: Gwangjang Market**
- **Price:** $2-5 per dish
- **Time needed:** 1.5-2 hours

Seoul's oldest traditional market and the best place for food. Try mayak gimbap (mini seaweed rice rolls — kids love them), bindaetteok (mung bean pancakes), and jeon (Korean pancakes).

---

### Day 4: DMZ Tour & Han River

**Morning: DMZ Tour (half-day)**
- **Best for:** Ages 8+ (not suitable for young kids)
- **Price:** ~$40-55/person (booked tour)

A half-day tour to the Korean Demilitarized Zone. You'll visit the Third Tunnel (an infiltration tunnel dug by North Korea), the Dora Observatory (where you can see North Korea through binoculars), and the Dorasan Station.

**Family Tip:** This is educational and fascinating for older kids but skip it with toddlers and younger children — there's a lot of walking and waiting. Book through Klook or Viator. Dress respectfully (no ripped jeans, military-style clothing, or revealing outfits).

**Alternative for younger kids: Coex Aquarium**
- **Price:** ~$20/adult, ~$15/kid
- If DMZ is too heavy, visit Coex Aquarium in Gangnam — excellent aquarium with a walk-through shark tunnel.

**Afternoon & Evening: Han River Park (Yeouido or Banpo)**
- **Best for:** All ages
- **Price:** FREE (rent bikes ~$5-10/hour)

The Han River parks are Seoul's backyard. Rent family bikes, have a picnic, paddleboats on the river. Banpo Bridge has the Moonlight Rainbow Fountain show at 7:30 PM and 8:30 PM (April-October).

**Family Tip:** Order fried chicken and beer (or soda) delivery to the park through an app — a classic Seoul experience. Bring a picnic blanket.

---

### Day 5: Gangnam & Departure

**Morning: COEX Starfield Library & Aquarium**
- **Best for:** All ages
- **Price:** FREE (library), ~$20/adult aquarium (optional)
- **Time needed:** 1-2 hours

The Starfield Library inside COEX Mall is Instagram-famous — 13-meter-tall bookshelves filled with 70,000 books. It's free, air-conditioned, and beautiful. The mall also has a massive food court, a Megabox cinema, and a SMTOWN K-pop museum.

**Lunch: Gangnam area — try samgyeopsal (Korean BBQ) at a family-friendly BBQ restaurant**

**Afternoon: K-pop & Departure**
- If you have time and your kids are K-pop fans, visit SMTOWN coexartium or K-Star Road in Gangnam. Then head to the airport.

## Best Family Hotels in Seoul

### Luxury Choice
**Lotte Hotel Seoul** — $200-400/night, Myeongdong. Connecting rooms, excellent pool, connected to Lotte Department Store. Family-friendly service.

**Grand Hyatt Seoul** — $180-350/night, Namsan. Huge rooms, pool with city views, kids' club, beautiful grounds.

### Mid-Range Excellence
**Holiday Inn Express Seoul Euljiro** — $80-130/night, near Myeongdong. Clean, reliable, great location near subway, breakfast included. Our top mid-range pick.

**ibis Ambassador Seoul Myeongdong** — $70-110/night. Compact but well-located. Myeongdong is walkable, and the airport bus stops nearby.

### Budget-Friendly
**Guesthouse Korea** — $30-50/night. Private family rooms with shared bathrooms. Breakfast included. Great for budget-conscious families.

## Practical Tips for Seoul with Kids

- **Seoul is very safe** — Kids can walk ahead of you on sidewalks without worry
- **Cold winters (Dec-Feb)** — Temperatures drop to -10°C. Layer up. Thermal underwear is essential
- **Hot summers (Jun-Aug)** — Humid with occasional rain. Pack light clothing and an umbrella
- **T-money card** — Get one at any convenience store ($2-3). It works on subway, bus, and in taxis. Kids 6-12 get discounted fares
- **English signage** — The subway system is well-signed in English. Google Maps doesn't work well for walking directions — use Naver Map or KakaoMap
- **Korean BBQ with kids** — Many BBQ restaurants have table grills. It's an interactive experience kids love
- **Airport transfer** — AREX Express Train from Incheon to Seoul Station is $8/person and takes 43 minutes. Much cheaper than taxis ($60-80)

## Plan Your Trip

For a family of four, budget $250-400/day for a comfortable trip (mid-range hotel, restaurants, 1-2 paid activities, subway transport).

**Best time to visit:** April-May (spring cherry blossoms) or September-October (autumn foliage). Both offer mild temperatures and beautiful scenery.

Seoul is a city that rewards curious families. It's safe enough to explore freely, fascinating enough to keep everyone engaged, and delicious enough to make every meal an adventure.
