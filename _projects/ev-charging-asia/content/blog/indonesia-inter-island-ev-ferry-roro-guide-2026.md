# Indonesia Inter-Island EV Ferry Guide: Taking Your EV Across the Archipelago via RORO [2026]

Indonesia is the world's largest archipelago — 17,000+ islands stretching 5,000 km from Sabang to Merauke. For EV drivers, this presents a unique challenge and an incredible opportunity. The backbone of inter-island vehicle travel is the **RORO (Roll-On/Roll-Off) ferry network**, operated primarily by ASDP Indonesia Ferry (state-owned) and private operators like Kaizen Ferry.

In 2026, Indonesia's EV ecosystem is maturing fast. PLN (Perusahaan Listrik Negara) has deployed **700+ SPKLU charging stations** across Java, Bali, Sumatra, and Lombok, and the Trans-Java Toll Road is now bookended by charging at both ends. The RORO ferries that connect Indonesia's major islands are increasingly EV-friendly — they accept electric vehicles at standard rates, and the terminals on both sides are gaining charging infrastructure.

This guide covers the three most important inter-island RORO routes for EV drivers, the charging infrastructure at every terminal, battery management strategy for sea crossings, and complete itineraries to help you plan your Indonesian island-hopping EV adventure.

## How RORO Ferries Work for EVs in Indonesia

RORO (Roll-On/Roll-Off) ferries operate with a simple premise: you drive your vehicle onto the ferry, the crew secures it on the vehicle deck, and you ride as a passenger during the crossing. For EV drivers, the process is nearly identical to petrol cars — with a few EV-specific considerations.

- **ASDP and Kaizen Ferry both accept EVs** at standard vehicle rates
- **Booking is recommended** — use the ASDP Ferry app or Kaizen Ferry app for advance tickets
- **No formal SOC restrictions** — but keeping under 80% is prudent for sea crossings
- **Documentation required:** STNK (vehicle registration), SIM (driver's license), booking confirmation
- **Vehicle dimensions matter** — standard EVs (Tesla Model Y, BYD Atto 3, Hyundai Ioniq 5, Wuling Air EV) fit easily; oversized vehicles need special arrangements
- **Cost:** Vehicle fares range from Rp 150,000 to Rp 1,800,000 depending on route and vehicle class

## EV Battery Strategy for RORO Ferries

| Crossing Duration | Recommended SOC Before Boarding | Expected SOC After Crossing | Strategy |
|------------------|-------------------------------|----------------------------|----------|
| **30-60 min** (Ketapang→Gilimanuk) | 65-75% | 60-70% | Minimal drain — charge at departure terminal |
| **1-2 hours** (Merak→Bakauheni) | 70-75% | 55-65% | Moderate drain — disable Sentry/dashcam |
| **3-4 hours** (Padangbai→Lembar) | 70-75% | 50-60% | Significant drain — pre-charge fully before arrival |

**Key EV tips for Indonesian ferries:**

- **Turn off Sentry Mode / Dashcam recording** — saves 5-8% battery over longer crossings
- **Enable Transport Mode** if your EV has it — prevents alarm triggers from the rocking motion
- **Crack windows slightly open (1-2 cm)** — vehicle decks can reach 35-40°C
- **Keep charging cable accessible** — in the boot, not the frunk — you'll need it on the other side
- **Download PLN Mobile app** — required to activate SPKLU chargers across Indonesia
- **Download offline maps for the destination island** — cell signal is patchy at sea

The **Ketapang–Gilimanuk** ferry is the busiest vehicle ferry in Indonesia, connecting Java's east coast (Banyuwangi) with Bali's west coast. It's the primary gateway for all vehicle traffic heading to Bali and sees hundreds of crossings daily.

## What to Bring for an Inter-Island EV Ferry Trip

- **Type 2 charging cable (Mode 3)** — essential for hotel AC charging on all islands
- **CCS2 adapter** — not typically needed (Indonesia is CCS2-native), but carry a CHAdeMO adapter if your EV supports it for older stations
- **Granny charger (portable EVSE)** — your emergency backup. A standard 220V Indonesian wall outlet adds 10-12 km/hour. Even a few hours of granny charging at a homestay or terminal office can save your trip
- **PLN Mobile app** — installed and registered before departure. Add GoPay, OVO, or QRIS payment method. Some SPKLU stations require an Indonesian phone number
- **ChargeIN app** — good backup for Bali and some Java chargers
- **Vehicle registration (STNK)** — required for ferry check-in
- **Offline Google Maps** — download maps for Java, Bali, Sumatra, and Lombok before you go
- **Join the "EV Indonesia" Telegram group** — real-time charger status updates from local EV drivers

## Practical Tips for Indonesian Ferry Travel with an EV

### Booking Ferry Tickets

- **ASDP Ferry app** — covers Merak–Bakauheni, Ketapang–Gilimanuk, and Padangbai–Lembar routes. Book 1-7 days in advance for peak periods
- **Kaizen Ferry app** — covers some Java-Bali routes with a premium service (faster boarding, better facilities)
- **Peak seasons** (Lebaran/Eid, Christmas-New Year, school holidays): book **7-14 days in advance** and expect queues of 2-4 hours at terminals
- **Low season:** walk-up is usually fine for all routes except Merak–Bakauheni, which is consistently busy
- **EV-specific:** When booking via the app, select "Mobil" (car) class. ASDP does not differentiate EV vs petrol pricing

### Best Times to Cross

| Route | Best Times | Avoid |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| **Ketapang→Gilimanuk** | Weekdays 6-9 AM or after 8 PM | Saturday mornings, Sunday afternoons, Lebaran week |
| **Merak→Bakauheni** | Weekdays early morning (4-7 AM) or late night (10 PM-2 AM) | Friday evenings (Jakarta weekend exodus), Monday mornings (return), holiday periods |
| **Padangbai→Lembar** | Weekdays 7-10 AM departures | Weekends June-August, MotoGP weekends, Nyepi period |

### Range Planning: Charge BEFORE the Ferry, Not After

This is the single most important rule: **always charge to 70-75% before boarding the ferry.** Here's why:

1. You don't know how long the queue will be — aircon while waiting in terminal traffic can drain 3-5% per hour
2. The ferry crossing itself drains 5-10% from phantom loads (BMS, tracking, security systems)
3. The destination terminal's charger might be occupied, broken, or have a queue
4. If you arrive at the destination with 30% or less, you risk range anxiety immediately after a long trip

**The rule of thumb:** Arrive at the departure terminal with enough range to reach the nearest DC charger on the other side — **plus 30% buffer.**

### What About Bali's Seasonal Traffic?

Bali has severe traffic during peak tourist season (June-August, December-January). The drive from Gilimanuk to Denpasar/Canggu (120 km) typically takes **2.5-3.5 hours** in normal conditions, but can stretch to **5-6 hours** during holiday weekends. Factor this into your range planning — heavy traffic with aircon can increase consumption by 10-15%.

### What to Do If the Terminal Charger Is Occupied

- **Check PLN Mobile app** for other nearby SPKLU stations — PLN shows real-time availability
- **Use the ChargeIN app** as a backup — covers Bali and some Java chargers
- **Head to a nearby hotel** — many hotels in Indonesia will let you use their wall box (even if not a guest) for a small fee (Rp 50,000-100,000)
- **Use a granny charger** at a terminal warung (small restaurant) — ask nicely, offer Rp 20,000-50,000 for the electricity
- **Join the local EV queue** — EV drivers in Indonesia are a friendly community and will often share real-time updates on which chargers are free

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I take any EV on an Indonesian RORO ferry?
Yes. ASDP and Kaizen Ferry accept all standard electric vehicles at normal vehicle rates. Standard EVs (Tesla Model Y, BYD Atto 3, Hyundai Ioniq 5, Wuling Air EV, Honda N-Van EV) fit easily. Oversized vehicles (4x4s with roof racks, Hummer EV) may need to check height/width limits — most ferry decks have 2.5m clearance.

### Do I need to book the ferry in advance for an EV?
During peak seasons (Lebaran, Christmas-New Year, school holidays), yes — book 7-14 days ahead through the ASDP app. Off-peak, same-day walk-up is usually fine for Ketapang-Gilimanuk and Padangbai-Lembar. Merak-Bakauheni is consistently busy — book 3-7 days ahead even off-peak.

### Is it safe to take my EV on an Indonesian ferry crossing?
Yes. Modern EV batteries are sealed and waterproof. The Sunda Strait and Lombok Strait are sheltered waters (not open ocean), and the ferry decks are covered. Your EV will be fine. The only precaution: ensure proper chocking and follow crew instructions for parking brake (some crews prefer it released on the deck for stacking).

### Can I charge at the ferry terminals?
For most major terminals, yes — but check the status before you go:
- **Merak:** ✅ Yes — 2x 50 kW DC + 4x AC
- **Bakauheni:** ✅ Yes — 3x 50 kW DC + additional SPKLU
- **Ketapang:** ✅ Yes — 1x 50 kW DC
- **Gilimanuk:** ✅ Yes — 2x 50 kW DC (NEW — early 2026)
- **Padangbai:** ❌ No — nearest DC is 20 km north at Amankila
- **Lembar:** ❌ No — nearest DC is 30 km north at Mataram

### What happens if there's a queue at the terminal charger?
Plan ahead. Use the PLN Mobile app to check charger availability. Have a backup plan: either arrive early, charge at a nearby town before approaching the terminal, or bring a granny charger for emergency use. The new Gilimanuk chargers (installed early 2026) have significantly reduced congestion at the Bali side.

### What's the easiest first-time Indonesian EV ferry route?
**Ketapang (Banyuwangi) → Gilimanuk (Bali)** is the easiest — it's a 30-minute crossing with ferries every 15 minutes, charging at both terminals, and Bali has excellent charging infrastructure on arrival. Perfect for a first inter-island EV trip from Surabaya or East Java.

### Is Lombok's charging network sufficient for an EV?
For a 3-5 day trip, yes — if you plan around Lombok's 8-10 public DC chargers and use hotel AC charging overnight. The network is strategically placed around the tourist circuit (Mataram → Senggigi → Mandalika). For full details, see our dedicated **[Bali to Lombok EV Ferry Guide](/blog/bali-lombok-ev-ferry-guide-2026)**.

### Can I drive an EV from Jakarta to Bali?
Absolutely. Take the Trans-Java Toll Road from Jakarta to Surabaya (780 km, fully electrified with SPKLU stations at every major rest area), then continue east to Ketapang (Banyuwangi) for the 30-minute ferry to Gilimanuk, Bali. The full Jakarta-to-Denpasar drive is approximately 1,000 km plus the ferry. Plan for 3-4 days of driving with charging stops every 2-3 hours.

### What's the best charging app for Indonesian inter-island travel?
**PLN Mobile** is the most essential app — it covers all SPKLU stations operated by PLN, which is the vast majority of public DC fast chargers in Indonesia. As a backup, **ChargeIN** covers many Bali chargers and some Java stations. For ferry booking, use the **ASDP Ferry app**. For real-time charger availability from the community, join the **"EV Indonesia" Telegram group**.

## The Bottom Line

Inter-island EV travel in Indonesia is not just possible — it's becoming genuinely practical in 2026. The three major RORO routes (Ketapang–Gilimanuk, Merak–Bakauheni, and Padangbai–Lembar) all have functional charging ecosystems on both sides, with new SPKLU installations at Gilimanuk and Bakauheni making the experience significantly smoother than even 12 months ago.

**Key takeaways:**
- **Java → Bali** (Ketapang–Gilimanuk) is the easiest route — 30 min crossing, charging at both terminals, ferries every 15 minutes. Start here for your first trip.
- **Java → Sumatra** (Merak–Bakauheni) requires the most planning — longer crossing, consistent crowds, but excellent terminal charging on both sides.
- **Bali → Lombok** (Padangbai–Lembar) is the most adventurous — 3-4 hour crossing, no terminal charging yet, but Lombok's strategic 8-10 DC points make it workable.
- **Charge to 70-75% before every ferry** — this is the single most important rule. Phantom drain plus uncertainty at the destination makes full-battery departures essential.
- **Bring a Type 2 cable and a granny charger** — hotel AC charging and emergency wall socket access are your backup lifelines.
- **PLN's 2026 expansion is changing the game** — new chargers at Gilimanuk, upgrades at Merak and Bakauheni, and planned installations at Padangbai mean the network is getting better every quarter.

Indonesia is uniquely suited to EV island-hopping. The distances between islands are short, the ferry network is extensive, and the charging infrastructure — while still concentrated on the Java-Bali-Sumatra corridor — is expanding rapidly. A well-planned EV road trip across Indonesia's islands is one of the most rewarding driving experiences in Southeast Asia, combining world-class scenery with the quiet, emissions-free joy of electric driving.
