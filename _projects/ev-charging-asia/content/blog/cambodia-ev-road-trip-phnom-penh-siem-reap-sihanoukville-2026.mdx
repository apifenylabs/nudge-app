# Cambodia EV Road Trip Guide: Phnom Penh to Siem Reap & Sihanoukville — Complete Charging Guide 2026

**Last updated: May 24, 2026**

Cambodia is the next frontier for EV road tripping in Southeast Asia. While the Kingdom of Wonder may not have Thailand's 9,000+ public chargers or Malaysia's highway networks, it's moving fast. As of early 2026, Cambodia has over **200 public charging stations** nationwide, with more than **10,500 registered electric vehicles** — and both numbers are accelerating.

For families, Cambodia offers something special: short distances between major destinations, flat terrain that's gentle on EV range, and increasingly smart hotel charging options. The two classic routes — Phnom Penh → Siem Reap (Angkor Wat) and Phnom Penh → Sihanoukville (beaches) — are both easily doable in a mid-range EV with 300 km+ real-world range.

In this guide, we cover:

- Phnom Penh's charging landscape (where to charge in the capital)
- Route 1: Phnom Penh → Siem Reap via National Highway 6
- Route 2: Phnom Penh → Sihanoukville via the Expressway
- EV-friendly hotels with charging
- Charging costs in USD and KHR (Riel)
- Practical tips for driving an EV in Cambodia
- The future outlook for Cambodia's EV infrastructure

## Cambodia's EV Landscape in 2026

Cambodia's EV transition is driven by a unique combination: **63% clean energy grid** (hydro + solar), government determination, and falling EV import prices. The Ministry of Mines and Energy (MME) launched the Prakas on EV Charging Services (No. 0389) in June 2025, establishing the legal framework for charging stations nationwide.

**Key numbers:**

| Metric | Figure |
|--------|:------:|
| Registered EVs (Sept 2025) | 10,568 |
| Public charging stations (Feb 2026) | 200+ |
| Clean energy share of grid | 63% |
| Planned ZO Motors fast-chargers by end 2026 | 200+ |
| AC charging tariff | 1,050 Riel/kWh (~$0.26) |
| DC fast charging (≤250 kW) | 1,350 Riel/kWh (~$0.33) |
| DC super-fast (>250 kW) | 1,450 Riel/kWh (~$0.36) |

The government aims to build a nationwide network of high-speed chargers along major national roads, with the goal of enabling EVs to recharge in just 30 minutes for 400 km of range. ZO Motors Cambodia alone plans 300 fast-charging stations nationwide, with 200 slated for 2026 and 70 more in 2027.

**The standard in Cambodia is CCS-2** for DC fast charging and **Type 2 (Mennekes)** for AC destination charging — the same as Thailand, which makes cross-border travel from Thailand seamless.

## Phnom Penh: Where to Charge in the Capital

Phnom Penh has the highest concentration of EV chargers in the country. Most are located at malls, luxury hotels, government buildings, and car dealerships. Here's where families can reliably charge in the city.

### AEON Malls

| Location | Charger Type | Speed | Notes |
|----------|:-----------:|:-----:|-------|
| AEON Mall Sen Sok | DC + AC | Up to 60 kW | Multiple chargers, convenient while shopping |
| AEON Mall Phnom Penh (Tuol Kork) | AC Type 2 | 7-22 kW | Good for top-up during grocery runs |
| AEON Mall Mean Chey | AC Type 2 | 7-22 kW | Newest AEON, fewer chargers |

AEON malls are the most reliable family-friendly charging spots in the city. You can park, charge for 1-2 hours while the kids enjoy the play areas, and return to a nearly full battery. Sen Sok has the best setup with DC fast charging.

### Hotels with Public Charging

Several Phnom Penh hotels allow non-guests to charge (often with a restaurant or café minimum spend):

| Hotel | Charger | Access |
|-------|:------:|--------|
| Rosewood Phnom Penh | AC Type 2 | Valet; restaurant/spa guests |
| Sokha Phnom Penh Hotel & Residence | AC Type 2 + DC | Hotel carpark, public access |
| Hyatt Regency Phnom Penh | AC Type 2 | Valet parking |
| Courtyard by Marriott Phnom Penh | AC Type 2 | Guest & visitor parking |

### Dealership Chargers

Most EV dealerships in Phnom Penh — including BYD (DG Motors), ZO Motors, and Mercedes-Benz — offer free or paid charging at their showrooms. DG Motors (on Street 271) has one of the most active charging hubs in the city with AC and DC options.

### Ministry of Public Works and Transport

The Ministry building itself hosts 4 charging stations — a visible sign of the government's commitment to e-mobility. These are publicly accessible during business hours.

> **Pro tip:** Download **PlugShare** and the **Fresh AI** app (which launched an EV Zone feature in 2026 mapping 200+ stations) before you arrive. Google Maps also shows many stations, but Fresh AI is more comprehensive for Cambodia.

## Route 1: Phnom Penh → Siem Reap (314 km via NH6)

**Distance:** ~314 km | **Driving time:** 5-6 hours | **Difficulty:** Easy to moderate | **Range requirement:** 350 km+ recommended

The road to Angkor Wat takes you along National Highway 6, a well-paved two-lane road (with occasional rough patches). The terrain is mostly flat — this is the Cambodian floodplain — so your EV will perform efficiently.

### Charging Strategy

**Option A: Midpoint charge at Kompong Thom (~167 km)**

Kompong Thom is the natural midpoint at roughly 167 km from Phnom Penh. There are now charging stations here:

- **ZO Motors Charging Station** (Kompong Thom) — DC fast charger, 30-50 kW
- **PTT Station with EV charger** — AC Type 2, good for a meal break
- **DG Motors partner station** — AC charging at a local hotel

You'll arrive with about 50% battery (depending on your EV), charge for 45-60 minutes while having lunch, and have plenty of range for the remaining 150 km to Siem Reap.

**Option B: Destination charging only (if you have 400 km+ range)**

If you're driving a long-range EV (Tesla Model 3 LR, BYD Atto 3 Extended, or similar), you can make the 314 km trip in one go. But we **strongly recommend** charging at Kompong Thom as a safety buffer — NH6 can have unexpected detours, and charging infrastructure is still sparse between towns.

**Option C: Hotel charging in Siem Reap**

Siem Reap has several hotels with AC destination chargers. If you book an EV-friendly hotel (see table below), you can arrive with a low battery and charge overnight at 7-22 kW Type 2.

### What to Expect on the Road

- **Road quality:** Generally good paved highway, with some bumpy sections near construction zones
- **Traffic:** Light to moderate outside towns; slow through Kompong Thom and Skun
- **Tolls:** No tolls on NH6
- **Food stops:** Kompong Thom has excellent roadside restaurants serving fish amok and lok lak
- **Kid stop:** Skun (about 75 km from Phnom Penh) is famous for its spider market — a quirky cultural stop your kids will never forget

> **Safety note:** NH6 has minimal street lighting between towns. Start early (7:00 AM) to arrive in Siem Reap before sunset. You'll also avoid the worst of the afternoon heat.

## Route 2: Phnom Penh → Sihanoukville (230 km via Expressway)

**Distance:** ~230 km | **Driving time:** 2-2.5 hours | **Difficulty:** Easy | **Range requirement:** 250 km+

The Phnom Penh–Sihanoukville Expressway (opened 2022) is Cambodia's first modern expressway and a game-changer for EV road trips. It cuts travel time from 4-5 hours on the old NH4 to just over 2 hours.

**Even better:** In 2025-2026, the expressway got **three new EV charging stations** at strategic points:

| Location | KM Marker | Direction | Charger Type |
|----------|:---------:|:---------:|:------------:|
| Kampong Speu Service Area | 63 km mark | Both directions | DC fast (60-100 kW) |
| Veal Renh Service Area | 109 km mark | Sihanoukville→Phnom Penh | DC fast (60-100 kW) |
| Prey Nob Service Area | 156 km mark | Both directions | DC fast (up to 150 kW) |

This means you can start in Phnom Penh with a full charge, skip the first stop, and top up at Prey Nob (156 km) if needed before the final 75 km to Sihanoukville. Or just charge at Kampong Speu for peace of mind.

### Driving on the Expressway

- **Speed limit:** 100-120 km/h (keep your EV in Eco mode for best range)
- **Toll:** ~$15 each way (paid at toll booths, cash or ABA card)
- **Traffic:** Light most days, moderate on Friday afternoons and Sunday evenings
- **Scenery:** Flat farmland giving way to palm-fringed hills as you approach the coast

### Charging in Sihanoukville

Sihanoukville's charging infrastructure is growing, driven by the resort industry:

- **Sokha Beach Resort** — AC Type 2 chargers in the carpark
- **Independence Hotel** — AC Type 2, available for guests
- **ZO Motors station** (city centre) — DC fast charging
- **PTT petrol station** (near Otres Beach) — AC Type 2

For families heading to **Koh Rong or Koh Rong Samloem**, park at the Sihanoukville Autonomous Port (secure parking, ~$5/day) and use the ferry. Your EV will still be at the same charge level when you return.

## Charging Costs in Cambodia (USD / KHR)

Cambodia's Electricity Authority (EAC) introduced regulated charging tariffs effective October 1, 2025:

| Charger Type | Rate (Riel/kWh) | Rate (USD/kWh) | Full Charge Cost* |
|:------------:|:---------------:|:--------------:|:-----------------:|
| AC (standard) | 1,050 Riel | ~$0.26 | ~$13.00 |
| DC fast (≤250 kW) | 1,350 Riel | ~$0.33 | ~$16.50 |
| DC super-fast (>250 kW) | 1,450 Riel | ~$0.36 | ~$18.00 |

*Based on a 50 kWh battery (e.g., BYD Atto 3).

For comparison, filling a petrol car with the equivalent range costs **$35-50** at Cambodia's pump prices (~$1.15/litre for gasoline). You're saving **55-65%** on fuel costs per trip.

**Payment methods:** Most chargers accept ABA Bank QR, cash (at manned stations), and some accept credit cards. We recommend loading up your **ABA mobile app** — it's the most widely accepted digital payment in Cambodia's EV ecosystem.

**Free charging:** Several hotels offer complimentary AC charging to guests. This can save you $10-15 per night — not huge, but it adds up over a week-long trip.

## Practical Tips for EV Driving in Cambodia

### Bring Your Own Type 2 Cable

This is the single most important piece of advice. Many destination chargers in Cambodia (especially hotel AC chargers) require you to provide your own Type 2 to Type 2 cable. A [decent 7.5-metre Type 2 cable](https://www.amazon.sg/dp/B0BCKCTDPK/?tag=evchargingasia-22) costs around $100-150 SGD and will save you from arriving at a hotel with a charger you can't use.

Also consider carrying:
- **CCS-2 portable charger** with a Schuko adapter for emergency top-ups from standard wall sockets
- **Multi-plug extension cord** (some hotel chargers are awkwardly placed)

### Use PlugShare and Fresh AI

- **PlugShare** — Global EV charging community map, actively used by Cambodia's small but growing EV community. Check recent check-ins for charger status.
- **Fresh AI (EV Zone)** — Launched February 2026, this Cambodian app maps 200+ stations with real-time availability. Essential for local travel.
- **Google Maps** — Shows many stations but lags on availability data.

### Border Crossing from Thailand or Vietnam

**Thailand → Cambodia (Poipet / Aranyaprathet):**
- **Charging standard:** Both countries use CCS-2 for DC charging and Type 2 for AC. No adapter needed — seamless!
- **Distance from border to Siem Reap:** ~160 km (2.5 hours on NH6)
- **Best strategy:** Charge at the PTT station in Aranyaprathet (Thailand side), then cross. There are chargers in Poipet town (Cambodia side) but they're less reliable.
- **Required documents:** Vehicle registration, insurance (cross-border coverage from your Thai insurer), passport with valid visa (eVisa available).
- **Vehicle permit:** Cambodia requires a temporary vehicle import permit at the border. Allow 30-60 minutes for processing. Cost: ~$10-20.

**Vietnam → Cambodia (Moc Bai / Bavet):**
- **Charging standard:** Vietnam uses CCS-2 + Type 2 for most newer chargers, but some older stations use CHAdeMO. Check compatibility.
- **Distance from border to Phnom Penh:** ~170 km (2.5 hours on NH1)
- **Best strategy:** Charge at the VinFast station in Moc Bai (Vietnam side) before crossing. The Bavet casino complex may have AC charging.
- **Required documents:** Vehicle registration, cross-border insurance, passport with visa. Vietnamese temporary export + Cambodian temporary import permits needed.

### General Driving Tips

- **Drive on the right** (Cambodia drives on the right, unlike Thailand)
- **Speed limits:** 60 km/h in towns, 90 km/h on national highways (posted), 100-120 km/h on expressway
- **Police checkpoints:** Common on NH6 and NH4. Always carry your passport, driving license (International Driving Permit accepted), and vehicle documents.
- **Fuel stations** often double as convenience stores with clean toilets — good for family pit stops even when you're not charging
- **Rainy season (May-October):** NH6 can flood in low-lying areas near the Tonle Sap. Check road conditions before departure. The expressway to Sihanoukville is less affected.

## Future Outlook: Cambodia's EV Policy and Infrastructure Plans

Cambodia is pushing hard on EV infrastructure, and the pace is accelerating:

### 2025-2026 Milestones

| Date | Milestone |
|:----:|-----------|
| June 2025 | MME Prakas No. 0389 — legal framework for EV charging stations |
| Sept 2025 | 10,568 EVs registered nationwide |
| Oct 2025 | EAC introduces regulated charging tariffs (1,050-1,450 Riel/kWh) |
| Oct 2025 | Government announces nationwide high-speed charger rollout plan |
| Jan 2026 | ZO Motors opens flagship charging station in Phnom Penh |
| Feb 2026 | Fresh AI launches EV Zone — 200+ stations mapped |
| 2026 (planned) | 200 new ZO Motors fast-chargers across 10+ provinces |
| 2027 (planned) | 70 additional ZO Motors stations |

### Policy Drivers

- **Clean energy advantage:** Cambodia's grid is 63% hydro and solar, making EVs genuinely green
- **Tax incentives:** EV import duties are being reduced to encourage adoption (currently ~30% lower than ICE vehicles)
- **UNDP support:** The UN Development Programme has funded charging stations in Battambang, Siem Reap, and Sihanoukville to fill gaps in remote areas
- **PTT partnership:** Thai oil giant PTT is installing EV chargers at its Cambodian petrol stations, mirroring its successful Thailand rollout

### What's Coming Next

- **Expressway expansion:** Plans to extend the Phnom Penh-Sihanoukville Expressway to the Thai border at Poipet and the Vietnamese border at Bavet — with integrated EV charging
- **Siem Reap new airport:** The new Siem Reap-Angkor International Airport (opened 2023) is being designed with EV charging capacity for rental fleets
- **Battambang-Koh Kong corridor:** A route through the Cardamom Mountains is being studied, which would open up western Cambodia for EV road trips

> **Reality check:** Cambodia is 5-7 years behind Thailand in EV infrastructure. But it's moving faster than any other CLMV country (Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, Vietnam) in terms of policy framework and private-sector investment. For adventurous families, now is the perfect time to explore — infrastructure is good enough for the main routes, and prices are still low.
