# Thailand Island EV Ferry Guide 2026: Koh Samui &amp; Koh Phi Phi from the Mainland

Thailand's islands are the crown jewels of Southeast Asian travel, and both Koh Samui and Koh Phi Phi rank among the most-visited destinations in the region. But if you're driving an EV, getting your vehicle to these islands — or deciding not to — requires careful planning.

This guide covers the two most popular island-from-mainland EV routes in Thailand:

1. **Koh Samui** — the only major Thai island with a car ferry, where you can drive your EV aboard and explore the full island
2. **Koh Phi Phi** — where no cars are allowed, so the EV journey ends at the mainland car park, with secure EV charging at the parking lot

We'll cover charging on both sides of each ferry, booking tips, costs, SIM recommendations, and emergency contacts.

---

## Part 1: Koh Samui — Driving Your EV onto the Island

Koh Samui is Thailand's second-largest island (after Phuket) and the only major Gulf island with a roll-on/roll-off car ferry. At roughly 50 km in circumference and 228 km², it's large enough that an EV is genuinely useful for getting around — and with ~12 Raceline charging locations, the infrastructure is adequate for a week-long stay.

### Route: Don Sak Pier (Surat Thani) → Koh Samui

The only vehicle ferry route to Koh Samui departs from **Don Sak Pier** (ท่าเรือดอนสัก) in Surat Thani province on the mainland. This is approximately 670 km south of Bangkok (7-8 hours driving) and 170 km north of Krabi (2 hours driving).

#### Ferry Operators & Schedules

| Operator | Route | Duration | Frequency | Vehicle Fare (one way) | Notes |
|----------|-------|----------|-----------|----------------------|-------|
| **Raja Ferry** | Don Sak → Na Thon (Samui) | 90 min | 6-8 daily (6:00-18:00) | ~฿500-600 per vehicle | Oldest operator, reliable, high frequency |
| **Seatran Ferry** | Don Sak → Na Thon (Samui) | 90 min | 4-6 daily (6:30-17:00) | ~฿550-650 per vehicle | Newer fleet, slightly more expensive |
| **Raja Ferry Express** | Don Sak → Na Thon (Samui) | 60 min | 2-3 daily | ~฿650-750 per vehicle | High-speed option, limited vehicle capacity |

**Important for EV drivers:**
- **Book at least 1-2 weeks in advance** during peak season (December-February, July-August, Songkran in April). Car ferry slots fill up fast.
- **Specify it's an EV** when booking. Some operators have EV-specific slots due to weight and safety considerations.
- **Arrive 45-60 minutes before departure** for check-in and vehicle loading.
- **Vehicle height limit:** Standard ferries accommodate vehicles up to 2.2 m. Tesla Model Y (1.62 m), BYD Atto 3 (1.61 m), MG4 (1.50 m) are all fine. Roof racks may need removal.
- **Battery level recommendation:** Charge to 70-75% before arriving at Don Sak. Ferry operators prefer under 80% for safety during sea crossing.

### Pre-Ferry Charging: Surat Thani & Don Sak Area

The last reliable charging before the ferry is in **Surat Thani city** (40 km from Don Sak Pier) or at **Don Sak itself**.

| Charger | Location | Type | Power | Cost (THB) | Distance to Don Sak Pier |
|---------|----------|------|-------|------------|--------------------------|
| **PEA Volta at Central Plaza Surat Thani** | 99/99 Moo 4, Khun Thale, Mueang Surat Thani | DC Fast | 120 kW | ฿7/min | 40 min (38 km) |
| **EA Anywhere at Lotus's Don Sak** | Don Sak District | DC Fast | 50 kW | ฿6.50/min | 5 min (3 km) |
| **PTT EV Station PluZ Surat Thani** | Phetkasem Road, Mueang Surat Thani | DC Fast | 50 kW | ฿7/min | 35 min (35 km) |
| **ChargeV at Big C Surat Thani** | 55/99 Moo 2, Tha Kham, Mueang Surat Thani | DC Fast | 60 kW | ฿6/min | 35 min (35 km) |

**Recommended charging strategy:**

1. **Primary stop: PEA Volta at Central Plaza Surat Thani** — This is your best option. Charge to 70-75% on the 120 kW chargers while grabbing a meal or coffee at the mall food court. It's on the way from Bangkok/Highway 4 and has 4 CCS2 connectors.
2. **Backup stop: EA Anywhere at Lotus's Don Sak** — Just 3 km from Don Sak Pier, this is the closest charger to the ferry. Use it for a quick top-up if you arrive earlier than expected or as a final top-off. Only 50 kW, so plan for ~20-30 min to add meaningful range.
3. **Avoid arriving below 30%** at Don Sak — the ferry wait can be 30-60 min, plus the 90 min crossing with minor phantom drain (5-10%) from the car's systems.

**Maps route for last charging:** Google Maps "PEA Volta Surat Thani Central" → then "Don Sak Pier" (38 km, 35 min drive).

### The Ferry Crossing: What EV Drivers Need to Know

| Step | Timing | What Happens |
|------|--------|--------------|
| **Arrival at Don Sak** | 45 min before departure | Queue at vehicle check-in booth. Present booking confirmation (printed or digital). Staff will direct you to the vehicle lane. |
| **Vehicle loading** | 15-20 min before departure | Drive onto the ferry following staff signals. Bumper-to-bumper parking on the deck. Handbrake on, gear in Park. |
| **Sea crossing** | 60-90 min | Exit the vehicle and go to the passenger cabin. Do NOT stay in the car. |
| **Arrival at Na Thon** | Upon docking | Wait for staff signal. Drive off the ferry. You'll exit directly onto Na Thon main road. |

**EV-specific ferry tips:**
- **Enable Transport Mode / Car Wash Mode** before boarding to disable alarm systems
- **Turn off Sentry Mode / Dashcam** (saves 5-8% battery over the crossing)
- **Leave climate control off** (the deck is shaded but can get hot — windows slightly open is better)
- **Do NOT leave charging cable connected** during the crossing
- **Have your destination charger pre-plugged** into Google Maps before you depart — cell reception can be patchy mid-crossing

### Driving Around Koh Samui

Koh Samui's ring road (Route 4169) circles the entire island — approximately **50 km** end to end. Even a short-range EV with 200 km of range can comfortably explore the entire island plus return to your accommodation without recharging on a single day trip.

**Power consumption notes for Koh Samui:**
- Flat ring road: normal consumption (~15 kWh/100 km)
- Inland hills (to Big Buddha, Khao Hua Jook viewpoint): +15-20% consumption
- Stop-and-go traffic in Chaweng: +10% consumption but regen braking helps
- **Total island circuit:** 50 km × 1.2 (hilly terrain factor) = ~60 km equivalent — trivial for any modern EV

**Key points of interest with nearby charging:**

## Part 2: Koh Phi Phi — EV to Mainland Parking, Then Ferry

Koh Phi Phi (including Phi Phi Don and Phi Phi Leh) is a **car-free island**. No vehicles are permitted on the island — not even mopeds for tourists (only local service vehicles). This means your EV journey ends at the nearest mainland parking lot.

### How It Works: EV to Ferry Workflow

1. **Drive your EV** to the mainland pier (your choice of Krabi, Phuket, or Phuket Airport area)
2. **Park your EV** at a secure long-term car park with EV charging
3. **Take a passenger ferry** (no vehicles) to Koh Phi Phi (90 min-2 hrs)
4. **On Koh Phi Phi**, walk, cycle, or take a long-tail boat taxi — no car needed
5. **Return**, collect your charged EV from the mainland parking

### Mainland Pier Options

| Pier | Location | Ferry to Phi Phi | Duration | Best For |
|------|----------|------------------|----------|----------|
| **Rassada Pier (Phuket Town)** | Phuket Town, 45 min from Phuket Airport | Multiple operators | 2 hr | Most frequent departures, best parking options |
| **Bang Rong Pier (Phuket)** | North-east Phuket, 25 min from airport | Chao Koh Group | 2 hr | Closest to Phuket Airport |
| **Krabi Pier (Klong Jilad / Ao Nang)** | Krabi Town / Ao Nang | Tigerline, Phi Phi Cruiser | 90 min-2 hr | Shorter crossing, Krabi has good EV charging |

### Koh Phi Phi Car-Free Reality

Once you park at the mainland pier, here's what to expect on Phi Phi:

- **No cars, no mopeds for tourists** — only local service vehicles (tractors, supply trucks)
- **Walk everywhere** — Phi Phi Don is only 3.5 km long and 2 km wide at its widest point
- **Long-tail boats** for day trips to Phi Phi Leh (Maya Bay), Bamboo Island, Mosquito Island — ฿1,200-2,000 per boat per trip, no EV needed
- **Bicycle rental** — ฿100-200/day, but the island is hilly so walking is often easier
- **Your EV stays safely parked** — this is the only case where you're truly leaving your EV behind, but with secure guarded parking at ฿100-200/day, it's a better deal than airport parking in most cities

| Charger Type | Speed | Locations on Samui | When to Use |
|-------------|-------|-------------------|-------------|

**Verdict:** DC chargers are your primary tool on Koh Samui. AC chargers are for overnight convenience only.

## The Bottom Line

**Koh Samui** is the premier Thai island destination for EV drivers. The car ferry from Don Sak Pier is well-established, the charging infrastructure on the island (~12 Raceline locations) is adequate for any length of stay, and the 50-km ring road makes range anxiety a non-issue. Charge at PEA Volta Central Surat Thani before the ferry, then rely on Solaris Koh Samui (DC) and hotel AC charging once on the island.

**Koh Phi Phi** is a different proposition entirely — no cars allowed, period. The EV road trip ends at the mainland car park in Phuket or Krabi. But the upside is that Phuket and Krabi both have excellent, fast EV charging infrastructure, and the parking costs are reasonable (฿100-200/day). Your EV will be clean, charged, and waiting for you when you return from one of Thailand's most beautiful car-free islands.

For the ultimate Thai island EV adventure, combine both in one trip: drive from Bangkok to Don Sak → Samui (3-4 days), then drive south to Phuket/Krabi → Phi Phi (3-4 days). You'll experience the best of both worlds — driving an EV on a large island and parking it for a car-free paradise.
