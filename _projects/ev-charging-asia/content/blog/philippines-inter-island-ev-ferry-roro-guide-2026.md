# Philippines Inter-Island EV RORO Guide: Taking Your EV Between Islands [2026]

The Philippines is an archipelago of over 7,600 islands, connected by an extensive Roll-On/Roll-Off (RORO) ferry network that's perfect for EV road trips. Unlike Malaysia or Indonesia where ferries are a niche option, RORO ferries in the Philippines are a primary mode of inter-island transport — and many now accept EVs.

This guide covers the most popular inter-island EV RORO routes, charging infrastructure at ferry terminals, battery management strategy for sea crossings, and recommended EV island-hopping itineraries.

## How RORO Ferries Work for EVs in the Philippines

RORO (Roll-On/Roll-Off) ferries have ramps that allow vehicles to drive directly onto the vessel. Here's what EV drivers need to know:

- **Most major RORO operators accept EVs**, but require advance booking
- **Booking is mandatory** — walk-up EV slots are rarely available
- **Vehicle dimensions matter** — height, width, and weight restrictions apply
- **Battery level restrictions** — most operators prefer under 80% SOC for sea crossing
- **Documentation** — OR/CR (Official Receipt/Certificate of Registration) required
- **Cost** — Vehicle rates range from PHP 1,500 to PHP 12,000 depending on route and vehicle size

## Pre-Ferry EV Preparation

### Documentation Required

- ✅ Original OR/CR (or certified digital copy via LTMS portal)
- ✅ Valid driver's license
- ✅ Vehicle insurance (Comprehensive recommended for ferry crossings)
- ✅ Booking confirmation (printed or digital)
- ✅ Valid government ID for all passengers
- ✅ Travel tax receipt (if applicable, for inter-regional routes)

### Battery Strategy for RORO Crossings

| Route Duration | Recommended SOC Before Boarding | Expected SOC After Crossing | Strategy |
|---------------|-------------------------------|----------------------------|----------|
| Under 2 hours | 65-75% | 60-70% | Minimal drain — charge at departure terminal |
| 2-6 hours | 70-75% | 55-65% | Moderate drain — disable Sentry/dashcam |
| 6-14 hours (overnight) | 75-80% | 45-55% | Significant drain — pre-cool/pre-heat cabin before boarding |
| 18+ hours (Manila→Iloilo) | 80% maximum | 35-45% | Aircon must run for crew comfort — factor in 10-15% drain |

**Critical tips for Philippine RORO ferries:**
- **Disable Sentry Mode / Dashcam recording** — this drains 1-3% per hour
- **Turn off cabin pre-conditioning** before boarding
- **Roll windows down slightly** (if safe) — makes loading/unloading communication easier
- **Transport Mode** — set your EV to Transport/Car Wash mode to disable alarm sensors during the rolling sea crossing
- **Handbrake release** — RORO ferries usually chock the wheels and require the handbrake to be released for deck stacking

### AC Charging at Islands Without DC Fast Charging

Some islands (Siquijor, Camiguin, Siargao, parts of Palawan beyond Puerto Princesa) have limited or no DC fast charging. In these areas:

- **Resorts with EV AC charging** are your best option
- **Standard 220V wall outlet** (13A) can add 50-60 km overnight
- **Type 2 portable charger** is essential — bring your own
- **Plan routes within 150 km from your overnight charging point**
- **Join the Philippine EV community on Facebook** for real-time charger status updates

## Ferry Booking Tips for Philippine EV Trips

1. **Book 1-2 weeks in advance** for popular routes (Batangas→Mindoro, Cebu→Bohol)
2. **Booking 3-4 weeks in advance** for overnight/ocean-going routes (Manila→Iloilo, Batangas→Puerto Princesa)
3. **Specify "Electric Vehicle" when booking** — some operators have limited EV slots
4. **Confirm vehicle dimensions** (height, width, weight) — some older RORO vessels have tighter decks
5. **Peak seasons** (Holy Week, All Saints' Day, Christmas, Chinese New Year, summer months) — book 3-4 weeks ahead
6. **Cancellation policies vary** — Montenegro has flexible rebooking, 2GO charges 50% cancellation within 48 hours
7. **Check-in window** — arrive 1-2 hours before departure for vehicle processing (longer for overnight routes)

## Essential Tips for Philippine EV Ferry Travel

### Before Departure
- [ ] Book all ferry tickets online (avoid walk-up for EVs)
- [ ] Confirm EV slot with operator 48 hours before departure
- [ ] Download offline maps for destination islands
- [ ] Join "EV Philippines" Facebook group for real-time updates
- [ ] Bring your Type 2 portable charging cable
- [ ] Check PlugShare for latest charger status on destination island
- [ ] Pack snacks and water — some RORO ferries have limited food options

### At the Terminal
- [ ] Arrive 1-2 hours before departure (longer for overnight/ocean routes)
- [ ] Proceed to vehicle loading area (usually marked)
- [ ] Present booking confirmation + OR/CR at the check-in booth
- [ ] Follow staff instructions for deck placement — weight distribution matters
- [ ] Set parking brake off (per crew instruction), chocks will be placed
- [ ] Disable Sentry/Dashcam, roll windows up partially
- [ ] Leave charging cable accessible (in frunk/trunk, not under cargo)

### During the Crossing
- [ ] Remain in passenger lounge — do not stay in your vehicle
- [ ] Keep your phone charged for arrival coordination
- [ ] For overnight ferries, book a cabin (PHP 1,500-3,000 per person)
- [ ] If seas are rough, EV battery and electronics are safe — the vehicle deck is enclosed

### On Arrival
- [ ] Return to vehicle deck when crew announces arrival
- [ ] Follow exit ramp instructions — one vehicle at a time
- [ ] Drive to the nearest DC charger within 10-15 minutes of disembarking
- [ ] Allow 30-40 minutes for a top-up to 80% (enough for a day of island exploration)

## FAQs: Philippine EV Inter-Island Travel

### Can I take a Tesla on a Philippine RORO ferry?
Yes. Tesla Model 3 and Model Y are the most common EVs on Philippine ferries. The Model X and Model S are also accepted but may have width restrictions on smaller RORO vessels. Tesla Superchargers are not available in the Philippines — use Shell Recharge or Lotus Energy (CCS2).

### Are BYD EVs accepted on ferries?
Yes. BYD Atto 3, Dolphin, and Seal are increasingly common in the Philippines and are accepted on all major RORO routes. The BYD Atto 3's height (1.61m) is well within ferry clearance limits.

### Do I need to book a cabin for overnight RORO ferries?
Not mandatory, but highly recommended. The vehicle deck is not passenger accessible during the crossing, and the passenger lounge can be uncomfortable for overnight trips. Cabins cost PHP 1,500-3,000 per person and include a bed, air con, and basic amenities.

### What if my destination island has no fast charging?
Islands like Siquijor, Camiguin, Bantayan, and Malapascua have no DC fast charging. Strategy: arrive with 65-75%, drive conservatively (small islands are only 20-40 km across), and rely on resort AC charging (standard wall outlet at 13A = 50-60 km range added overnight). A fully charged EV can explore an entire small island for 2-3 days without needing a DC charge.

### Is it safe to charge at Philippine resorts?
Generally yes. Most mid-range to luxury resorts with EV charging use dedicated EVSE units (7 kW AC). Some budget resorts only offer standard wall outlets — confirm in advance. Always carry your own Type 2 to Type G (UK 3-pin) adaptor for Philippine power sockets.

### Which is the easiest first-time EV ferry route?
**Batangas → Calapan (Mindoro)** is the easiest — it's a short 45-minute crossing with multiple daily departures, good charging on both sides, and the island is small enough to explore without range anxiety. Perfect for a first-time EV inter-island adventure from Manila.

### Can I drive all the way from Manila to Cebu via RORO?
Not directly in one trip. The route is Manila → Batangas → RORO to Mindoro → RORO to Caticlan (for Panay) → RORO to Iloilo → RORO to Bacolod → RORO to Dumaguete → RORO to Cebu. This requires 5-6 separate ferry crossings and 3-4 days of travel. Most EV travelers fly to Cebu and pick up a rental EV there, or do the direct Manila → Iloilo overnight RORO (18-22 hours).

## The Bottom Line

Inter-island EV travel in the Philippines is not only possible — it's an incredible adventure. The RORO ferry network is extensive, operators are increasingly EV-friendly, and charging infrastructure at major terminals is growing fast. The most accessible routes (Batangas→Mindoro, Cebu→Bohol→Siquijor) are well-served with charging on both sides of the crossing.

The key to success: **book ahead, arrive early, charge before boarding, and always have a fallback plan for AC charging on smaller islands.** The savings over petrol (60-70% on energy costs) plus the unique experience of island-hopping in an EV make it well worth the planning effort.

For your first inter-island EV trip, start with the Manila → Batangas → Calapan → Puerto Galera route — it's the most forgiving introduction to Philippine EV ferry travel, with charging coverage that removes almost all range anxiety.
