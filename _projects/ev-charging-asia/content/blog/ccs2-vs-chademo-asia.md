# CCS2 vs CHAdeMO: Which Charging Standard Should You Pick in Asia?

One of the biggest decisions when buying an EV in Asia is choosing which charging standard to support. The wrong choice could mean struggling to find compatible chargers on your road trips — or paying premium prices for slow charging.

## Key Takeaways
- CCS2 is the global standard — dominant in Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, and growing everywhere
- CHAdeMO is essential for Japan but declining globally — Nissan Leaf and older EVs only
- Most new Asian charging stations offer both connectors, but CCS2 stalls are always more numerous
- Future-proof your EV purchase by prioritizing CCS2
- For frequent Japan travelers, CHAdeMO is still essential — but check if your car supports both

## CCS2 (Combined Charging System)

CCS2 is rapidly becoming the global standard. It combines a Type 2 AC connector with two DC pins below, supporting up to 350kW charging.

**Used by:** BMW, Mercedes, Hyundai, Kia, Tesla (Model 3/Y), and most new EVs from European and Korean manufacturers.

**Dominant in:** Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, and increasingly in China alongside GB/T.

**Advantages:**
- Higher charging speeds (up to 350kW vs 50-100kW typical CHAdeMO)
- Larger selection of compatible EVs
- Growing faster in new installations across Southeast Asia
- Standard across Europe — good for international compatibility

**Disadvantages:**
- Very limited in Japan outside newer stations
- Requires an adapter for China's GB/T network

## CHAdeMO

CHAdeMO is the Japanese standard, developed by TEPCO, Nissan, and Mitsubishi. The name stands for "Charge de Move" — and it was first to market with DC fast charging.

**Used by:** Nissan Leaf, Mitsubishi Outlander PHEV, and some older EVs. Tesla offered CHAdeMO adapters but discontinued them.

**Dominant in:** Japan (excellent coverage), declining elsewhere.

**Advantages:**
- Excellent coverage across Japan — especially on expressways
- Many stations are still CHAdeMO-only in rural Japan
- V2G (Vehicle-to-Grid) capability built into the standard
- Bidirectional charging ready

**Disadvantages:**
- Maximum power typically 50-100kW (newer 2.0 supports 400kW but rare)
- Declining globally — fewer new stations adding CHAdeMO
- Limited EV models available
- Minute-based pricing in Japan can be expensive

## Which Should You Choose?

### For Most Drivers in Southeast Asia

**CCS2 is the clear choice.** Broader availability, faster charging speeds, and better future-proofing. Every new DC charging station installed in Thailand, Malaysia, and Singapore includes CCS2. CHAdeMO is often added as a secondary option.

### For Japan-Focused Drivers

**CHAdeMO is still essential.** Japan has excellent CHAdeMO coverage — especially along expressways and in rural areas. However, the number of dual-standard stations (CHAdeMO + CCS2) is growing. If you frequently drive in Japan, look for an EV that supports both standards.

### The China Question

China uses GB/T — a completely different standard. Neither CCS2 nor CHAdeMO works natively. You need a GB/T adapter regardless of which standard your EV supports. Always check before crossing the border from Hong Kong to mainland China.

## Country-by-Country Breakdown

| Country | Primary Standard | Secondary | Notes |
|---------|----------------|-----------|-------|
| Singapore | CCS2 | CHAdeMO (declining) | Most new stations are CCS2-only |

| Malaysia | CCS2 | CHAdeMO | Gentari and JomCharge favor CCS2 |
| Japan | CHAdeMO | CCS2 (growing) | Dual-standard expanding on expressways |
| Hong Kong | CCS2 | CHAdeMO | All new installs are CCS2 |
| China | GB/T | — | Unique standard, adapters required |

## Charging Speed Comparison

|----------|:------------:|:---------:|:---------------------------:|
| CCS2 | 50-150kW | 350kW | 20-35 min |
| CHAdeMO | 50kW | 100kW (typical) | 40-60 min |
| CHAdeMO 2.0 | 100kW | 400kW | 15-25 min (rarely available) |

## The Verdict

Future-proof your EV purchase by prioritizing CCS2. It's the standard that's growing everywhere — in Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, Hong Kong, and even alongside CHAdeMO in Japan. The only real reason to need CHAdeMO is if you frequently drive a Nissan Leaf in Japan or plan to keep an older EV for several more years.

For the best of both worlds, choose an EV that supports CCS2 natively but can accept a CHAdeMO adapter. This gives you access to Japan's extensive CHAdeMO network while benefiting from CCS2's faster speeds and broader coverage everywhere else.
