# Seoul to Busan EV Road Trip: 5-Day Family Journey via Gyeongju, Daegu & Korea's Ultra-Fast Charging Corridor

South Korea has the most advanced and fastest public EV charging infrastructure in Asia. The 400 km Seoul–Busan corridor along the Gyeongbu Expressway is lined with ultra-fast E-Pit and Chaevi chargers offering **up to 350 kW** — meaning a 10-minute stop can add 200+ km of range. This guide covers a 5-day family itinerary hitting Seoul, Daegu, Gyeongju, and Busan with all the charging details you need.

## Key Takeaways

- Korea's **Gyeongbu Expressway** (Seoul–Busan) has chargers every 20–30 km — one of the densest EV corridors in the world
- The 400 km journey costs approximately **₩18,000–28,000 (USD $13–20)** in charging vs ₩65,000–80,000 for petrol
- **E-Pit (SK Networks)** and **Chaevi (LG-backed)** offer 350 kW ultra-fast chargers — a 10–18 minute stop is all you need
- The intermediate city of **Gyeongju** is a must-stop: it's Korea's "museum without walls" with UNESCO World Heritage sites
- Korea's EV apps accept foreign credit cards more reliably than Japan — **EV Infra** and **E-Pit** apps are all you need
- **T-money or Hi-pass card** needed for expressway tolls (~₩17,000 Seoul–Busan)

## The Ultra-Fast Charging Corridor: What Makes Korea Special

South Korea's EV infrastructure is remarkable for two reasons: **speed** and **density**.

### Speed
Korean ultra-fast chargers from E-Pit and Chaevi deliver up to **350 kW** — among the fastest in Asia. A compatible EV (Hyundai Ioniq 5/6, Kia EV6, Genesis GV60) can charge from 10% to 80% in just **18 minutes**. Even the Charge myEV network offers 200 kW chargers.

### Density
On the Gyeongbu Expressway alone, there are over 30 charging locations between Seoul and Busan. The longest gap between chargers is just 28 km (near Cheonan). Range anxiety simply doesn't exist on this route.

## Charging Networks in Korea

### 1. E-Pit (SK Networks) — The Premium Choice

E-Pit's cherry-red chargers are Korea's showpiece. They offer 350 kW from sleek, well-lit stations with canopies. Most E-Pit stations have 4–6 stalls.

**Locations on route:** Osan (40 km), Cheonan (90 km), Gimcheon (200 km), and 6 other spots
**Cost:** ₩370–450 per kWh (about ₩15,000–20,000 for a full charge on a 60 kWh battery)
**App:** E-Pit (iOS/Android) — accepts foreign Visa/Mastercard
**Tip:** E-Pit stations have automatic payment via license plate recognition if you register your plate in the app

### 2. Chaevi (LG / GS Caltex) — Fast-Growing Alternative

Chaevi is the fastest-growing network in 2026, with 350 kW chargers at GS Caltex gas stations along the expressway. Their stations are conveniently right next to convenience stores and restrooms.

**Locations on route:** GS Caltex Anseong (75 km), GS Caltex Gimcheon (200 km), GS Caltex Gyeongju IC
**Cost:** ₩350–430 per kWh
**App:** Chaevi (iOS/Android) — supports Apple Pay and Google Pay

### 3. Charge myEV (Korean EV Association) — The Backbone

The most widespread network with 200 kW chargers. Slower than E-Pit/Chaevi but more numerous, especially at rest areas.

**Cost:** ₩310–380 per kWh
**App:** Charge myEV (iOS/Android)

### 4. Tesla Superchargers V4

As in Japan, Tesla's V4 Superchargers (250 kW) are now open to non-Tesla EVs with an adapter. Two locations near Cheonan and Daegu.

## 5-Day Family Itinerary

### Day 1: Explore Seoul (charges at hotel)

Start with a fully charged EV. Most major hotels in Seoul now offer EV charging:

- **Lotte Hotel Seoul** (Myeongdong): 4 × 7 kW AC, complimentary for guests
- **Grand Hyatt Seoul** (Namsan): 6 × 11 kW AC + 1 × 100 kW DC
- **Four Seasons Seoul** (Gwanghwamun): 4 × 7 kW AC

**Family activities in Seoul:**
- **Lotte World** — massive indoor/outdoor theme park with EV charging in the parking lot (Chaevi, 200 kW)
- **COEX Aquarium** — one of Asia's largest, right next to Starfield Library (free entry)
- **Bukchon Hanok Village** — traditional Korean houses with a great view of the city

### Day 2: Seoul → Daegu (240 km)

**Departure:** Leave Seoul after morning rush (post-10 AM). Charge to 90% before leaving — your hotel's overnight AC charger should have this covered.

**Charging stop:** Location A: E-Pit Osan Expressway (40 km from Seoul) — 350 kW, 10 min to 80%. Or better: skip the first stop and charge at **E-Pit Cheonan** (90 km, lunch break).

**Lunch at Cheonan SA (rest area):** Korea's highway rest areas are legendary. Try the *dalkgalbi* (spicy stir-fried chicken) at Cheonan SA. The service area has an E-Pit 350 kW station right next to the food court.

**Arrive Daegu:** Check into your hotel, then explore.

**Daegu charging:** 
- E-Pit Daegu Station (350 kW, 6 stalls)
- Chaevi at GS Caltex Daegu IC
- Most hotels in Daegu's Dongseongro district offer AC charging

**Family activities in Daegu:**
- **Daegu Spiky World** (E-World 83 Tower area) — interactive science museum designed for kids
- **Apsan Observatory** — cable car up to the mountain for panoramic city views at sunset
- **Seomun Market** — fantastic night market with *bibimbap*, *kalguksu*, and *yakgwa* (honey cookies)

### Day 3: Daegu → Gyeongju (80 km, 1 hr)

Short driving day — perfect for families.

**Charge before leaving:** Top up at E-Pit Daegu Station (15 min).

**Arrive Gyeongju:** Drop your bags, then head to the historic sites. Everything in Gyeongju is within 15 km of the city center.

**Gyeongju charging:**
- Chaevi at GS Caltex Gyeongju IC (200 kW)
- E-Pit Gyeongju Expressway (150 kW)
- **Hilton Gyeongju** — 4 × AC chargers for hotel guests
- **Komfortel Gyeongju** — 2 × 7 kW AC

**Family activities in Gyeongju:**
- **Bulguksa Temple** — UNESCO World Heritage, Korea's most famous temple. 20 min drive from city center
- **Seokguram Grotto** — stunning Buddha statue with sea views. Note: steep walk from parking lot
- **Cheomseongdae** — East Asia's oldest astronomical observatory (7th century). The kids will love the ancient history
- **Gyeongju National Museum** — free admission, excellent children's exhibits
- **Bomun Lake** — family-friendly walking and biking trails. Rent a 4-person bike for ₩15,000/hr
- **Gyeongju World Culture Expo Park** — hands-on cultural experiences and light shows in the evening

**Pro tip:** Rent a *hanbok* (traditional Korean clothing) for the family in Gyeongju — kids usually love it, and it makes for amazing photos at Bulguksa Temple.

### Day 4: Gyeongju → Busan (100 km, 1.5 hr)

**Departure:** After a morning visit to any sites you missed, head south. No charging needed if you're above 50%.

**Arrive Busan:** Check into Haeundae area, leave the car at the hotel, and use public transit for the day (Busan's subway is excellent).

**Busan charging:**
- E-Pit Haeundae (350 kW, 6 stalls — near the beach)
- Chaevi at Shinsegae Centum City (200 kW, 8 stalls — world's largest department store)
- Charge myEV at Busan Station (200 kW)
- **Paradise Hotel Busan** (Haeundae) — 4 × AC chargers for guests
- **Shilla Stay Haeundae** — 2 × 11 kW AC

**Family activities in Busan:**
- **Sea Life Busan Aquarium** — Haeundae Beach. One of Korea's best aquariums. Penguin feeding show at 2 PM
- **Haeundae Beach** — Korea's most famous beach. The kids can play while you watch from a café
- **Gamcheon Culture Village** — colorful hillside village with art installations and photo spots
- **Busan Children's Museum** — interactive exhibits for ages 3–12
- **Songdo Beach Cable Car** — glass-bottom cable car over the sea

### Day 5: Busan Exploration + Return

**Morning:** Visit **Jagalchi Fish Market** (Korea's largest seafood market) — the second floor has restaurants that cook your purchase fresh. Kids find the octopus tanks fascinating.

**Afternoon:** Either head back to Seoul by EV (charge at E-Pit Cheonan on the way) or take the KTX high-speed train (2 hr 15 min, ~₩60,000) and have your EV transported separately — some rental companies offer one-way rentals with Seoul return.

## Seasonal Considerations

### Spring (Mar–May) — Cherry Blossom
Gyeongju's cherry blossoms are spectacular. Bulguksa Temple and Bomun Lake are covered in blooms. Book accommodation 3 months ahead. Range unaffected by weather.

### Summer (Jun–Aug)
Hot and humid (30–35°C). Beach season in Busan — Haeundae is packed. A/C might cut range by 5–10%, but chargers are so frequent it won't matter.

### Autumn (Sep–Nov) — Best Season
Crisp air, perfect range, gorgeous foliage at Bulguksa Temple and the Naejangsan area (optional detour near Jeonju).

### Winter (Dec–Feb)
Cold but dry. Range drops 15–25% in sub-zero temperatures. The ultra-fast chargers still work well. Gyeongju is less crowded and magical in light snow.

## Renting an EV in Korea

Major rental companies now offer EVs:

- **Lotte Rent-a-Car** — Largest fleet. Hyundai Ioniq 6, Kia EV6, Genesis GV60. ~₩80,000–150,000/day. English booking available.
- **Socar** — Car-sharing via app. Tesla Model 3 and Hyundai Ioniq 5 available. ₩15,000–25,000/hour. 
- **Jeju Rent-a-Car** — Good for island trips but also has mainland branches.

**Pro tip:** Book a Hyundai Ioniq 6 or Kia EV6 — their 800V architecture makes full use of Korea's 350 kW chargers.

## The Verdict

Korea's Seoul-to-Busan EV road trip is arguably the best family EV road trip experience in Asia. The ultra-fast charging corridor means you spend minutes, not hours, waiting to charge. The intermediate stops in Daegu (urban energy) and Gyeongju (UNESCO heritage) break up the journey perfectly. And Busan's beaches and seafood are the perfect payoff.

For a 5-day family itinerary, this route is hard to beat — especially if your EV supports 800V fast charging.
