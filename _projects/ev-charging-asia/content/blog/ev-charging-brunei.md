# EV Charging in Brunei: A Practical Guide for Electric Vehicle Owners in the Sultanate (2026)

Brunei Darussalam — the tiny, oil-rich sultanate on the island of Borneo — is not the first country that comes to mind for electric vehicle travel. With petrol prices among the lowest in the world (around BND $0.53 per litre), the financial case for going electric is weaker here than almost anywhere else in Southeast Asia. Yet something unexpected is happening: Brunei's EV numbers are growing.

As of January 2026, **2,000 plug-in hybrid and fully electric vehicles** are registered in Brunei, according to Transport Minister Pengiran Shamhary. While the vast majority are PHEVs, pure battery EVs have climbed from just 19 units in 2023 to over 136 by early 2025 — and that number is accelerating in 2026 as more models hit the local market.

The charging infrastructure, while modest, is expanding. **40 charging stations with 45 charging points** now dot the country, concentrated in Bandar Seri Begawan but reaching out along the major highways to Tutong, Kuala Belait, and even the Temburong district via the stunning Temburong Bridge.

This guide covers everything you need to know about EV charging in Brunei in 2026: the networks, the routes, the import rules, the best hotels with charging, and the equipment you should pack before you go.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Brunei has **40 public charging stations (45 charging points)** nationwide, mostly 7–22 kW AC with a handful of DC fast chargers including one 350 kW Porsche unit
- **2,000 PHEVs + EVs** are registered as of early 2026, though pure BEVs remain under 200
- **No import duty** on EVs, but **20% excise duty** applies — no special EV purchase subsidies currently
- The **Temburong Bridge** (30 km, Southeast Asia's longest) connects the main landmass to the Temburong district and is EV-friendly with a charger at the Bangar end
- Best route: **Bandar Seri Begawan → Tutong → Kuala Belait** is 110 km on smooth highway — easily doable even in a short-range EV
- Most EV charging is free at hotels and government buildings — the network is publicly funded and usage is often complimentary
- Porsche, BYD, and ChargEV are the key charging providers

## Current EV Landscape in Brunei

### Low Fuel Prices, High Barriers

Brunei's unique economics are the elephant in the room. Petrol costs roughly BND $0.53 per litre — that's about USD $0.39. Filling a typical sedan costs less than BND $30. Against that backdrop, the primary reasons Brunei residents buy EVs are **environmental consciousness, tech enthusiasm, and long-term positioning** rather than fuel savings.

That said, Brunei's **National Climate Change Policy (BNCCP)** names electric vehicles as one of its ten key strategies. The country committed to reducing carbon emissions by focusing on the transport sector, which accounts for a sizable share of emissions. An **EV Joint Task Force** was established in 2019 to develop local production capabilities and infrastructure.

### EV Import Rules

| Detail | Information |
|--------|-------------|
| Import duty on EVs | 0% (no import duty) |
| Excise duty | 20% |
| Purchase subsidies | None currently |
| Road tax for EVs | Reduced rate compared to ICE |
| Driving side | Left-hand traffic (RHD vehicles) |
| Main import port | Muara Port |
| Vehicle age restriction | No confirmed cap (verify before importing used EVs) |

**Key takeaway:** While there's no import duty, the 20% excise duty plus the already-high upfront cost of EVs means a new EV in Brunei can cost significantly more than a comparable petrol car. BYD has emerged as the top-selling brand, with the **BYD Sealion 6** gaining particular popularity in 2025–2026.

> **Pro tip for importers:** Because Brunei follows left-hand traffic, used EVs from Japan (RHD, CHAdeMO) are physically compatible with local roads but may face charging connector challenges as Brunei moves toward CCS2. Used EVs from Malaysia and Singapore are ideal candidates for import.

## Charging Infrastructure in Brunei

### Overview of the Network

Brunei's charging network is small but functional. As of mid-2026, there are **40 stations with 45 charging points** across all four districts. The network is largely government-funded and maintained through partnerships with private operators.

| District | Estimated Charging Points | Coverage |
|----------|--------------------------|----------|
| Brunei-Muara (incl. BSB) | ~25 | Best — multiple stations in the capital |
| Tutong | ~6 | Moderate — highway coverage improving |
| Belait (incl. Kuala Belait, Seria) | ~8 | Good — oil town infrastructure |
| Temburong | ~2 | Minimal — one station at Bangar |

### Charger Types

Most of Brunei's public chargers are **AC Type 2 (7–22 kW)** units, suitable for destination charging while you shop, dine, or work. This means a full charge from empty takes 4–8 hours on a typical EV. The network is actively adding DC fast chargers in 2026.

| Charger Type | Power | Locations | Time to Full Charge (60 kWh battery) |
|-------------|-------|-----------|---------------------------------------|
| AC Type 2 | 7–22 kW | Hotels, malls, government buildings | 3–8 hours |
| DC Fast (CCS2) | 50–150 kW | Select fuel stations, dealerships | 30–60 min (to 80%) |
| Porsche Turbo Charger | 350 kW | Porsche Centre Brunei, BSB | 18 min (to 80%, compatible models) |

### Key Charging Locations in Bandar Seri Begawan

1. **Porsche Centre Brunei** — 350 kW DC ultra-fast charger (CCS2). Compatible with most modern EVs via CCS2. Located at the Gadong commercial area. The only true ultra-fast charger in the country as of 2026.

2. **ChargEV @ The Mall, Gadong** — 22 kW AC Type 2. Free charging for shoppers. Located in Brunei's premier shopping destination.

3. **ChargEV @ Times Square** — 22 kW AC Type 2. Central location near restaurants and hotels.

4. **ChargEV @ Brunei International Airport** — 7 kW AC. Overnight charging for travellers leaving their car at the airport.

5. **BYD Brunei / GWM Showroom (Gadong)** — 60 kW DC CCS2 + AC. BYD dealership charging available for all EVs, not just BYD customers.

6. **Government Buildings (multiple)** — 7–22 kW AC. Many ministries and government complexes have free public charging.

### How to Pay

This is one of the best parts of charging in Brunei: **almost all public chargers are free to use.** The government subsidises charging as an incentive to encourage adoption. A few private chargers (like the Porsche 350 kW unit) may require registration, but generally you can just plug in and charge.

For the ChargEV network, download the **ChargEV app** (works in Malaysia and Brunei) to locate stations and check availability, though payment is rarely needed.

## Recommended Routes for EV Road Trips in Brunei

Brunei is small — barely 5,765 km² — but its road network is modern and well-maintained, making it surprisingly good for EV road trips. The challenge is that the country is split into two disconnected landmasses: the main western part (Brunei-Muara, Tutong, Belait districts) and the isolated Temburong district to the east, separated by Malaysia's Limbang district. The **Temburong Bridge** (officially the Sultan Haji Omar 'Ali Saifuddien Bridge, completed in 2020) solved this beautifully with a 30 km sea crossing — now Southeast Asia's longest bridge.

### Route 1: Bandar Seri Begawan → Kuala Belait (110 km)

**Distance:** 110 km one way | **Drive time:** 1.5 hours | **Difficulty:** Easy

Brunei's main artery runs from the capital west through Tutong, Seria, and on to Kuala Belait along the **Muara–Tutong Highway** and **Tutong–Kuala Belait Highway**. This is a flat, dual-carriageway road with minimal elevation change.

**Charging strategy:**
- Start with a full charge in BSB
- Optional top-up at Tutong (7–22 kW AC, while you grab lunch)
- Arrive in Kuala Belait with plenty of range — charge at the hotel or at ChargEV stations in town
- Return with a brief stop in Tutong if needed

**Estimated consumption:** ~18–20 kWh (80% of a typical 60 kWh battery)

**Best stops:**
- **Tutong Town** — short detour to see the Tutong district's traditional stilt villages
- **Seria Energy Lab** — interactive energy museum, free entry, fun for families
- **Kuala Belait** — the oil town at Brunei's western edge, with a waterfront promenade and local seafood restaurants

### Route 2: Bandar Seri Begawan → Temburong via Temburong Bridge (85 km)

**Distance:** 85 km one way | **Drive time:** 45 min | **Difficulty:** Easy

The Temburong Bridge drive is the most spectacular road trip in Brunei. The 30 km sea crossing features sweeping views of the Brunei Bay on both sides, with the bridge arcing gracefully across the water. On a clear day, you can see the rainforest-covered hills of Temburong and the distant mountains of Malaysian Borneo.

**Charging strategy:**
- Full charge in BSB (essential — Temburong has minimal charging)
- One charger available in **Bangar** (AC 7 kW) — verify availability on PlugShare before you go
- Plan for a day trip — the round trip is only 170 km, well within range of any EV with 250+ km range

**What to do in Temburong:**
- **Ulu Temburong National Park** — primary rainforest accessed by longboat (book a tour — {{KLOOK}})
- **Bangar Town** — the district capital, small and charming
- **Flying frog walk** at the park's canopy walkway — 60 metres above the forest floor

### Route 3: Bandar Seri Begawan City Loop (10–40 km)

The capital loop covers the major attractions without ever needing to worry about range. BSB is small, with everything within 20 km of the waterfront.

**Suggested loop:**
1. **Omar Ali Saifuddien Mosque** — the iconic golden-domed mosque
2. **Kampong Ayer (Water Village)** — the world's largest stilt settlement, accessible by water taxi
3. **Royal Regalia Museum** — the Sultan's ceremonial regalia and gifts
4. **Gadong Night Market** — Brunei's best street food
5. **Jame' Asr Hassanil Bolkiah Mosque** — the largest mosque in Brunei

**Charging:** Park at The Mall, Gadong or Times Square and charge while you explore. Both have free AC charging.

## Equipment Checklist for EV Charging in Brunei

Brunei's charging infrastructure is growing but still limited. Packing the right equipment makes the difference between a smooth trip and getting stuck.

### Essential Items

- **Type 2 to Type 2 (Mennekes) charging cable** — The most common cable for AC charging in Brunei. Most public stations don't provide a tethered cable.
- **CCS2 charging cable** — For DC fast charging (the Porsche 350 kW station and newer DC chargers use tethered cables, but having your own CCS2 cable is wise backup).
- **Type 2 to Schuko (domestic plug) adapter** — For emergency charging at homestays, guesthouses, or private homes. Brunei uses UK-style 3-pin (Type G) sockets — bring a **Type G to Schuko adapter** as well.
- **Portable EVSE (Granny Charger)** — A Level 1 charger with a Type G (UK 3-pin) plug. This lets you charge from any standard wall socket at 2–3 kW. Essential for Temburong trips where public charging is scarce.
- **Phone mount and USB-C cable** — For navigation and charging apps. Bring a 12V car charger too.

### Nice to Have

- **Portable tyre inflator** — Roads are excellent in Brunei, but a puncture in Temburong without nearby service stations is a long wait.
- **Window sunshades** — Brunei is hot and humid year-round. Sunshades protect the cabin and reduce air-conditioning load.
- **Rain ponchos and towel** — For charging in the rain (and it WILL rain — Brunei has a tropical rainforest climate).
- **OBD-II Bluetooth scanner** — For monitoring battery health and real-time consumption on longer drives. Pairs with apps like ABRP (A Better Routeplanner).

## Safety Tips for Driving an EV in Brunei

### General Safety

1. **Brunei is very safe** — It has one of the lowest crime rates in Asia. You can leave charging cables in your boot without worry. Petty crime is almost non-existent.

2. **Speed cameras are everywhere** — Brunei has fixed and mobile speed cameras. Speed limits are strictly enforced: 50 km/h in towns, 80–90 km/h on highways. Fines are steep.

3. **Fuel stations are sparse on certain routes** — Brunei has fewer petrol stations than you'd expect. The BSB–Kuala Belait highway has stations at Tutong and Seria, but the Temburong Bridge has none. Plan accordingly for both petrol and EV range.

4. **Wildlife on the road at night** — Monitor lizards, civets, and the occasional monkey cross rural roads. Drive carefully after dark, especially on the Seria bypass and Temburong approach roads.

5. **Heavy rain reduces visibility** — November to March is the wet season. Sudden downpours reduce visibility to near zero. If you're caught in a heavy storm, pull over safely and wait it out.

### EV-Specific Safety

6. **Always verify chargers before relying on them** — Call ahead, check the ChargEV app, or ask in the Brunei EV Facebook group. A reported charger may be occupied, broken, or ICEd (parked over by a petrol car).

7. **Carry your granny charger on Temburong trips** — Temburong has exactly one confirmed public charger. If it's occupied or broken, your only option is a wall socket at a homestay.

8. **The 350 kW Porsche charger is CCS2 only** — If you drive an older CHAdeMO EV (Nissan Leaf), you won't be able to use the country's fastest charger. Check compatibility before you go.

9. **Brunei's heat will increase consumption** — Air conditioning is mandatory year-round. Expect 15–20% higher consumption than WLTP ratings. Factor this into route planning.

10. **Border crossings are limited** — While you can theoretically drive from Brunei into Sarawak (Malaysia), cross-border EV travel remains complex. Malaysia's charging infrastructure doesn't extend to the remote Sarawak border crossings near Brunei. Stick to intra-Brunei driving for now.

## Planning Your Brunei EV Trip: Step by Step

### How to Bring an EV into Brunei

1. **For tourists:** Brunei allows temporary vehicle import. Ensure your EV's charging connector is CCS2 compatible (the regional standard). Process through Muara Port and pay the necessary temporary import duties.

2. **For expats:** If you're relocating to Brunei, you can import one vehicle duty-free as part of your relocation allowance. Used EVs under 3 years old are typically allowed. Engage a local customs broker for the paperwork.

3. **For residents:** Buy from authorized dealers (BYD, GWM/Ora, Porsche, BMW i-series). The most popular models in Brunei are the BYD Sealion 6, BYD Atto 3, ORA Good Cat, and Porsche Taycan.

## FAQ: EV Charging in Brunei

### 1. Is EV charging in Brunei free?

**Yes, most public charging stations in Brunei are free to use.** The government subsidises charging as an incentive for EV adoption. Some private stations (like dealership chargers and the Porsche 350 kW unit) may require registration but are generally also complimentary. Always confirm beforehand.

### 2. How many public EV chargers are there in Brunei?

As of early 2026, there are **40 charging stations with 45 charging points** nationwide. The majority are AC Type 2 (7–22 kW), with one 350 kW DC ultra-fast charger at Porsche Centre Brunei and a growing number of 50–60 kW DC chargers at BYD and GWM dealerships.

### 3. Can I drive my EV from Brunei to Malaysia (Sarawak)?

**It is possible but not recommended without serious planning.** The land border crossings between Brunei and Sarawak (Malaysia) at Sungai Tujoh and Kuala Lurah have no dedicated EV charging facilities on either side. Malaysia's Sarawak charging network is sparse compared to Peninsular Malaysia. Stick to intra-Brunei driving unless you have confirmed charging on the Sarawak side.

### 4. What EV models are available to buy in Brunei?

BYD is the market leader, with the **BYD Sealion 6** (PHEV) and **BYD Atto 3** (BEV) being the most popular models. Other available brands include **GWM/Ora** (Good Cat), **Porsche** (Taycan), **BMW** (i4, iX, i7), and **Mercedes-Benz** (EQS, EQE). Several dealers bring in limited units — expect wait times for popular models.

### 5. Do I need a special driving licence for an EV in Brunei?

**No.** A standard Brunei driving licence (or International Driving Permit for tourists) is sufficient for driving an EV. The same traffic rules apply. Remember: Brunei drives on the **left-hand side** (RHD vehicles), much like Malaysia and Singapore.

### 6. Is the Temburong Bridge safe for EVs?

**Absolutely.** The 30 km Temburong Bridge is a modern, well-maintained structure with no special restrictions for EVs. It's a smooth, flat drive across Brunei Bay with spectacular views. Just ensure you start with a full charge — Temburong has limited charging infrastructure on the other side.

### 7. What's the best time of year for an EV road trip in Brunei?

**February to April and July to September** offer the best conditions. These are the drier months with less rainfall. November to January is the monsoon season with heavy daily downpours. Brunei's temperature is fairly constant year-round (24–32°C), so the main variable is rain.

## Final Thoughts on EV Charging in Brunei

Brunei is an unconventional choice for an EV road trip. The charging infrastructure is small, the petrol prices are laughably low, and the entire country is smaller than many single cities in neighbouring Malaysia. But that's also its charm.

You can drive from one end of Brunei to the other in under two hours. The roads are smooth, well-lit, and almost traffic-free compared to Bangkok or Kuala Lumpur. The Temburong Bridge alone is worth the trip — a breathtaking 30 km sea crossing that rivals any coastal drive in Southeast Asia. And with free charging at most stations, your "fuel" cost for an entire weekend of driving is effectively zero.

Is Brunei an EV destination on the level of Thailand or Malaysia? Not yet — and it may never need to be. But for the traveller who wants to explore one of Southeast Asia's most underrated countries in an electric car, Brunei offers a uniquely stress-free experience. Small, safe, and slowly but surely going electric.
