From the skyscrapers of BGC and Makati to the pristine beaches of Boracay and El Nido, the heritage streets of Iloilo, and the pine-scented air of Baguio — there's never been a better time to hit Philippine roads in an EV.

We've ranked the Philippines' top 10 EV-friendly hotels based on charger availability, speed, reliability, location, and overall family experience. Whether you're island-hopping in Visayas or road-tripping through Luzon, these hotels will keep you charged and comfortable.

## EV Charging in the Philippines: What You Need to Know

### Charging Networks

| Network | Coverage | Connector | App Required | Payment |
|---------|----------|-----------|:------------:|---------|
| ChargeSC | Metro Manila, Clark, Cebu, Davao | CCS2, Type 2 | Yes (ChargeSC App) | Credit card, GCash |
| EVroam | Metro Manila, major cities | CCS2, Type 2 | Yes (EVroam App) | Credit card, Maya |
| Meralco eVehicle | Metro Manila (selected malls, offices) | CCS2, Type 2 | Yes (MSmart App) | Credit card |
| Cleanfuel EV | Cleanfuel petrol stations nationwide | AC Type 2 | No (pump attendant) | Cash/GCash |
| Hotel destination chargers | Growing (free for guests) | Type 2 (mostly) | Varies | Complimentary |

### Connector Standards

- **The Philippines is adopting CCS2 and Type 2**, aligned with ASEAN's unified standard
- CHAdeMO stations exist but are being phased out — mostly found at older Meralco stations
- Tesla owners need a CCS2-to-NACS adapter (available at ChargeSC stations)
- **Bring your own Type 2 cable** — many hotel chargers don't provide one
- The Philippines uses 220V, 60 Hz (same as US plugs — bring international adapters)

### Apps to Download Before Your Trip

1. **ChargeSC** — the largest private EV network in the Philippines, essential for Cebu and Clark
2. **EVroam** — growing network covering Metro Manila and key provincial routes
3. **PlugShare** — community-checked charger reliability across the archipelago
4. **Waze** — better traffic routing than Google Maps for Metro Manila's chaotic streets
5. **GCash** — not charging-related, but essential for almost every digital payment in the Philippines
6. **Grab** — ride-hailing and food delivery; useful for days you want to leave the EV parked

## Tips for Booking EV-Friendly Hotels in the Philippines

- **Metro Manila is the easiest EV destination.** Most charging infrastructure is concentrated in BGC, Makati, Alabang, Quezon City, and Clark. Book your Manila hotel first, then plan onward.
- **Provincial charging is expanding fast.** ChargeSC and EVroam are aggressively building out stations along the SLEX, NLEX, and TPLEX corridors. By mid-2026, most major provincial routes will have at least one public DC charger within range.
- **Call ahead to verify charger status.** Many Philippine hotels that advertise "EV charging" may have a single Type 2 outlet that's occasionally blocked. Call the concierge 24 hours before arrival to confirm availability and request a reserved EV bay.
- **Baguio and Tagaytay require the most planning.** Both mountain destinations consume more battery due to elevation gain. Arrive with at least 30% battery and plug in immediately. The descent from Baguio back to Manila will give you free range via regenerative braking.
- **Boracay and other island destinations:** No private vehicles are allowed on Boracay. Use the hotel's mainland parking and charging facility. For Palawan (El Nido and Puerto Princesa), EV charging is limited — check PlugShare before attempting.
- **Electrical safety:** The Philippines uses 220V/60 Hz with two-pin flat (US-style) and two-pin round sockets. Bring a universal adapter for your EV's Type 2 cable.
- **Summer (March-May) heat affects battery performance.** Charge to 80% instead of 100% on hot days to preserve battery health. Most hotel parking is covered, which helps.
- **Typhoon season (June-November) can disrupt charging.** During major storms, some public chargers may be offline. Hotels with backup generators usually keep their chargers running — confirm this when booking.
