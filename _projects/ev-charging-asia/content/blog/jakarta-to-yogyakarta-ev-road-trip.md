# Jakarta to Yogyakarta EV Road Trip: Java's Cultural Heart by Electric Car

Driving an EV from Jakarta to Yogyakarta along the Trans-Java Toll Road has gone from a niche experiment to a genuinely practical trip. Indonesia's EV infrastructure has expanded dramatically — over 15,000 public charging points nationally as of mid-2026 — and the 570 km Jakarta–Yogyakarta route now has reliable SPKLU (Stasiun Pengisian Kendaraan Listrik Umum) and PLN charging stations at every major rest area. This guide covers the full route, charging stops, cultural highlights, and family-friendly tips.

## Key Takeaways

- The 570 km Jakarta–Yogyakarta route along the Trans-Java Toll Road requires **2–3 charging stops** in a mid-range EV (400+ km range)
- Total charging cost is approximately **IDR 250,000–400,000 (USD $15–25)** compared to IDR 700,000–900,000 for petrol
- **SPKLU (PLN)** and **ChargeIN (state-owned)** are the most reliable networks on the toll road, with chargers at rest areas every 60–80 km
- **GoTo Electric and Blue Bird** have growing charger networks at convenience stores and parking lots
- The best charging strategy: charge at **KM 149 (Cipali)** and **KM 379 (Bawen)** — strategic mid-points with food courts and family amenities
- Yogyakarta has over 20 public charging points for exploring the city and nearby Borobudur and Prambanan temples

## Charging Networks on the Route

### 1. SPKLU (PLN) — The Government Backbone

PLN's SPKLU network is the most widespread on Java's toll roads. Their charging stations are located at official rest areas (Tempat Istirahat dan Pelayanan / TIP) with clean restrooms, mosques, and food courts.

**Locations:** KM 40, KM 149, KM 229, KM 379, KM 429, KM 487 on the Trans-Java route
**Charger types:** 60 kW–120 kW CCS2, some 50 kW CHAdeMO
**Cost:** IDR 2,450–3,000 per kWh (via SPKLU app)
**App:** PLN Mobile / SPKLU (iOS/Android)

### 2. ChargeIN (PT Energi Pelabuhan Indonesia / IPC-TMI)

A fast-growing network with modern 120 kW CCS2 chargers. ChargeIN stations tend to be newer than SPKLU and less crowded.

**Locations:** KM 229 (shared with SPKLU), KM 379, KM 487
**Cost:** IDR 2,700–3,200 per kWh
**App:** ChargeIN (iOS/Android)

### 3. GoTo Electric

Gojek's EV charging network, mostly concentrated in Jakarta, Bandung, and Surabaya. Growing presence at Alfamart and Indomaret convenience stores.

**Cost:** IDR 2,500 per kWh
**App:** GoTo Electric (iOS/Android)

### 4. Stasiun Pengisian Kendaraan Listrik (Private Operators)

Various private operators (TronS, MPMRent, BYD Indonesia) have installed chargers at malls and hotels but coverage is sparse on the toll road itself.

## Detailed Step-by-Step Route

### Stage 1: Leaving Jakarta

**The biggest challenge** of this road trip is simply getting out of Jakarta. Traffic on the Jakarta–Cikampek Toll Road (Japek) can be heavy even on weekends. 

**Best departure time:** Leave by 6 AM on weekdays, or 5 AM on weekends. Avoid Friday afternoons (exodus to Bandung and Central Java) and Monday mornings (return to Jakarta).

**Pre-trip charging:**
- Charge to 90%+ the night before
- Best overnight options: your hotel (increasingly common), SPKLU at Senayan City (120 kW), or ChargeIN at Grand Indonesia (120 kW)
- Backup: SPKLU at KM 6 Japek toll road (right after the CBD toll gate)

**Jakarta charging network tips:**
- **SPKLU locations:** Senayan City, Kota Kasablanka, fX Sudirman, and 8 other malls
- **ChargeIN:** Grand Indonesia, Pacific Place, Sensus Building
- **GoTo Electric:** Select Alfamart and Indomaret in South and West Jakarta
- **PLN Mobile app** shows real-time availability for all SPKLU stations

### Stage 2: Jakarta → Cipali KM 149 (150 km, ~2 hr)

The first 150 km takes you from Jakarta through Karawang and Subang to the first proper charging stop at KM 149 Palimanan (Cirebon area).

**Charging recommendation:** Charge at **SPKLU KM 149 Rest Area** (120 kW CCS2, 4 connectors). It's a large rest area with multiple food options and a children's playground.

**Alternative:** If you're running low before KM 149, SPKLU KM 86 (Cikopo) also has chargers but tends to be crowded.

**What to do while charging (25–40 min):**
- Grab *Nasi Jamblang* (Cirebon-style steamed rice with side dishes)
- Let the kids play at the playground
- Stretch your legs — the rest area has decent walking paths
- Check your next charger availability on the PLN Mobile app

### Stage 3: Cipali KM 149 → Bawen KM 379 (230 km, ~3 hr)

This is the longest segment — across the Cipali toll road (one of Java's longest continuous toll sections, 116 km) and on toward Semarang.

**Charging recommendation:** **PLN SPKLU or ChargeIN at KM 379 Bawen Rest Area** (120 kW CCS2). This is the most important charging stop — don't skip it. The next reliable charging is over 100 km away.

**KM 379 Amenities:**
- Large food court with Soto, Sate, and Bakso
- Musholla (prayer room)
- Clean toilets with attendants
- Small playground for children
- Mini-mart (Indomaret)

**Pro tip:** KM 379 is a major rest area and can get busy. If all SPKLU stalls are occupied, check ChargeIN — they're often co-located with fewer users.

### Stage 4: Bawen KM 379 → Yogyakarta (140 km, ~2 hr)

The final stretch takes you through the hills south of Semarang, past Ungaran, and down the toll road to Yogyakarta.

**Optional charging stop:** PLN SPKLU Ungaran (KM 429, 100 kW) — a good top-up point if you arrived at KM 379 with low charge and want extra margin for exploring Yogyakarta.

**Arriving in Yogyakarta:** Take the Ring Road (Jalan Lingkar Selatan or Jalan Magelang) to your hotel. Many hotels in the Malioboro area and northern suburbs now offer overnight AC charging.

## 4-Day Family Itinerary: Yogyakarta

### Day 1: Arrive + Malioboro Evening

**Afternoon:** Arrive, check into your hotel, charge the car overnight. Best hotels with EV charging:

- **The Phoenix Hotel Yogyakarta** — AC 7 kW, complimentary for guests, Merdeka area
- **Hyatt Regency Yogyakarta** — 4 × AC 7 kW, Kaliurang area with great view of Merapi
- **Aryaduta Hotel Yogyakarta** — 2 × AC 7 kW, Malioboro area

**Evening:** Walk along Jalan Malioboro — the famous shopping street comes alive at night with street food, puppeteers, and souvenir stalls. Kids love the *becak* (cycle rickshaws) and the *andong* (horse-drawn carriages).

**Dinner:** Try *Gudeg* (Yogyakarta's signature jackfruit stew) at Gudeg Yu Djum or Gudeg Bu Tjitro.

### Day 2: Borobudur Temple (40 km from Yogyakarta)

**Morning (pre-6 AM):** The best time to visit Borobudur is at sunrise. The 40 km drive takes about 1 hour. Enter through the Manohara Gate.

**Charging:** SPKLU Borobudur Parking Lot (60 kW CCS2) — charge while you explore. Also, Taman Wisata Candi has 2 AC chargers.

**Why sunrise matters:** The silhouette of Borobudur's stupas against the rising sun, with Mount Merapi and Merbabu in the background, is one of the most iconic views in Southeast Asia. The temperature is cooler and crowds thinner.

**Family tips:**
- The temple has 2,672 relief panels — make a game of finding animals and daily life scenes
- Take water — the climb to the top involves 9 terraces
- The Manohara Hotel next door has a pool you can use with admission
- Borobudur is stroller-friendly up to the fourth terrace; you'll need to carry it after that

**Afternoon:** Return to Yogyakarta, have lunch at *Lesekahan* (traditional low-table dining), and rest. Visit the **Sonobudoyo Museum** (free entry, excellent puppet collection) if the kids have energy.

### Day 3: Prambanan Temple + Ramayana Ballet

**Morning (8 AM):** Drive 17 km east to Prambanan Temple — a UNESCO World Heritage Hindu temple complex. Allow 2–3 hours to explore.

**Family tips:**
- The main temple (Candi Siwa) is 47 meters tall — the kids will be impressed
- Much more open and less crowded than Borobudur
- Candi Sewu (adjacent Buddhist temple complex) is a short walk through the grounds
- Horse-drawn carts inside the complex for tired little legs

**Lunch:** Eat at the food stalls near the Prambanan parking lot — they serve excellent *nasi goreng* and *sate klatak*.

**Afternoon:** Visit **Keraton Yogyakarta** (Sultan's Palace) — the highlight is the gamelan performance for visitors. Or visit **Taman Sari** (Water Castle) — a former royal garden with underground tunnels the kids can explore.

**Evening:** **Ramayana Ballet at Prambanan** (8 PM, Tuesday–Thursday and weekends) — an outdoor performance of the Ramayana epic with the floodlit temple as backdrop. Book tickets in advance. It's magical for all ages.

**Alternative:** At Taman Sari, you can watch a more accessible 1-hour version of wayang wong (traditional Javanese dance-drama) at 10 AM daily.

### Day 4: Merapi Jeep Tour + Return

**Morning:** Take a **Merapi Lava Tour Jeep** — an unforgettable 3-hour tour around the slopes of Mount Merapi, Java's most active volcano. Tours depart from the Kaliurang area. You'll see lava remains from 2010, drive through villages buried in ash, and visit the Museum Merapi. The jeeps are open-air and kids absolutely love them.

**Charging note:** SPKLU at Kaliurang area (60 kW CCS2) — charge while you explore.

**Afternoon:** Drive back to Jakarta or head to Solo for the Solo Batik Carnival. If returning, retrace the route with charging stops at KM 379 (Bawen) and KM 149 (Cipali).

## Seasonal Considerations

### Dry Season (May–October) — BEST TIME
Clear skies, perfect for sunrise at Borobudur. Roads are dry and range is optimal. Book hotels months ahead for July–August.

### Rainy Season (November–April)
Frequent downpours, especially afternoon. Wipers and A/C (defogging) may reduce range by 5–10%. Some rest area flooding is possible near KM 86 (Cikopo). Bring umbrellas and rain jackets. Borobudur in the rain is still atmospheric but you won't see sunrise.

## Renting an EV in Indonesia

- **MPM Rent** — Indonesia's largest car rental with EV options. Hyundai Ioniq 5 and BYD Atto 3. ~IDR 700,000–1,500,000/day. Branches at Soekarno-Hatta Airport and major cities.
- **Blue Bird** — Taxi company now offering EV rentals through their Blue Bird Rent-a-Car service. Toyota bZ4X available.
- **TRAC** — ASTRA's rental arm. Various EVs available at Jakarta locations.

## The Verdict

The Jakarta to Yogyakarta EV road trip is a journey through the heart of Java: from the dense metropolis of Jakarta, across the vast rice paddies of West Java and the teak forests of Central Java, to the cultural capital of Yogyakarta. The Trans-Java Toll Road is well-maintained and the charging network on this corridor is increasingly reliable. 

Is it as seamless as Korea or Japan? Not quite yet — you'll want a 400+ km EV, charge planning is essential, and the apps can still be glitchy at times. But for 2026, it's absolutely doable and the savings over petrol are substantial. The payoff — Borobudur at sunrise, Prambanan at dusk, and the warmth of Javanese hospitality — is worth every minute of planning.
