# Philippines EV Charging Guide 2026: Complete Guide for EV Owners &amp; Visitors

The Philippines is one of Southeast Asia's most exciting — and challenging — EV markets. With over 60,000 EVs registered as of early 2026, 912 publicly accessible charging stations across the country, and a government that has pivoted hard toward electric mobility, the archipelago is rapidly catching up with its ASEAN neighbours.

But the Philippines presents unique challenges: an archipelagic geography requiring ferry crossings between islands, a charging network heavily concentrated in Metro Manila with sparse coverage in the Visayas and Mindanao, and frequent typhoons that test both grid reliability and outdoor charging equipment. For EV owners who plan carefully, however, the rewards are extraordinary — driving through the rugged beauty of Luzon's mountain roads, island-hopping in the Visayas with EV-friendly ferries, and exploring the urban centres of Cebu and Davao with zero tailpipe emissions.

This comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know about EV charging in the Philippines in 2026: which network to join, what every kWh actually costs, how to set up home charging, the best road trip routes, government incentives still active, and a city-by-city breakdown of charging density.

## Key Takeaways

- **The Philippines has 912+ publicly accessible charging stations** (as of March 2026), with 250+ DOE-accredited EV charging station providers operating across the country
- **Public DC fast charging costs PHP 25–45/kWh**, translating to roughly PHP 400–700 per 100 km — roughly 40–60% the cost of petrol (PHP 1,000–1,200/100 km for a typical sedan)
- **Home charging is significantly cheaper at ~PHP 12/kWh** (Meralco residential tariff), costing only PHP 190 per 100 km
- **ACMobility leads the market** with the widest network, followed by ChargeSC (best mall coverage) and EVroam (fastest-growing highway network)
- **CCS2 is the universal standard** — every major public DC charger in the Philippines uses CCS2
- **Government incentives are generous**: full import duty exemption on EVs until 2028, excise tax exemption for pure EVs, and the new PHP 60-billion EV Incentive Strategy (EVIS)
- **Target: 7,300 charging stations by 2028** under the Comprehensive Roadmap for the Electric Vehicle Industry (CREVI)
- **Cross-island EV travel is now practical** with EV-friendly RoRo ferries connecting Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao

## The Philippines' EV Charging Landscape in 2026

The Philippines has made remarkable progress since the Electric Vehicle Industry Development Act (EVIDA) was signed into law in 2022. From fewer than 200 charging points in early 2023, the country has grown to 912 publicly accessible charging stations (1,569 individual charging points) by March 2026, with 250+ DOE-accredited EVCS providers.

### What's Driving the Growth?

1. **EVIDA and CREVI.** The Electric Vehicle Industry Development Act established the legal framework, while the Comprehensive Roadmap for the Electric Vehicle Industry (CREVI) set the target of 7,300 charging stations by 2028 and 2.4 million EVs on the road.

2. **The EVIS Fund.** In early 2026, the government launched the PHP 60-billion ($1 billion) Electric Vehicle Incentive Strategy (EVIS), redirecting funds previously allocated to ICE vehicle incentives under the CARS program toward EV manufacturing, charging infrastructure, and consumer subsidies.

3. **The Green Lane Initiative (EO 18).** EV infrastructure projects are classified as "Strategic Investments," mandating fast-tracked permit processing. Developers report getting permits in under 4 months compared to the previous 9 months.

4. **Zero-tariff policy extended through 2028.** Full exemption from customs duties on battery electric vehicles, their components, and charging equipment has significantly lowered EV purchase prices.

5. **Private sector expansion.** ACMobility, ChargeSC, EVroam, and Meralco are aggressively expanding their networks. Tesla entered the market in 2025 with Supercharger stations in key Metro Manila locations.

### Types of Chargers You'll Find

| Charger Type | Power Output | Typical Locations | Charging Speed (60 kWh battery) |
|---|---|---|---|
| AC Level 2 (Slow) | 7–22 kW | Condos, hotels, offices, malls | 3–8 hours (full charge) |
| DC Fast | 50–120 kW | Petrol stations, malls, highway stops | 30–60 min (20–80%) |
| DC Ultra-Fast | 150–350 kW | New highway hubs, flagship stations | 15–25 min (20–80%) |

### Connector Compatibility

**CCS2 is the universal DC fast-charging standard** in the Philippines. Every major network — ACMobility, ChargeSC, EVroam, Meralco, Tesla Supercharger — uses CCS2. You'll also find:

- **Type 2 AC** — standard for slower AC charging at condos, hotels, and office parking
- **CHAdeMO** — available at select older stations, primarily for Nissan Leaf units
- **GB/T** — not used in the Philippines. Chinese-brand EVs sold locally (BYD, MG, GWM) use CCS2 on their Philippine-market models

### Deep Dive: Each Network

#### ACMobility (300+ stations)

ACMobility, a joint venture between Ayala Corporation and Mitsubishi, is the clear market leader. Their network spans Metro Manila extensively, with growing coverage along the North Luzon Expressway (NLEX) and South Luzon Expressway (SLEX) corridors. They partnered with BYD in 2025 to co-locate chargers at BYD dealerships nationwide.

**Key locations:** Greenbelt (Makati), Ayala Malls circuit-wide, Nuvali (Laguna), Alabang Town Center, Glorietta, Market! Market! (BGC), and along NLEX rest stops.

**Pricing:** PHP 25–35/kWh DC, PHP 15–20/kWh AC. No membership fee required.

**App:** ACMobility app available on iOS and Android. Shows real-time availability, starts charging sessions, handles payment.

#### ChargeSC (200+ stations)

ChargeSC (operated by EO Charging under San Miguel Corporation) has built the strongest presence in SM Malls, Robinsons Malls, and SMC-owned petrol stations. If you're shopping at an SM Mall — from SM North EDSA to SM City Cebu — there's a good chance ChargeSC has a station there.

**Key locations:** SM Mall of Asia, SM North EDSA, SM Megamall, SM City Cebu, SM Lanang Premier (Davao), Petron stations along major highways.

**Pricing:** PHP 28–40/kWh DC, PHP 18–25/kWh AC. Tiered pricing based on time of day and charging speed.

**App:** ChargeSC app with station locator, reservation system, and payment integration with GCash and credit cards.

#### EVroam (150+ stations)

EVroam is the fastest-growing network in 2025–2026, focused on highway corridor coverage. They've aggressively deployed along the Tarlac-Pangasinan-La Union Expressway (TPLEX), opening up North Luzon for EV road trips.

**Key locations:** TPLEX rest stops, SCTEX (Subic-Clark-Tarlac Expressway), major provincial petrol stations in Pampanga, Tarlac, Pangasinan, and La Union.

**Pricing:** PHP 30–45/kWh DC, PHP 20–25/kWh AC. PHP 5–10 idle fee applies after 30 minutes.

**App:** EVroam app with trip planning features, charger status, and payment via Maya or credit card.

#### Meralco EV (80+ stations)

As the Philippines' largest power distributor, Meralco brings deep expertise (and cheap power) to its charging network. Stations are concentrated in Metro Manila, with a focus on workplace charging and fleet operations.

**Key locations:** Meralco headquarters (Pasig), selected Shell stations, BGC, Makati office buildings, Quezon City government buildings.

**Pricing:** PHP 25–30/kWh DC, PHP 15–18/kWh AC. Among the most affordable rates.

**App:** Meralco EV app. Integration with Meralco online billing for postpaid subscribers.

#### Tesla Supercharger (8+ stations)

Tesla entered the Philippine market in 2025 with its first Supercharger station in BGC. As of mid-2026, Tesla operates 8+ Supercharger stations, all concentrated in Metro Manila and the CALABARZON region. Tesla Superchargers are open to all CCS2-compatible EVs, not just Tesla owners.

**Key locations:** BGC High Street, McKinley Hill (Taguig), Nuvali (Laguna), Alabang (Muntinlupa), Pampanga (Clark).

**Pricing:** ~PHP 32/kWh. No membership required. Idle fee of PHP 5/min applies once charging is complete.

## Home Charging in the Philippines

Home charging is the backbone of EV ownership in the Philippines. Most EV owners charge overnight at home, waking up to a full battery every morning.

### What You Need

1. **A dedicated 220V outlet.** A standard Philippine 220V wall outlet can deliver 1.5–2.4 kW, adding about 10–15 km of range per hour. This is sufficient for most daily commutes.

2. **A Level 2 wall box (recommended).** A 7 kW wall box (PHP 30,000–80,000 installed) charges a 60 kWh battery from empty to full in about 8 hours — perfect for overnight charging. Popular brands include BYD, Charge Amps, ABB, and local brands distributed through ACMobility.

3. **Proper electrical setup.** Most Filipino homes will need a dedicated circuit breaker for the EV charger. In older subdivisions, you may need to upgrade your Meralco service capacity.

4. **Weatherproofing (critical).** Many Metro Manila homes park on driveways or carports. Install a weatherproof charging enclosure rated IP54 or higher to protect against monsoon rains and typhoons.

### Installation Cost Breakdown

### Condo Charging

Condo EV charging is growing rapidly in Metro Manila. EVIDA requires new building permits to include EV charging provisions. For existing condos:

- Check with your building admin about existing EV charging infrastructure
- Many BGC, Makati, and Ortigas condos now have ACMobility or ChargeSC stations in visitor parking
- For dedicated parking slots, you may need to install a metered outlet through the building association
- The "Right to Charge" movement is gaining traction, with building associations increasingly required to approve reasonable EV charger installations

## Government Incentives

### Active Incentives in 2026

| Incentive | Details | Validity |
|---|---|---|
| **Import duty exemption** | 0% customs duty on CBU EVs (cars, SUVs, trucks, buses, motorcycles, e-bikes, tricycles) and spare parts | Until 2028 |
| **Excise tax exemption** | Pure BEVs fully exempt from automobile excise tax (typically 5–50% of vehicle price) | Under EVIDA |
| **Reduced excise tax (hybrids)** | Hybrids pay 50% of normal excise tax rate | Under EVIDA |
| **EVIS Fund** | PHP 60 billion ($1 billion) for EV manufacturing incentives, charging infrastructure, and consumer subsidies | Announced 2026 |
| **Green Lane permits** | Fast-tracked permitting for EV infrastructure (EO 18) — permits in under 4 months | Active |
| **Government fleet mandate** | 5% of government and public corporation fleets must be EVs | Under EVIDA |

### What Was Cancelled

In April 2026, the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) formally terminated the CARS (Comprehensive Automotive Resurgence Strategy) program, redirecting the multi-billion peso incentives away from Toyota, Mitsubishi, and other ICE manufacturers toward EV development under EVIS. If you were considering a subsidised ICE vehicle under CARS — those subsidies are gone.

## Best EV Road Trip Routes

### Route 1: Manila → Baguio (Classic Mountain Escape)

- **Distance:** 250 km via NLEX → SCTEX → TPLEX → Marcos Highway
- **Drive time:** 4–5 hours
- **EV range required:** 300+ km (allow for mountain climbing consumption)
- **Charging:** ACMobility at Dau (Pampanga), EVroam at TPLEX rest stops (Tarlac, Pangasinan), ChargeSC at Baguio (SM Baguio)
- **Best for:** Weekend mountain getaway, 5–10°C cooler than Manila

The classic Manila-to-Baguio drive is now fully EV-friendly. Charge at EVroam's TPLEX stops in Tarlac and Pangasinan, each with 120 kW DC chargers. Once in Baguio, SM Baguio has ChargeSC DC chargers, and several hotels (The Manor, Azalea Residences) have overnight AC charging.

### Route 2: Manila → Batangas Beaches

- **Distance:** 100–120 km via SLEX → STAR Tollway
- **Drive time:** 1.5–2.5 hours
- **EV range required:** 200+ km (round trip comfortable)
- **Charging:** ACMobility at Nuvali (Laguna), ChargeSC at SM Lipa, multiple AC/DC options in Tagaytay (Sky Ranch, Taal Vista Hotel)
- **Best for:** Beach weekend, family outings, Pico de Loro, Anilao diving

Manila to Batangas is the easiest EV road trip from Metro Manila. Most EVs can do the round trip on a single charge. Charge at Nuvali (ACMobility DC) on the way down, and at SM Lipa or Tagaytay tourist spots while exploring.

### Route 3: Manila → Clark → Subic

- **Distance:** 80–120 km via NLEX → SCTEX or NLEX → Subic-Tipo Expressway
- **Drive time:** 1.5–2 hours
- **Charging:** ChargeSC at Clark (SM Clark, Clark Freeport), ACMobility at Subic (Harbor Point, Subic Bay Freeport)
- **Best for:** Short weekend trip, duty-free shopping, water parks

The Clark-Subic corridor is well-served with charging options. Clark Freeport has multiple stations at hotels and the SM mall. Subic Bay is slightly more sparse but Harbor Point has ACMobility DC charging.

### Route 4: Cebu City Circuit

- **Distance:** 100 km round trip within Metro Cebu
- **Charging:** ChargeSC at SM City Cebu, ACMobility at Ayala Center Cebu, separately at Mactan Island resorts
- **Best for:** Urban exploring, island hopping via ferry

Cebu has strong charging density for a provincial city. SM City Cebu, Ayala Center Cebu, and several hotels in Mactan provide good coverage. The Cebu-Bohol-Siquijor loop is covered in our dedicated [Visayas EV island-hopping guide](/blog/cebu-bohol-siquijor-philippines-ev-island-road-trip).

### Route 5: Davao City Explorer

- **Distance:** 50 km within Davao Metro
- **Charging:** ChargeSC at SM Lanang Premier, ACMobility at Abreeza Mall, and growing hotel charging network (Waterfront Insular Hotel, Dusit Thani)
- **Best for:** Mindanao urban exploration, Mount Apo base, Samal Island

Davao's charging infrastructure is limited but growing. SM Lanang Premier has reliable DC charging. Hotel charging is available at key business hotels. Longer Mindanao road trips (Davao→General Santos, Davao→Cagayan de Oro) still require careful planning — charging outside Davao Metro is sparse.

## Cross-Island EV Travel

One of the Philippines' unique challenges is inter-island travel. Here's how to island-hop in an EV:

### Luzon ↔ Visayas (via ferry)

- **Route:** Matnog (Sorsogon) → Allen (Samar) — 1.5 hour RoRo ferry
- **What you need:** RoRo (Roll-on/Roll-off) ferries accept EVs. Most 2GO and SuperCat ferries now have EV-specific loading procedures.
- **Charging at ports:** ChargeSC and ACMobility both have stations at major port terminals (Batangas Pier, Matnog Port)
- **Preparation:** Ensure your EV has at least 50% charge before boarding. Ferry deck charging is generally not available during crossing.

### Visayas Island Hopping

The most EV-friendly inter-island routes are in Visayas:
- Cebu → Bohol (2 hours by fast ferry)
- Bohol → Siquijor (1.5 hours)
- Siquijor → Dumaguete (30 minutes)
- Dumaguete → Cebu (via ferry)

See our detailed [Cebu-Bohol-Siquijor EV island road trip guide](/blog/cebu-bohol-siquijor-philippines-ev-island-road-trip) for the full itinerary.

### Manila → Mindoro

- **Route:** Batangas Pier → Calapan (Mindoro) — 2 hours RoRo
- **Charging:** ACMobility at Batangas Pier, limited options in Puerto Galera (resort charging mainly)

## Challenges & Tips

### Typhoon Season (June–November)

- **What happens:** Typhoons can knock out power for days. If you rely on home charging, keep your battery above 50% during storm warnings.
- **At public stations:** Covered or indoor parking stations (most SM Malls, Ayala Malls) continue operating. Exposed stations may be temporarily shut down for safety.
- **Generator backup:** Some ACMobility and ChargeSC stations have generator backup — check each station's status in the app before heading out.

### Charger Reliability

As with many emerging EV markets, charger downtime is a real issue. Some tips:

- Always check recent user reviews on PlugShare before driving to an unfamiliar station
- Have a backup charging plan for any destination beyond 50 km from home
- The four major apps all show real-time availability — use them
- Keep a Type 2 AC charging cable in your car for emergency top-ups at hotels or condos

### The "Right to Charge"

Filipino condo and subdivision dwellers may face resistance when requesting EV charger installation. Key developments in 2026:

- **EVIDA mandates EV-ready provisions** in new building permits
- The DOE has released guidelines requiring building associations to reasonably accommodate EV charger requests
- If your building admin refuses, escalate through the DOE's EV industry portal at EVIndustry.ph
- Many BGC and Makati condos now have established EV charging policies

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I drive an EV from Manila to Baguio?**
A: Yes, comfortably. The route via NLEX → SCTEX → TPLEX is well-served with EVroam and ACMobility DC chargers. Charge at TPLEX rest stops in Tarlac or Pangasinan, and top up at SM Baguio on arrival.

**Q: Can I take an EV on a ferry in the Philippines?**
A: Yes. RoRo ferries accept EVs on the vehicle deck. 2GO, SuperCat, and local barge operators now have EV-specific loading procedures. Key routes are Batangas→Mindoro, Matnog→Samar, and all Visayas island routes.

**Q: What is the cheapest way to charge?**
A: Home charging overnight at Meralco residential rates (PHP 9–12/kWh) is by far the cheapest. For public charging, ACMobility's AC rates (PHP 15–20/kWh) are the most affordable public option.

**Q: Is CCS2 the standard connector?**
A: Yes. Every major DC fast charging network in the Philippines uses CCS2. Tesla sold their Philippine vehicles with CCS2-compatible ports. CHAdeMO exists only at older stations.

**Q: Are EV chargers free at shopping malls?**
A: Some SM Malls and Ayala Malls offered free charging in 2023–2024 as a promotion, but by 2026 nearly all stations charge per kWh. Expect to pay PHP 25–45/kWh for DC and PHP 15–25/kWh for AC at malls.

**Q: What is the government doing about EV incentives?**
A: The government has (1) eliminated import duties on EVs until 2028, (2) exempted pure EVs from excise tax, (3) launched the PHP 60 billion EVIS fund, (4) mandated 5% EV fleet for government agencies, and (5) fast-tracked EV infrastructure permits under EO 18.

**Q: How many EVs are in the Philippines?**
A: Over 60,000 EVs registered as of early 2026, with 912 publicly accessible charging stations (1,569 charging points). The target is 7,300 stations by 2028 and 2.4 million EVs on the road under CREVI.

**Q: What should I do before a long road trip?**
A: (1) Install all four charging apps (ACMobility, ChargeSC, EVroam, Meralco EV), (2) Download offline maps for your route, (3) Check PlugShare for recent user reports at your planned charging stops, (4) Have at least one backup charging location per leg, (5) Carry a Type 2 AC cable for emergency charging at hotels.
