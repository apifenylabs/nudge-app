# Indonesia EV Charging Guide 2026 — Jakarta to Bali & Beyond

Indonesia's EV transition is gathering real momentum. With the government targeting 15 million electric two-wheelers and 2 million electric cars by 2030, the charging infrastructure is expanding rapidly — especially along the Java toll road corridor connecting Jakarta, Bandung, Semarang, Surabaya, and Bali.

This is the most comprehensive guide to EV charging in Indonesia in 2026.

## The EV Charging Landscape in Indonesia (2026)

Indonesia's charging network (known as **SPKLU** — Stasiun Pengisian Kendaraan Listrik Umum) has grown substantially:

- **2,100+** public charging stations
- **5,800+** charging points
- **PLN** (state electricity company) operates the largest network
- **CCS2** is the official standard, with some CHAdeMO legacy units
- Key private players: **PLN Icon+**, **Charge+**, **V-GREEN**

### Major Charging Networks

| Network | Stations | Connectors | Payment |
|---------|----------|------------|---------|
| **PLN Icon+** | 900+ | CCS2, Type 2 AC | App / QR |
| **Charge+** | 450+ | CCS2 | App / QR |
| **V-GREEN** (Hyundai) | 300+ | CCS2, CHAdeMO | App / Card |
| **GESITS** | 200+ | CCS2 | App / QR |
| **E-Mobility** (Toyota) | 150+ | CCS2 | App |

### The PLN App (Mandatory)
Every EV driver in Indonesia needs the **PLN Mobile** app. It's the primary way to:
- Find PLN-operated stations
- Check real-time availability (improving but not always accurate)
- Pay via QR/OVO/GoPay
- Activate chargers (some need manual activation via the app)

## Charging Along the Java Toll Road

The **Trans-Java Toll Road** (Jakarta → Surabaya, ~760 km) is now fully EV-drivable. Here's the corridor breakdown:

### Jakarta → Bandung (~150 km)
- **Puncak corridor**: Multiple PLN stations every 30 km
- **Cipularang Toll**: New 120 kW chargers at rest areas
- **Bandung city**: 40+ stations, concentrated in Dago, Cihampelas, and the city center

### Bandung → Semarang (~350 km)
- **Brebes Exit**: PLN 60 kW station at rest area KM 260
- **Pemalang**: V-GREEN 120 kW — the fastest on this stretch
- **Semarang**: 25+ stations including malls and hotels

### Semarang → Surabaya (~320 km)
- **Ngawi**: PLN 60 kW (new, opened March 2026)
- **Madiun**: Charge+ 100 kW
- **Surabaya**: 50+ stations, Tunjungan Plaza has DC fast charging

### Bali (Tourist Hub)
Bali has **80+ charging stations**, mostly concentrated in:
- **South Bali**: Kuta, Seminyak, Canggu, Sanur — 50+ stations
- **Ubud**: 15+ stations at resorts and villas
- **Nusa Dua**: Premium hotel zone with valet charging
- **Denpasar**: 20+ stations, including the airport

## Best EV Charging Apps for Indonesia

### 1. PLN Mobile
Essential — but mixed reviews. The app covers 900+ stations but real-time status isn't always updated.

**Tip**: Always call the station contact (listed in app) before relying on a specific charger.

### 2. Charge+
More reliable real-time data. Good coverage in Jakarta, Bandung, and Surabaya.

### 3. PlugShare Indonesia
The community check-ins are invaluable here. Users report which stations are working, ICE'd, or slow.

## Charging Costs in Indonesia

Electricity in Indonesia is relatively affordable:

- **PLN DC fast charging**: Rp 2,467/kWh (~$0.15 USD)
- **PLN AC charging**: Rp 1,644/kWh (~$0.10 USD)
- **Private chargers (Charge+/V-GREEN)**: Rp 3,500-5,000/kWh (~$0.22-0.31 USD)
- **Hotel charging**: Often free for guests

**Real-world cost**: A full charge for a BYD Dolphin (45 kWh) costs roughly **Rp 111,000 ($7 USD)** at a PLN DC charger — about 1/4 the cost of petrol for equivalent range.

## Top EV Road Trip Routes in Indonesia

### 1. Jakarta → Bandung → Semarang → Surabaya (Full Java Crossing)
**Distance**: ~760 km  
**Charging stops**: 4-5 stops (PLN Icon+ stations every 60-80 km)
**Best for**: Ambitious cross-island EV road trip. Entirely toll road, well-covered.
**Difficulty**: Easy — just plan stops ahead for longer stretches.

### 2. Denpasar → Ubud → Bedugul → Singaraja (Bali Loop)
**Distance**: ~200 km  
**Charging stops**: Denpasar (start full), Ubud AC charger at hotel, optional Bedugul
**Best for**: A weekend exploration of Bali's highlands and north coast.
**Tip**: The climb to Bedugul uses more battery, but regen descent on the north side recovers some.

### 3. Surabaya → Malang → Batu
**Distance**: ~90 km  
**Charging stops**: Malang has several PLN stations; Batu has chargers near tourist attractions
**Best for**: Mountain scenery (Gunung Bromo approach), apple farms, cool climate
**Tip**: Fill up in Surabaya — Malang and Batu have fewer fast chargers.

## Practical Tips

### Language & Support
- Most apps have English options
- Call the listed phone number on each station — staff often speak basic English
- WhatsApp is widely used for station support

### Payment
- **GoPay**, **OVO**, **QRIS** — all widely accepted
- Credit cards accepted at major stations, but QR/GoPay is more reliable
- **Avoid cash** — many newer stations are cashless

### What to Watch For
- **Load shedding**: Some areas experience scheduled power outages — check before relying on a station
- **Flood season** (Nov-Mar): Lower ground stations in Jakarta and coastal areas may be inaccessible
- **Converter needed**: Some older stations have only CHAdeMO — carry a CCS2-to-CHAdeMO converter for backup

### Hotel Charging
Increasingly common at mid-range and luxury hotels. **Best bets**:
- **Aryaduta** chain — complimentary EV charging
- **Accor** hotels (Novotel, Pullman, MGallery) — growing network
- **JW Marriott** and **Ritz-Carlton** properties in Jakarta and Bali
- Many boutique villas in **Seminyak** and **Canggu** now advertise EV charging

## The Future (2026-2027)

- **PLN targeting** 5,000 stations by 2027
- **Battery swapping stations** launching for two-wheelers (already 200+ in Jakarta)
- **IBC** (Indonesia Battery Corporation) building integrated charging hubs
- **New capital (IKN Nusantara)** designed as EV-first city with embedded charging
- **EV subsidy** extended through 2027 for locally-assembled EVs
