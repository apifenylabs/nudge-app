# Thailand EV Charging Guide 2026: Complete Guide for EV Owners &amp; Visitors

Thailand registered 45,668 electric vehicles in January 2026 alone — a 210% increase over the same month in 2025. With over 3,720 charging stations and 11,622 individual chargers nationwide, the Kingdom is rapidly becoming Southeast Asia's most EV-friendly road trip destination. But navigating the charger landscape can still feel overwhelming for newcomers.

This comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know about EV charging in Thailand in 2026: which network to choose, what it costs, how to charge at home or in a condo, how to plan road trips to major destinations, and what's changed under the new EV3.5 policy framework.

## Key Takeaways

- **Thailand now has 3,720+ charging stations with 11,622 chargers** — 6,524 DC fast chargers and 5,098 AC slow chargers — exceeding the National EV Policy's 2025 targets
- **Public DC fast charging costs 5.30–10 THB/kWh**, translating to just 91–160 THB per 100 km — less than half the cost of petrol (350 THB/100 km)
- **Home charging is the cheapest option at ~4.50 THB/kWh** (TOU off-peak), costing only 72 THB per 100 km
- **PTT EV Station PluZ leads with ~30% market share** and 624+ stations, followed by EA Anywhere (~16%) and PEA VOLTA (~13%)
- **EV3.5 incentives replaced EV3.0 in 2024 and run through 2027**, with subsidies now at 50,000–100,000 THB for locally assembled EVs
- **CCS2 is the universal standard** — all major networks support it, making any EV compatible with any public fast charger
- **Plan 2–3 charging stops for intercity routes** — most modern EVs with 400+ km range can reach any major Thai city with one or two top-ups

## Thailand's EV Charging Landscape in 2026

The growth of Thailand's EV charging infrastructure has been nothing short of remarkable. From just 2,658 locations with 9,694 chargers at the end of 2023, the network has expanded to 3,720 stations and 11,622 chargers as of early 2026 — representing roughly 20% year-over-year growth in station count and an even faster pace in charger density.

### What's Driving the Growth?

Several factors have converged to accelerate charger deployment:

1. **Government policy targets.** Thailand's 30@30 plan aims for ZEVs to constitute 30% of annual vehicle production by 2030, with 12,000 DC fast chargers deployed nationwide. The target of having charging stations within 200 km of each other on all major highways is expected to be met by late 2026.

2. **Private investment from oil and energy companies.** PTT (Thailand's state-owned oil and gas company) is aggressively converting petrol station real estate into charging hubs, targeting 7,000 charge points by 2030. Energy Absolute (EA) and Bangchak are making similar moves.

3. **Chinese automaker expansion.** BYD, GWM, SAIC-MG, GAC Aion, Neta, and Changan have collectively invested over 137 billion THB (~USD $4 billion) in Thailand's EV supply chain, and many include charging infrastructure commitments as part of their go-to-market strategy.

4. **Ride-hailing and fleet electrification.** SHARGE's joint venture with Grab targets 40 high-throughput charging stations by 2027, each with 12 chargers designed for commercial EV fleet turnover.

### Types of Chargers You'll Find

| Charger Type | Power Output | Typical Locations | Charging Speed (60 kWh battery) |
|---|---|---|---|
| AC Level 2 (Slow) | 7–22 kW | Homes, condos, hotels, offices | 3–8 hours (full charge) |
| DC Fast | 50–150 kW | Highway petrol stations, shopping malls | 25–60 min (20–80%) |
| DC Ultra-Fast | 150–360 kW | New highway hubs, flagship stations | 10–20 min (20–80%) |

### Connector Compatibility

The good news: **CCS2 (Combined Charging System Type 2) is the universal standard in Thailand.** Every major network supports it, and every modern EV sold in Thailand uses it. You'll also find:

- **CHAdeMO** — still available at EA Anywhere, PEA VOLTA, and EV Station PluZ stations, mainly for older Japanese EVs (Nissan Leaf, Mitsubishi Outlander PHEV)
- **Type 2 AC** — used for slower AC charging at most stations

If you drive a Tesla, your car comes with a CCS2-compatible port (since late 2023 models), so you can use any public charger in Thailand without an adapter.

### Deep Dive: Each Network

#### 1. PTT EV Station PluZ (Market Leader)

PTT EV Station PluZ is Thailand's largest EV charging network by station count, leveraging PTT's existing petrol station real estate. You'll find them at familiar, well-lit locations along every major highway and in urban areas. Most highway stations offer DC fast charging at up to 150–200 kW, while urban locations offer a mix of AC and DC.

**Pricing:** Time-of-use (TOU), typically 6.00–7.70 THB/kWh for DC fast charging, plus a flat 20 THB reservation fee per session. AC charging runs 4–7 THB/kWh.

**Coverage:** Excellent on highways — PTT petrol stations are everywhere. In cities, expect chargers at PTT/OR-branded stations and select partner locations.

**App:** "EV Station PluZ" — available on iOS and Android. Shows real-time availability, charger types, and pricing.

**Verdict:** The safe choice. You'll find one when you need it, especially on long-distance routes. The 20 THB booking fee is a minor annoyance.

#### 2. EA Anywhere (Best for Highway Trips)

EA Anywhere, operated by Energy Absolute (EA), is the second-largest network with roughly 491 stations. They've focused on building dedicated charging hubs along major highway corridors — Bangkok to Pattaya, Hua Hin, and Khao Yai — with high-power DC chargers.

**Pricing:** A flat 7.29 THB/kWh for DC. **No peak/off-peak distinction** — you pay the same at 2 AM or 2 PM. This predictability is valuable for trip planning.

**Coverage:** Strong on the Bangkok-Pattaya-Hua Hin corridor and in urban shopping malls across Bangkok, Phuket, and Chiang Mai. Less present in rural areas compared to PTT.

**App:** "EA Anywhere" — clean interface, real-time slot availability, and in-app payment.

**Verdict:** If you take highway road trips — Bangkok to Pattaya, Hua Hin, or the northeast — choose EA Anywhere. The flat rate means no surprises.

#### 3. PEA VOLTA (Cheapest Off-Peak)

Operated by the Provincial Electricity Authority (PEA), PEA VOLTA offers Thailand's cheapest DC fast charging — but only if you charge off-peak. Their 360 kW ultra-fast chargers (at select locations) are the fastest in Thailand.

**Pricing:** Time-of-use (TOU) rates effective from February 2025:

| Charger Power | Peak (THB/kWh) | Off-Peak (THB/kWh) |
|---|---|---|
| < 50 kW | 6.90 | 5.30 |
| 50–99 kW | 8.20 | 6.30 |
| 100–299 kW | 8.50 | 6.50 |
| 300 kW+ | 9.70 | 7.20 |

**Off-peak hours** are generally 10:00 PM – 9:00 AM (confirm times in the app).

**Coverage:** Over 118 stations with a focus on government buildings, provincial offices, and urban centres across all regions. Partnership with Bangchak puts chargers at Bangchak petrol stations in Bangkok.

**App:** "PEA VOLTA" — available on iOS and Android. Shows real-time charger status and integrates with the PEA network map.

**Verdict:** Best for budget-conscious drivers who can charge late at night or early morning. The 360 kW chargers are future-proof for next-gen EVs.

#### 4. Tesla Supercharger

Tesla operates 29 Supercharger stations across Thailand as of February 2026. Newer V3 and V4 Superchargers deliver up to 250 kW. Since 2024, all Superchargers in Thailand support both Tesla (NACS) and CCS2 — meaning **non-Tesla EVs can also use them** (though you'll need Tesla's app to activate).

**Coverage:** Concentrated along major routes — Bangkok, Pattaya, Hua Hin, Phuket, Chiang Mai, Korat (Nakhon Ratchasima), and key highway rest stops.

**Pricing:** Not publicly published; charged per kWh through the Tesla app. Typically competitive with other DC networks.

**Verdict:** Best-in-class experience for Tesla owners. Great backup option for CCS2 EVs. Only 29 locations limits its utility as a primary network.

#### 5. SHARGE (New Entrant — Fleet Focus)

SHARGE is the newest major entrant, launched via a 400 million THB joint venture with Grab. Their stations are designed for high-turnover commercial use, with 12 chargers per location.

**Pricing:** 7.50–9.00 THB/kWh, with special rates for Grab drivers from ~6 THB/kWh.

**Coverage:** Currently limited to 20+ locations in Bangkok and key urban areas, expanding to a target of 40 stations by 2027.

**Verdict:** Worth knowing about if you're a frequent Grab user or drive commercially. Limited relevance for casual tourists.

#### 6. EVOLT

EVOLT operates 100+ stations focused on shopping malls, hotels, and office buildings. They support both AC and DC charging with a membership system.

**Pricing:** 8–10 THB/kWh — generally the most expensive option.

**Coverage:** Good in malls (Central, Siam Paragon, Emporium) and business districts.

**Verdict:** Convenient if you're already parking at a mall, but expensive as a primary charging strategy.

#### 7. Other Notable Networks

- **Elex by EGAT** (~100 stations, starting at ~7.5 THB/kWh) — state-backed reliability
- **GINKA Charge Point** (expanding rapidly across hotels and resorts, generally ≤6.5 THB/kWh)
- **On-ion EV Charger** (~50 stations, starting ~7.25 THB/kWh) — new-generation UX-focused app
- **MG SUPER CHARGE** (~120 stations, open to all brands) — MG's manufacturer-backed network
- **MEA EV Charging** (~30 stations in Bangkok area, starting ~7.5 THB/kWh) — Metropolitan Electricity Authority

Bangkok has the densest charging network in Thailand. You'll find chargers at:

- **Shopping malls:** Siam Paragon, CentralWorld, ICONSIAM, EmQuartier, Central Embassy — all have DC fast chargers, mostly EA Anywhere, EVOLT, or Tesla Supercharger
- **Petrol stations:** PTT and Bangchak stations across the city have DC chargers
- **Condos and offices:** Many newer residential buildings (especially in Sukhumvit, Thonglor, Ekkamai) have shared or dedicated EV charging bays
- **Hotels:** Most 4- and 5-star hotels in central Bangkok offer EV charging for guests — check when booking

**Recommendation:** You never need to worry about range in Bangkok. Use PEA VOLTA off-peak for the cheapest rates, or EA Anywhere for convenience.

## Home and Condo Charging

Home charging remains the most cost-effective and convenient way to own an EV in Thailand. Here's what you need to know.

### Single-Home Installation

**Cost:** A home wallbox installation (including the charger unit and certified electrician) ranges from **20,000–50,000 THB**, depending on the brand and installation complexity.

**Types of chargers:**
- **Portable charger (Granny charger):** Plugs into a standard Thai wall socket (220V, 10–16A). Delivers ~2–3 kW. Cheapest option but very slow (20+ hours for a full charge).
- **Level 2 wallbox (7–22 kW):** Dedicated installation. Charges a 60 kWh battery in 3–8 hours. Recommended for all EV owners.
- **Three-phase wallbox (22 kW):** Requires three-phase power. Charges in ~3 hours. Home 15/45 amp metering setup can often support this.

**Certification requirements:**
- All EV chargers sold in Thailand must have **MEA** (Metropolitan Electricity Authority — for Bangkok, Nonthaburi, Samut Prakan) or **PEA** (Provincial Electricity Authority — for the rest of Thailand) certification
- Installation must be done by a licensed electrician
- From 2026 onwards, DIY home charger installations are being phased out — only certified installers will be allowed to connect new home chargers to the grid

**Electricity costs:**
- Standard residential rate (including Ft): ~4.50 THB/kWh at typical consumption tiers
- Time-of-Use (TOU) residential rate: ~4.50 THB/kWh off-peak, slightly higher peak
- The PEA / MEA TOU tariff for homes is your best bet if you can charge overnight

### Condo and Apartment Charging

Charging in a condo is more complex but increasingly feasible in 2026:

**If the building already has chargers:**
- Many newer condos (built 2023 onwards) include EV charging bays as standard amenities
- Usage is typically through a RFID card or QR code system, billed separately or added to common area fees
- Some condos charge a flat monthly fee; others per-kWh

**If you need to install a new charger:**
1. **Obtain building approval** — You need the condo juristic person (JP) and/or co-owner committee to approve the installation
2. **Hire a certified installer** who can work with the building's electrical system
3. **Install a submeter** so your charging is billed separately from common area electricity
4. **Cost** — Typically 30,000–80,000 THB including the charger, installation, and submeter, depending on distance from the main electrical room

**Legal note:** Thailand has no national "right to charge" law yet, but the Condominium Act allows co-owners to make modifications pending JP approval. In practice, most Bangkok condos are cooperative, especially if you pay for the installation yourself.

### MEA vs PEA: What's the Difference?

| | MEA (Metropolitan Electricity Authority) | PEA (Provincial Electricity Authority) |
|---|---|---|
| **Area** | Bangkok, Nonthaburi, Samut Prakan | Everywhere else in Thailand |
| **Charger certification** | MEA-approved models only | PEA-approved models (may differ) |
| **Application** | Via MEA office or authorized installer | Via PEA provincial office or authorized installer |
| **TOU rates** | Available for homes | Available for homes |

**If you move between MEA and PEA areas,** your wallbox will work on either grid — you just need the appropriate authority to inspect and approve the connection.

## Cross-Border EV Charging

Driving an EV into or out of Thailand to neighbouring countries is still in its early stages, but progress is being made.

### Cambodia

Cross-border EV travel between Thailand and Cambodia faces the most significant challenges:

- **Border crossings:** Aranyaprathet–Poipet (main), Hat Lek–Koh Kong (southern)
- **Cambodia's charging infrastructure:** Very limited outside Phnom Penh and Siem Reap. Chargers are sparse along the Thai-Cambodia border corridor
- **Insurance:** Thai motor insurance does not cover driving in Cambodia. You'll need a separate Cambodian insurance policy
- **Vehicle import:** Temporary import permits are required at the border; paperwork can be complex for foreign-plated EVs
- **Practical advice:** Not yet recommended for casual EV trips. The few travellers who do it report relying on standard wall outlets at hotels

### Laos

Laos has made more progress on EV charging:

- **Border crossings:** Nong Khai–Vientiane (main), Chiang Khong–Houayxay (northern)
- **Vientiane has a handful of DC fast chargers** — enough to get you to a hotel and back
- **Luang Prabang** — emerging coverage at a few hotels
- **The ASEAN "GO" cross-border EV charging initiative** (launched 2025) now covers the Thailand-Laos corridor, allowing users of the GO app to find and use chargers in both countries
- **Practical advice:** Feasible if your destination is Vientiane or Luang Prabang, but charge fully on the Thai side before crossing

### Myanmar

Cross-border EV travel to Myanmar is **not recommended in 2026** due to:

- Ongoing security concerns and travel advisories from most foreign governments
- Extremely limited charging infrastructure inside Myanmar
- Complex border procedures
- Thailand cut electricity, internet, and fuel supplies to several border areas in Myanmar in early 2026

**Verdict:** Wait until Myanmar's political situation stabilises and basic charging infrastructure exists.

### Future Developments

The ASEAN Economic Community is pushing for harmonised cross-border EV charging standards. Key developments:

- **ASEAN CCS2 standardisation** — All ASEAN member states have agreed to adopt CCS2 as the common DC fast charging connector
- **The "GO" network** — A cross-border app covering Thailand, Laos, and Cambodia (with Vietnam and Malaysia planned)
- **AHN (ASEAN Harmonised Network)** — A long-term project to create a unified payment and roaming system across all ASEAN countries

## EV Policy in Thailand: EV3.0 and EV3.5 Incentives in 2026

Thailand's electric vehicle incentive framework has transitioned from EV3.0 (2022–2025) to EV3.5 (2024–2027), with significant changes taking effect in 2026.

### Key Timeline

| Period | Scheme | Status in 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| 2022–2025 | EV3.0 | Ended December 2025 |
| 2024–2027 | EV3.5 | **Active** |
| 2025–2030 | 30@30 target | In progress |

### What EV3.5 Means for Buyers in 2026

Under EV3.5, the incentive structure has been scaled back from EV3.0:

**Subsidies (for locally assembled vehicles only):**
- Electric passenger cars (battery ≥ 50 kWh): **50,000 THB** per vehicle (reduced from 100,000–150,000 THB under EV3.0)
- Electric pickup trucks: **50,000 THB** per vehicle
- Electric motorcycles: Subsidies available but reduced

**Tax incentives:**
- Import duty reduction: Up to 40% reduction for CBU (completely built-up) imports during 2024–2025 (expired)
- Excise tax: Reduced from 8% to 2% for EVs priced under 7 million THB
- **Since January 2026:** Imported EVs not covered by EV3.5 commitments face a 10% excise tax (up from 2%)

**Key change for 2026:** Chinese automakers have raised EV prices for 2026 as EV3.5 subsidies are reduced and import tax advantages phase out. Expect less aggressive pricing compared to 2024–2025.

### Production Offset Requirements

Manufacturers in the EV3.5 scheme must offset imported EVs with domestic production at a ratio of:
- **1:2 by 2026** — For every vehicle imported, two must be produced locally
- **1:3 by 2027** — For every vehicle imported, three must be produced locally

A July 2025 adjustment allows **1 vehicle produced for export to count as 1.5 vehicles** toward the production offset, making it easier for manufacturers to meet commitments while using Thailand as an export base.

### State of the EV Market

- **238,000 EVs** have been registered under EV3.0 and EV3.5 programmes as of October 2025
- **~140 billion THB** in cumulative investments from 32 EV3.0 and 11 EV3.5 manufacturers
- **BEV sales** reached ~90,000 units in the first ten months of 2025, accounting for 18% of total vehicle sales
- **EV registrations surged 59%** in January–September 2025 vs the same period in 2024

## Practical Tips for Tourists and EV Road Trippers

### Before You Go

1. **Download and register for at least three apps:** EA Anywhere, PEA VOLTA, and EV Station PluZ. Test payment with a small top-up before your trip.

2. **Know your EV's real-world range.** Most EVs achieve 15–18 kWh/100 km on highways (vs. 12–15 in city driving). Account for air conditioning usage, which adds ~10% consumption in Thailand's heat.

3. **Carry both CCS2 and Type 2 cables.** While most DC chargers have tethered cables, AC chargers often require you to bring your own Type 2 cable.

4. **Plan charging stops before 80% battery.** Charging slows significantly above 80% SoC (state of charge). It's faster to do two 20–80% charges than one 20–100%.

5. **Check hotel EV charging availability.** When booking via [booking.com](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Thailand) or Agoda, filter for hotels with EV charging. Many mid-range and upscale hotels in tourist areas now offer complimentary charging.

### On the Road

6. **Charge at PTT stations for reliability.** PTT has the widest highway network and well-lit, staffed locations open 24/7.

7. **Never let your battery drop below 15%** when travelling between cities. If you see a fast charger, top up — don't wait for the next one if the gap is uncertain.

8. **Avoid charging between 5:00 PM and 9:00 PM** on PEA VOLTA or PTT if you want off-peak rates. EA Anywhere's flat rate means no penalty for peak-hour charging.

9. **Watch for charger hogging.** During Thai public holidays (Songkran in April, New Year, Chinese New Year), highway chargers can have queues. Many chargers are free for the first 90 minutes, then charge idle fees.

10. **Use Plugshare for crowdsourced info.** Even though it's not Thai-built, Plugshare has good coverage and user reviews showing which chargers are working or broken.

### Bangkok–Chiang Mai Road Trip (695 km)

This classic route up Highway 1 (Phahonyothin Road) is now thoroughly practical for EVs. Our dedicated [Bangkok to Chiang Mai guide](/blog/bangkok-chiang-mai-ev-road-trip) covers it in detail, but here's the quick version:

**Recommended charging strategy:**
- **Start:** Full charge in Bangkok (home or overnight)
- **Stop 1:** Nakhon Sawan / Tak Fa — ~230 km (~30% remaining, charge to 80%)
- **Stop 2:** Lampang / Lamphun — ~200 km further (charge to 80%)
- **Arrive:** Chiang Mai — ~70 km from Lampang

**Total charging cost:** ~500–900 THB vs. 1,800–2,500 THB for petrol.

### Phuket Island Driving

Phuket is about 50 km north-to-south. A full charge in a typical EV covers the entire island multiple times. Key tips:

- Charge at the airport upon arrival if you have a depleted battery
- Most west-coast resorts (Kamala, Surin, Bang Tao) have installed EV chargers for guests
- Patong's public parking at Jungceylon has DC fast charging
- The Phuket–Phang Nga Bridge area also has a few stations if you're heading to Khao Lak

### Pattaya Day Trip from Bangkok

The easiest EV road trip in Thailand:

- **Distance:** ~150 km (1.5 hours on Highway 7)
- **Range needed:** ~300 km round trip (with a buffer)
- **No charging needed** for most EVs — but if you want to top up, EA Anywhere and PTT have hubs near the Pattaya exit
- **Charging in Pattaya:** Terminal 21, Central Festival, and several hotels

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. How many EV charging stations are there in Thailand in 2026?

As of early 2026, Thailand has approximately **3,720 charging stations with 11,622 individual chargers** — roughly 6,524 DC fast chargers and 5,098 AC slow chargers. This exceeds the National EV Policy's original 2025 targets. The number continues to grow at roughly 20% year-over-year.

### 2. What is the cheapest way to charge an EV in Thailand?

**Home charging is cheapest** at ~4.50 THB/kWh (residential TOU off-peak). For public DC charging, **PEA VOLTA off-peak at 5.30 THB/kWh** is the best deal. A full charge (50 kWh) costs ~265 THB at PEA VOLTA off-peak vs. ~375 THB at EA Anywhere's flat rate.

### 3. Can I use my international driving license to drive an EV in Thailand?

Yes, but with requirements:
- **Tourists:** A valid International Driving Permit (IDP) from your home country is required. Your home-country driver's license alone is **not** sufficient.
- **Expats:** If you hold a Thai residence visa, you must exchange your foreign license for a Thai driver's license (valid for 1–5 years).
- **EV-specific:** No special license is needed to drive an EV — the same categories apply. However, your car registration must clearly show it's an EV.

### 4. Do I need a Thai phone number to use EV charging apps?

**Most apps prefer a Thai phone number for registration,** but some options exist:
- **EA Anywhere:** Accepts international numbers in some regions
- **PEA VOLTA:** Thai number strongly recommended
- **PTT EV Station PluZ:** Thai number required
- **Workaround:** Buy a Thai SIM card at the airport (AIS, TrueMove, or dtac offer tourist SIMs with data + a Thai number for ~300–1,000 THB)

### 5. Are EV charging stations free anywhere in Thailand?

**Some hotels and shopping malls offer free charging** (usually AC slow charging) as an amenity:
- Many 4–5 star hotels in Phuket, Khao Yai, and Chiang Mai offer complimentary charging to guests
- Central Group shopping malls (CentralWorld, CentralFestival, etc.) often provide 1–4 hours of free AC charging with validated parking
- Some condos include EV charging in common area fees — check with your building management
- **Important:** Free charging is almost always slow AC (7–22 kW). Don't rely on it as your primary charging source; treat it as a bonus.

### 6. Is it safe to drive an EV through Thailand's rainy season (June–October)?

Yes, EVs are perfectly safe in Thailand's monsoon conditions. Key considerations:
- EV batteries are sealed and waterproof (IP67 or higher); wading through floodwater up to 30 cm is generally safe, but avoid deep flooding
- **Regen braking** works better than engine braking in wet conditions, giving you more control
- Range drops by 10–15% in heavy rain due to increased rolling resistance and AC/defogger usage
- Charging during heavy rain or lightning storms is safe — all chargers have ground-fault protection and weather-rated enclosures
- **Practical tip:** If flood warnings are issued, charge to 100% when you can — you never know when power might go out

### 7. Can I rent an EV in Thailand as a tourist?

Yes, several companies now offer EV rentals:
- **EV Rental Thailand** — Specialises in EVs (BYD Atto 3, Neta V, MG4) with delivery to Bangkok hotels or airports
- **Hertz Thailand** — Has a growing EV fleet at major airports (BKK, DMK, HKT, CNX)
- **Local rental agencies** in Phuket and Chiang Mai increasingly offer EVs
- **Expect to pay** 1,200–2,500 THB/day for a compact EV, comparable to a mid-size petrol car
- **Check:** Rental agreements typically include a charging cable but may require a deposit for it. Ask which networks have partner discounts.

### 8. What happens if a public charger is broken?

Charger reliability varies by network:
- **PTT and EA Anywhere** have the best uptime records (~95%+ availability)
- **Smaller networks** can have chargers offline for days or weeks
- **What to do:** Always have a backup charger within 20 km. Use Plugshare to check recent user reports before you leave
- **Reporting:** Most apps have a "Report Issue" button — use it to help other drivers
