# Vietnam EV Charging Guide 2026: Road Tripping from Hanoi to Ho Chi Minh City with an Electric Car

Driving the length of Vietnam — 1,600 km from Hanoi's ancient streets to Ho Chi Minh City's electric energy, via the Hai Van Pass, the beaches of Da Nang, and the rice paddies of the Mekong Delta — is one of Southeast Asia's greatest road trips. And in 2026, it's finally practical to do it in an electric car.

Vietnam's EV infrastructure has exploded. The V-Green network (VinFast) has over 1,500 DC fast-charging points along the QL1A highway corridor, while CCS2 networks like EVOne and EBOOST have filled the gaps for non-VinFast EVs. Hotel destination charging is becoming standard at resorts from Hoi An to Nha Trang. This guide covers everything you need: the route, the charging stops, the costs, and the family-friendly pit stops that make the journey as memorable as the destination.

## Key Takeaways

- The 1,600 km Hanoi → HCMC journey along QL1A requires **4–6 charging stops** in a mid-range EV (400+ km range), taking 3–4 driving days
- Total charging cost is approximately **VND 1,000,000–1,500,000 (USD $41–62)**, roughly half the cost of petrol
- **V-Green (VinFast)** has the best coverage along QL1A — stations every 80–120 km with 60 kW DC chargers
- **EVOne and EBOOST** provide CCS2 alternatives with 120–240 kW ultra-fast charging at key hubs
- The **Hai Van Pass** (Da Nang–Hue) is the most scenic leg — budget 30% extra consumption for the mountain climb
- Overnight charging at **Vinpearl and partner hotels** is free for guests, saving VND 200,000–400,000 per night
- **Can Tho and the Mekong Delta** are accessible for day trips from HCMC, with growing V-Green coverage

## Charging Networks on the Route

### 1. V-Green / VinFast — The Dominant Network

V-Green is the backbone of EV travel in Vietnam. With 900+ stations nationwide and 1,500+ DC fast-charging plugs, it's the only network with truly national coverage. Stations are concentrated on the QL1A highway corridor, spaced roughly 80–120 km apart.

**Charger types on QL1A:**
- **V-Green DC Fast (60 kW)** — Most common, found at petrol stations and rest stops. VinFast proprietary connector (CCS2 adapter available).
- **V-Green DC Ultra-Fast (180 kW)** — Newer stations at major hubs: Vinh, Da Nang, Quy Nhon, Nha Trang, Long Thanh (HCMC).
- **V-Green AC Destination (7–22 kW)** — Found at Vinpearl hotels and partner resorts. Free for hotel guests.

**Cost:** VND 3,500–5,000/kWh (60 kW DC ~3,800/kWh; 180 kW ultra-fast ~5,000/kWh)

**App:** V-Green (iOS/Android) — shows real-time availability, supports QR payment via Momo, VNPay, and ZaloPay.

**Pro tip:** Register your payment method (Momo or bank card) in the V-Green app *before* your trip. Rural stations often have poor internet — download the station list offline.

### 2. EVOne — Best for Non-VinFast EVs

EVOne is the leading independent CCS2 network, with 200+ stations and 150+ DC plugs. They focus on urban areas and tourist destinations rather than highway rest stops, making them ideal for city charging and destination charging.

**Coverage on the route:**
- **Hanoi** — 35+ stations (Vincom Center, Times City, Aeon Mall)
- **Da Nang** — 20+ stations (Aeon Mall, Helio Center)
- **Nha Trang** — 12+ stations (Vincom Plaza, Lotte Mart)
- **HCMC** — 45+ stations (Vincom, Crescent Mall, AEON)
- **QL1A corridor** — 30+ stations (growing; check app for latest)

**Cost:** VND 4,000–6,000/kWh (pay-as-you-go); VND 3,500/kWh (VND 99,000/month subscription)

**Connectors:** CCS2 + CHAdeMO (120 kW max)

**App:** EVOne (iOS/Android) — supports international credit cards and e-wallets.

### 3. EBOOST — Ultra-Fast Highway Charging

EBOOST focuses on premium highway charging with 180–240 kW CCS2 ultra-fast chargers. Limited locations but strategically placed for the QL1A route.

**Key stations on the route:**
- **Phap Van** (Hanoi highway entrance) — 8 plugs, 180 kW
- **Ninh Binh** — 6 plugs, 180 kW
- **Da Nang (Tuy Loan)** — 4 plugs, 240 kW
- **Long Thanh** (near HCMC airport) — 6 plugs, 180 kW

**Cost:** VND 5,500–7,000/kWh — premium speed, premium price. Worth it for a quick top-up when you're in a hurry.

### 4. Dat Xanh — Building-Based Charging

Dat Xanh Group has installed charging stations at their residential and commercial properties. Mostly AC chargers (22 kW Type 2) and a few DC fast chargers, concentrated in HCMC and Hanoi. Useful for overnight city charging.

**Cost:** VND 3,200–4,500/kWh (AC); VND 4,500–5,500/kWh (DC)

### 5. Hotel and Resort Charging

Destination charging has become common at Vietnam's better hotels, especially Vinpearl properties and international chains. Most use Type 2 AC (7–22 kW) or VinFast proprietary DC chargers. Many are free for hotel guests.

## Detailed Route: Hanoi → Da Nang (660 km, 2 days)

### Day 1: Hanoi → Ninh Binh → Vinh (290 km)

**Departing Hanoi:** Charge to 100% before leaving. Best options:
- **V-Green at Landmark 72** (60 kW, 8 plugs) — fastest option leaving the city
- **EVOne at Times City or Aeon Mall Ha Dong** — CCS2, good for non-VinFast EVs
- **EBOOST Phap Van** (180 kW, 8 plugs) — right at the QL1A entrance, perfect last stop

**Departure tip:** Leave by 7 AM. Hanoi morning traffic is brutal — the first 30 km can take 90 minutes. Aim to be past Phap Van toll plaza by 8:30 AM.

**Stop 1: Ninh Binh (90 km, ~1.5 hr)**

Ninh Binh, known as "Ha Long Bay on land," is a perfect first pit stop. The V-Green DC station near Tam Coc and the EVOne station at Ninh Binh city center both offer 60 kW charging.

**If you're stopping for the day:** Take a 2-hour Tam Coc boat ride through limestone karst caves and rice paddies. Visit Bai Dinh Pagoda (Southeast Asia's largest Buddhist complex) or hike Mua Cave for the iconic panoramic view.

**Charging note:** If you started with 100%, you won't need to charge here unless you plan to explore Ninh Binh extensively. But it's a great rest stop regardless.

**Stop 2: Vinh (200 km from Ninh Binh, ~3 hr)**

Vinh marks the halfway point of Day 1. The V-Green DC station at Vinh city center has 6 plugs at 60 kW. EVOne also has a station at the Vinh bus station.

**Recommended:** Charge here even if you have range. The next 370 km to Da Nang is the longest stretch with limited charging in the middle (Dong Hoi is your only reliable stop).

**Where to stay in Vinh:** Most road trippers push through to Da Nang on Day 2, but if you want to break the journey:
- Muong Thanh Grand Vinh (Type 2 AC, free for guests)
- Vinpearl Hotel Vinh (V-Green DC, free for guests)

### Day 2: Vinh → Phong Nha → Hai Van Pass → Da Nang (370 km)

This is the most scenic and challenging day of the entire trip. The stretch from Vinh to Da Nang includes the UNESCO-listed Phong Nha National Park, the DMZ (Demilitarized Zone), the Hai Van Pass, and the approach to Da Nang's stunning coastline.

**Stop 1: Phong Nha (200 km from Vinh, ~3 hr)**

V-Green DC at Phong Nha visitor center (60 kW, 4 plugs). Charge while you explore the world's largest cave. The 30-minute boat ride into Phong Nha Cave itself is a must — stalactites, underground rivers, and cathedral-like chambers.

**Family tip:** Phong Nha offers easier cave experiences for kids (Phong Nha Cave boat ride, Paradise Cave walkways). The nearby pub and restaurant row in Phong Nha town has excellent Vietnamese food.

**Charging: EVOne also has a station at Phong Nha Farmstay (Type 2 AC, 22 kW)** — good for a slow charge while you have lunch.

**Stop 2: Dong Ha / Dong Hoi (optional top-up)**

If you're running low before the Hai Van Pass, the V-Green DC station at Dong Hoi (60 kW, 4 plugs) can give you a quick 20-minute boost. Charging here ensures you have plenty of range for the Hai Van climb.

**The Hai Van Pass — The Crown Jewel**

The 21 km Hai Van Pass between Da Nang and Hue is Vietnam's most famous driving road. Winding along the Truong Son mountain range with the South China Sea on one side, it's a motorcyclist's paradise and a driver's dream.

**EV considerations for the pass:**
- The climb from the Hue side gains 500 metres elevation over 10 km
- Expect 25–35% higher energy consumption on the climbing sections
- Regenerative braking on the descent recovers 5–10% charge (depending on your EV)
- **Top tip:** Arrive at the Da Nang (south) side of the pass with at least 40% charge. If you're coming from the north, charge to 80% in Hue or Dong Ha

**Don't miss the viewpoint at the Hai Van Pass summit (KM 0).** The panoramic view of Da Nang Bay, Son Tra Peninsula, and the Marble Mountains is worth every hairpin turn.

**Arriving in Da Nang**

Da Nang is Vietnam's most EV-friendly city after Hanoi and HCMC. Charging options:
- **V-Green at Vincom Da Nang** — 4 plugs, 60 kW DC, near the Han River bridge
- **EVOne at Aeon Mall Da Nang** — 6 plugs, CCS2 120 kW
- **InterContinental Danang Sun Peninsula** — 4x AC 22 kW (free for guests)
- **Best Western Premier Son Tra** — 2x Type 2 AC (free for guests)

## Da Nang: The Perfect Base for Exploration

Da Nang deserves at least a day or two. It's Vietnam's most livable city — beautiful beaches (My Khe, the "China Beach"), a vibrant food scene, and a gateway to both Hue and Hoi An.

### What to Do in Da Nang

- **My Khe Beach** — 20 km of white sand, gentle waves, and affordable beachfront restaurants. Perfect for families.
- **Son Tra Peninsula** — Home to the endangered red-shanked douc langur. The road to the summit (Ban Co Peak) is 600 m elevation gain — great for testing your EV's hill-climbing.
- **Marble Mountains** — Five marble and limestone hills with caves, pagodas, and panoramic views. Elevator available for families with small children.
- **Dragon Bridge** — See the bridge breathe fire and water at 9 PM on weekends.
- **Ba Na Hills** — 45-minute drive from Da Nang. The cable car holds world records, and the French Village is surreal. The climb to 1,487 m will use ~30% of your battery.

### EV-Friendly Hotels in Da Nang

- **InterContinental Danang Sun Peninsula** — 4x AC 22 kW + 2x DC 60 kW (free for guests) — the best EV hotel in central Vietnam
- **Vinpearl Da Nang Resort & Villas** — 4x DC 60 kW (free for guests)
- **Best Western Premier Son Tra** — 2x Type 2 AC (free for guests)
- **Premier Village Da Nang** — 4x Type 2 AC (free for guests)

### Day Trip: Da Nang → Hoi An (30 km)

A 30-minute drive south along Vo Nguyen Giap Street becomes Vo Chi Cong Road — one of Vietnam's most scenic coastal drives.

**Charging in Hoi An:**
- **Vinpearl Resort & Spa Hoi An** — 3x 60 kW DC (free for guests)
- **EVOne at Hoi An Night Market** — 2 plugs, CCS2 60 kW
- **Four Seasons The Nam Hai** — 2x DC 60 kW + 3x AC 22 kW (free for guests)
- **La Siesta Hoi An Resort & Spa** — 2x AC 22 kW (free for guests)

**Don't miss:** Hoi An Ancient Town after dark — the lantern-lit streets, the Japanese Covered Bridge, Cao Lau noodles at Ba Le, and a boat ride on the Thu Bon River releasing a floating lantern. It's magical for all ages.

## Detailed Route: Da Nang → HCMC (970 km, 2–3 days)

### Day 3: Da Nang → Quy Nhon → Nha Trang (530 km)

This is the longest driving day. Start early — ideally 6 AM.

**Stop 1: Quy Nhon (300 km from Da Nang, ~4 hr)**

Quy Nhon is an underrated coastal city. The V-Green DC station at Quy Nhon city center (60 kW, 4 plugs) is your primary charging stop.

**What to do while charging (30–40 min):**
- Grab Banh Xeo (Vietnamese sizzling pancakes) at a local spot near the station
- Visit the Thap Doi (Twin Cham Towers) — a 10-minute walk from the V-Green station
- Walk along the Quy Nhon beachfront promenade

**Alternative charging:** EVOne has a station at Quy Nhon's provincial bus station (CCS2, 60 kW).

**Stop 2: Tuy Hoa (optional top-up)**

If you're in a smaller EV (under 400 km range), stop at V-Green DC Tuy Hoa (60 kW, 4 plugs) for a quick 15-minute top-up. This gives you enough range to comfortably reach Nha Trang.

**Arriving in Nha Trang**

Nha Trang is Vietnam's premier beach resort city. The coastline with its islands, offshore reefs, and long beach promenade is spectacular.

**Charging in Nha Trang:**
- **V-Green at Vincom Plaza Nha Trang** — 4 plugs, 60 kW DC
- **EVOne at Lotte Mart Nha Trang** — CCS2, 60 kW
- **Vinpearl Nha Trang** — 4x DC 60 kW (free for guests)
- **EVOne at Nha Trang Center** — CCS2, 120 kW

**Where to stay:**
- **Vinpearl Nha Trang** (on Hon Tre Island, accessible by cable car) — 4x DC 60 kW, free for guests. The resort includes a water park, aquarium, and private beach. Incredible for families.
- **Mia Resort Nha Trang** — 2x Type 2 AC (free for guests)
- **InterContinental Nha Trang** — 2x Type 2 AC (free for guests)

**Don't miss:** The VinWonders Nha Trang amusement park on Hon Tre Island, boat trips to the islands (Mun Island for snorkeling, Tam Island for lunch), and the Po Nagar Cham Towers.

### Day 4: Nha Trang → Phan Thiet → HCMC (440 km)

The final driving day takes you down the southern coast, past fishing villages and salt flats, through the resort town of Phan Thiet (Mui Ne), and into the Mekong Delta region.

**Stop 1: Phan Thiet / Mui Ne (230 km from Nha Trang, ~3 hr)**

The V-Green DC station at Phan Thiet city center (60 kW, 4 plugs) and the EVOne station at Mui Ne (CCS2, 60 kW) bookend this popular beach area.

**What to do while charging:**
- Visit the Fairy Stream — a shallow creek through red sand dunes and jungle. Kids can walk in the water (ankle-deep) and spot tiny crabs.
- Red Sand Dunes at sunset — the kids will love "sand sledding" on pieces of cardboard.
- Fresh seafood at the Mui Ne fishing village morning market.

**Stop 2: Long Thanh / HCMC entrance**

The final charging stop before entering HCMC. EBOOST Long Thanh (180 kW, 6 plugs, CCS2) or V-Green DC at the Long Thanh petrol station before the HCMC ring road.

**Arriving in Ho Chi Minh City**

HCMC is chaotic, exhilarating, and full of energy. Driving in the city requires nerves of steel — the traffic is dense, scooters weave everywhere, and lane markings are merely suggestions.

**Charging in HCMC:**
- **V-Green at Landmark 81** (Binh Thanh District) — 8 plugs, 60 kW DC, covered parking
- **EVOne at Crescent Mall** (District 7) — 6 plugs, CCS2 120 kW
- **EVOne at AEON Tan Phu** — 6 plugs, CCS2 120 kW
- **EBOOST Long Thanh** (near the airport) — 180 kW, 6 plugs
- **Dat Xanh AC** at properties across Phu My Hung (District 7) — 22 kW Type 2

**Where to stay:**
- **InterContinental Saigon** — 2x DC 60 kW + 4x AC 22 kW (free for guests)
- **Vinpearl Landmark 81** — DC 60 kW (free for guests)
- **Renaissance Riverside Hotel Saigon** — 2x AC 22 kW (free for guests)
- **Hotel des Arts Saigon (MGallery)** — AC 11 kW (free for guests)

## Day Trip: HCMC → Can Tho & the Mekong Delta

The Mekong Delta is the rice bowl of Vietnam — a vast network of rivers, swamps, and islands. Can Tho, the delta's largest city, is 170 km from HCMC along QL1A.

**The drive:** 170 km, ~3 hours each way. QL1A is well-paved and relatively flat. The main challenge is traffic — expect slow-going through the delta towns of My Tho and Vinh Long.

**Charging on the route:**
- **V-Green DC My Tho** (70 km from HCMC, 60 kW) — good for a top-up
- **V-Green DC Can Tho** (170 km, 60 kW, 4 plugs) — located near the Ninh Kieu Wharf
- **EVOne Can Tho** — at the Can Tho bus station (CCS2, 60 kW)

**What to do in Can Tho:**
- **Cai Rang Floating Market** (6–9 AM) — the Mekong Delta's largest floating market. Hire a boat and watch locals trading fruit, vegetables, and noodles from their boats. Go early — it's most active at sunrise.
- **Bang Lang Stork Garden** — hundreds of storks nest here. Magical for kids.
- **Can Tho night market** — try banh xeo, hu tieu (southern-style noodle soup), and fresh sugarcane juice.

**Return charging:** Charge at V-Green Can Tho before heading back. It's a 170 km return with no reliable charging between Can Tho and My Tho.

## EV Market Context: Why Vietnam Is Southeast Asia's EV Leader

Vietnam's EV transition is unique in Southeast Asia — it's driven by a single domestic manufacturer with government backing, rather than a mix of international brands.

### VinFast Dominance

VinFast has sold over 150,000 EVs domestically since 2023. The VF 8 (420 km WLTP) is the most popular model for road trips, while the VF e34 (340 km WLTP) and VF 7 (350 km WLTP) dominate city driving. The VF 3 mini-SUV became the best-selling vehicle in Vietnam in 2025.

Key models on Vietnamese roads:
| Model | Range (WLTP) | Battery | Best For |
|-------|-------------|---------|----------|
| VinFast VF 8 | 420 km | 82 kWh | Road trips, families of 4 |
| VinFast VF e34 | 340 km | 42 kWh | City commuting |
| VinFast VF 7 | 350 km | 59.6 kWh | Compact family car |
| VinFast VF 9 | 438 km | 123 kWh | Large families (7 seats) |
| BYD Atto 3 (CCS2) | 410 km | 60 kWh | Non-VinFast road trips |
| MG 4 Electric (CCS2) | 350 km | 51 kWh | Budget CCS2 option |

### Tax Incentives (Continuing Until 2027)

- **0% registration fee** for EVs (vs. 10–12% for petrol cars) — saves VND 50–200 million on purchase
- **Reduced VAT** on EV charging services (8% vs. 10%)
- **Import tax exemption** on CBU (completely built-up) EVs
- **Free parking** at select VinFast-partnered locations
- **Toll road discounts** for EVs (some highways offer 50% off)

### BYD and Chinese Competition

BYD entered Vietnam in late 2025 with the Atto 3, and Chinese brands like MG and GAC Aion are establishing dealerships. The import tax elimination has made them price-competitive with VinFast. The main limitation is CCS2 charging infrastructure — though EVOne and EBOOST are expanding fast.

## Practical Tips for EV Driving in Vietnam

### Essential Apps

| App | Purpose | Notes |
|-----|---------|-------|
| **V-Green** | Find & pay at VinFast chargers | Mandatory for VinFast; QR payment |
| **EVOne** | Find & pay at EVOne stations | International card OK |
| **EBOOST** | Reserve ultra-fast chargers | Deposit required |
| **PlugShare** | Community-checked charger status | Best for seeing real-world reports from other EV drivers |
| **Google Maps** | Navigation | Download offline maps for rural areas |
| **Momo / VNPay** | Mobile payments | Essential for V-Green and many EVOne stations |
| **Grab** | Ride-hailing | Has **Grab EV** option in Hanoi, HCMC, and Da Nang — great for last-kilometer transport |

### Grab EV Option in Cities

Grab has deployed over 1,000 VinFast EVs in Hanoi, HCMC, and Da Nang. When you book a Grab in these cities, select the "GrabEV" option (usually VND 2,000–3,000/km, slightly less than standard car). It's a great way to get around without worrying about parking or city traffic — especially in HCMC's District 1.

### Finding Chargers: PlugShare Is Your Best Friend

While the V-Green and EVOne apps cover their own networks, **PlugShare** aggregates all networks and shows user-reported status. Before you set off each day:
1. Open PlugShare and filter by CCS2 + VinFast proprietary
2. Check recent check-ins (last 24 hours) for working status
3. Read comments about station condition, payment issues, and nearby amenities
4. Flag your planned charging stops for the day

**Pro tip:** PlugShare reviews often mention "this station is behind the petrol station, look for the blue sign" or "bring exact change — card reader broken." These real-world details are invaluable.

### Hotel Booking with EV Charging

Always confirm charging availability when booking. Many hotels list "EV charging" but the charger may be:
- Occupied by another guest
- Broken and not yet repaired
- Only compatible with VinFast proprietary (not CCS2)

**How to confirm:**
1. Call or message the hotel via Booking.com or Agoda chat
2. Ask: "What type of charger? (VinFast proprietary / Type 2 AC / CCS2 DC)"
3. Ask: "Is it reserved for hotel guests? Any fee?"
4. Offer to pay a deposit to reserve the EV bay

**Best EV-friendly hotel chains:**
- **Vinpearl** — All properties have V-Green DC chargers, free for guests
- **InterContinental** — DC + AC chargers at all Vietnam properties
- **Four Seasons Nam Hai** (Hoi An) — DC + AC, free for guests
- **Mia Resort** (Nha Trang) — Type 2 AC, free for guests

### What to Pack for an EV Road Trip in Vietnam

- **Type 2 charging cable** — For hotel AC chargers (most hotels don't provide cables)
- **CCS2 to VinFast adapter** (if driving a non-VinFast EV) — ~VND 3,000,000
- **Granny charger (Type 2 to Schuko)** — For emergency charging at homestays or wall outlets
- **Power bank** — For keeping your phone alive (essential for charging apps)
- **Offline maps** — Download QL1A corridor on Google Maps
- **Rain gear** — Vietnam's weather is unpredictable; rain jackets for sudden downpours
- **Reusable water bottles** — Most charging stops have water refill stations
- **Sunscreen and hats** — For beach stops and temple visits
- **Polarized sunglasses** — The sun on QL1A is intense, especially heading south

### Driving Safety Tips

- **Watch for scooters** — They're everywhere, day and night. Check your mirrors constantly.
- **Use your horn** — In Vietnam, a light tap on the horn signals "I'm here" rather than aggression. It's expected.
- **Overtake carefully** — QL1A is mostly two lanes. Overtake only when you have clear visibility.
- **Beware of livestock** — Cows, water buffalo, and dogs wander onto QL1A, especially in Central Vietnam.
- **Night driving is not recommended** — QL1A at night has poor lighting, unlit trucks, and scooters without headlights. Plan to arrive at your destination by 6 PM.
- **Toll booths accept cash and e-tags** — Get an e-tag (VND 100,000 deposit at any toll station) for faster passage.

## Seasonal Considerations

### Best Time: December–April (Dry Season)

- Clear skies across the entire route
- Hai Van Pass visibility is excellent
- Nha Trang and Da Nang beaches are at their best
- Mekong Delta is accessible (roads are dry)
- Range is optimal (no A/C needed for cooling or defogging)

### May–August (Hot Season)

- Central Vietnam (Da Nang, Hoi An, Nha Trang) is hot and humid
- A/C usage reduces range by 10–15%
- Afternoon thunderstorms are common
- Good: beaches are warm, crowds are moderate
- Tip: charge in the cooler morning or evening for best battery efficiency

### September–November (Typhoon Season)

- **Avoid the Hai Van Pass during typhoons** — landslides and closures are common
- Central Vietnam (Da Nang, Hue) gets heavy flooding
- Coastal highways may be closed
- Range can drop 15–20% due to heavy rain and A/C (defogging)
- Mekong Delta floods — some roads become impassable
