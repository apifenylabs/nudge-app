# Myanmar EV Charging Landscape 2026 — Emerging Market, Real Potential

Myanmar represents one of Southeast Asia's most challenging yet potentially rewarding EV markets. In 2026, the country's charging infrastructure remains in its infancy, with Yangon leading the way and plans for a Mandalay–Naypyidaw–Yangon highway charging corridor slowly taking shape. # Myanmar EV Charging Landscape 2026 — Emerging Market, Real Potential

**Last updated: June 7, 2026**

Myanmar is one of Southeast Asia's most complex — and most intriguing — EV stories. With a population of over 54 million, a rapidly urbanising Yangon, and a government pushing hard on EV adoption despite serious infrastructure challenges, the country presents a unique mix of opportunity and reality.

As of mid-2026, here's where things stand: **87 licensed companies** are active in Myanmar's EV sector, imports of battery electric vehicles (BEVs) attract **zero customs duty**, charging stations are expanding along the Yangon-Mandalay highway corridor, and a growing number of EV models — mostly from China — are hitting Yangon's streets.

But the reality is also sobering. Myanmar's national grid is unreliable, charging infrastructure is almost entirely concentrated in major cities, and the political situation means many parts of the country remain difficult to access for foreign travellers.

**Can you drive an EV in Myanmar in 2026?** Yes — if you stick to the Yangon-Mandalay corridor and plan carefully. For the adventurous traveller with patience, Myanmar's EV landscape is worth understanding.

Let's break down everything you need to know.

## Why Myanmar Matters for EV Travel in 2026

Myanmar occupies a strategic position in mainland Southeast Asia, bordering Thailand, Laos, China, India, and Bangladesh. For EV overlanders, Myanmar is the missing link between Thailand's mature EV infrastructure and South Asia's emerging networks.

## The Policy Landscape

### Zero Import Duty on BEVs (April 2025 – March 2026)

On March 31, 2025, the Ministry of Planning and Finance issued **Notification No. 27/2025**, reducing the customs duty rate for battery electric vehicles (BEVs) and their essential components to **zero percent**. This exemption applies to:

- Completely Built-Up (CBU) vehicles — fully assembled imports
- Completely Knocked Down (CKD) kits — assembled locally
- Semi-Knocked Down (SKD) kits — partially assembled
- Essential EV components and accessories as per Myanmar's 2022 Customs Tariff

This policy was initially effective until **March 31, 2026**, with strong expectations of extension given the government's EV ambitions.

### Pilot Import Programme (January 2025 – March 2026)

The Ministry of Commerce's **Notification No. 40/2025** established a formal pilot project for EV imports with clear requirements:

**For importers:**
- Approval from the National-Level Steering Committee
- Registration certificate for an EV sales showroom
- Import quotas determined by the committee
- Mandatory warranties, spare parts, and after-sales service arrangements
- Bank guarantee of **MMK 500 million (~$240,000 USD)** deposited with the Central Bank

**For showroom requirements:**
- Minimum compound area: 10,000 sq ft
- Showroom/service/waiting area: minimum 5,000 sq ft
- Warehouse: minimum 5,000 sq ft
- Glass front and sides per international standards
- 50 sq ft signboard displaying the trademark
- Must be a standalone commercial building (not residential)
- 10% area deviation allowed depending on location

These requirements are designed to ensure that EV importers are serious players with proper infrastructure, not fly-by-night operators.

### Reduced Duties on ICE Vehicles (June 2025 – May 2026)

Interestingly, the government also reduced customs duties on fuel-powered vehicles manufactured domestically under SKD and CKD systems (Notification No. 39/2025). SKD passenger vehicles under 2000cc now attract just **5% duty** (down from 7.5%), and CKD versions just **3%** (down from 5%). This dual approach suggests a pragmatic transition rather than an overnight EV mandate.

## Charging Infrastructure in Myanmar

### The State of Charging Networks

Myanmar's charging infrastructure is still in its early stages but growing steadily. As of mid-2026:

- **Approved charging stations**: Over **140 stations** have been approved by the steering committee
- **Operational stations**: Approximately **60+** are actually installed and working
- **AC vs DC**: ~99% of Myanmar's charging stations currently operate on AC grid power
- **Urban concentration**: The majority are in **Yangon**, with growing coverage in Mandalay, Naypyidaw, and along the Yangon-Mandalay highway

### Key Charging Locations

#### Yangon (the most EV-ready city)

Yangon has the highest concentration of charging stations in Myanmar. You'll find chargers at:

- **Shopping centres**: Junction City, Myanmar Plaza, Capital Hypermarket, Ocean Supercentre
- **Hotels**: Sedona Hotel Yangon, Novotel Yangon Max, Chatrium Hotel Royal Lake, Parkroyal Yangon
- **Apartment complexes**: Star City (Thanlyin), several condominiums in Bahan and Tamwe townships
- **Dedicated charging hubs**: A few standalone charging stations in Hlaingthaya and Insein industrial zones

#### Yangon–Mandalay Highway Corridor

This is the most important EV route in Myanmar. The **NR1/Yangon-Mandalay Expressway** (approximately 650 km / 400 miles) now has roughly **15 operational charging points** at:

- **Bago** (~80 km from Yangon): 1-2 stations at highway rest stops
- **Naypyidaw** (~320 km from Yangon): The capital has 5+ stations at government buildings and hotels
- **Meiktila** (~470 km from Yangon): Junction town with growing charger coverage
- **Mandalay** (~650 km from Yangon): 5+ stations at hotels, shopping centres, and the university area

The spacing is roughly **40-60 km between chargers** on the main highway, which is manageable for modern EVs with 300+ km range.

#### Other Routes

- **Yangon–Pathein** (Irrawaddy Delta): 2-3 chargers under development
- **Yangon–Mawlamyine** (southeast via Phayagyi-Thaton): 3-5 chargers planned/installed
- **Mandalay–Bagan** (the classic tourist route, ~180 km): 2-3 stations operating at Bagan hotels
- **Mandalay–Hsipaw–Lashio** (northeastern hill route): Very limited — bring patience

### Charging Speed and Reliability

The hard truth: Myanmar's charging infrastructure is **AC-dominant and grid-dependent**.

- **AC charging (Level 2)**: Most stations offer 7-22 kW AC. A full charge from empty takes 4-8 hours
- **DC fast charging**: Limited availability. A few stations (mostly newer installations) offer 50-60 kW CCS2
- **Grid reliability**: Yangon has relatively stable power, but the national grid faces frequent load-shedding. Many charging stations operate solar battery buffers to maintain uptime

**Pro tip**: If you're travelling beyond Yangon, call ahead to confirm a charger is working. The EV community in Myanmar is small and active on Facebook groups — join them before your trip.

### Payment Methods

EV charging payment in Myanmar is fragmented:

- **Mobile apps**: Some operators have their own apps (mostly in Burmese)
- **QR code / Wave Money**: The most common local payment method
- **Bank transfer**: Accepted at most stations
- **Cash / Credit cards**: Less common — carry MMK cash as backup
- **Hotel charging**: Usually free for guests (negotiate in advance)

## EV Travel Routes in Myanmar

### Route 1: Yangon → Naypyidaw → Mandalay (The Classic)

**Distance**: ~650 km
**Recommendation**: ✅ Feasible with planning
**Charging confidence**: Medium

This is Myanmar's most EV-friendly route. Charging stations are spaced 40-60 km apart along the highway. Start Yangon with a full charge, top up in Naypyidaw (the capital has the best charging infrastructure outside Yangon), and refill at Meiktila before reaching Mandalay.

**Day trip from Mandalay: Mandalay → Bagan**
- Distance: ~180 km
- Charging: Bagan has 2-3 hotel chargers. Your hotel will likely let you charge overnight
- Why go: Bagan's 2,000+ ancient temples are one of Asia's greatest archaeological sites

### Route 2: Yangon → Bago → Golden Rock (Kyaiktiyo)

**Distance**: ~250 km (return to Yangon same route)
**Recommendation**: ⚠️ Adventurous — check station availability
**Charging confidence**: Low-Medium

Golden Rock is one of Myanmar's most sacred Buddhist sites. The route goes via Bago (80 km from Yangon) where you can charge. The final approach involves a truck ride to the summit — you won't drive an EV to the top.

### Route 3: Yangon → Pathein (Irrawaddy Delta)

**Distance**: ~190 km
**Recommendation**: ⚠️ Verify charger status before departing
**Charging confidence**: Low

Pathein is famous for its colourful umbrellas and the Irrawaddy Delta. Charging infrastructure is basic but improving. Ideal as a day trip if you can charge at your destination.

### Route 4: Mandalay → Hsipaw (Shan Hills)

**Distance**: ~200 km
**Recommendation**: ❓ Only for experienced overlanders
**Charging confidence**: Very Low

The road to Hsipaw winds through some of Myanmar's most beautiful mountain scenery, but charging infrastructure is minimal. If you attempt this route, carry a portable charger and plan overnight charging at your guesthouse.

## Practical Challenges for EV Travel in Myanmar

### 1. Grid Reliability

Myanmar's national electricity grid is under strain. **Load-shedding** (scheduled blackouts) is common, especially during the hot season (March-May) and dry season.

- **Yangon**: Relatively stable (3-6 hours daily load-shedding)
- **Mandalay**: Moderate (6-8 hours daily)
- **Smaller towns**: Unpredictable (8-12+ hours daily)
- **Rural areas**: May only have power 6-12 hours per day

Many hotels and businesses run generators during outages. Check if your planned charging location has generator backup.

### 2. Charger Compatibility

Almost all new public chargers in Myanmar use **CCS2** — the same standard as Thailand, Laos, and most of ASEAN. However:

- **CHAdeMO**: Very rare
- **Type 2 AC**: Common at slower chargers
- **Domestic plug (Type C/D/G)**: Useful as backup if you carry a portable charger

### 3. Seasonal Considerations

| Season | Months | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Hot season | Mar-May | Peak electricity demand, more load-shedding |
| Rainy season | Jun-Oct | Flooding possible on Yangon-Mandalay highway |
| Cool season | Nov-Feb | Best travel window — stable power, comfortable driving |

### 4. Documentation and Permits

Foreign travellers driving their own vehicle into Myanmar need:
- **Carnet de Passage** (or temporary import permit)
- **International Driving Permit** (IDP) — Myanmar is not a signatory to the 1968 Vienna Convention, so check with Myanmar embassies
- **Vehicle insurance** purchased at the border
- **Tourist visa** with sufficient validity

Myanmar's land borders with Thailand are open for EV overlanding at **Mae Sot–Myawaddy** and **Mae Sai–Tachilek**, but conditions change frequently. **Check current border status before planning a crossing.**

## The Bottom Line: Should You EV Road Trip in Myanmar?

**Yes — with caveats.** If you're already in Thailand with an EV and want to push into less-travelled territory, Myanmar offers an unforgettable experience. The Yangon-Mandalay-Bagan triangle is feasible with careful planning, and the lack of tourist crowds means you'll have temples and roads almost to yourself.

**Best for**: Experienced EV travellers, overlanders with flexible schedules, heritage and culture seekers
**Not ideal for**: First-time EV road trippers, travellers needing guaranteed infrastructure, short itineraries

### Quick Tips

1. **Join the Myanmar EV community** — Facebook groups like "Myanmar Electric Vehicle Owners" are invaluable for real-time charger status
2. **Always carry a backup charging cable** — Type 2 to Type 2 and a domestic socket adapter can save you
3. **Plan for load-shedding** — Charge early in the day when power is more stable
4. **Stay at EV-friendly hotels** — Many hotels in Yangon, Mandalay, and Bagan now advertise EV charging
5. **Carry MMK cash** — Credit card acceptance is limited outside major hotels and restaurants
6. **Check border crossings** — Mae Sot–Myawaddy is the most reliable for overland entry from Thailand

## What's Next for Myanmar's EV Sector?

Looking ahead, several trends will shape Myanmar's EV landscape:

- **Extension of zero-duty policy**: Strongly expected given the government's commitment to the pilot programme
- **Solar + charging integration**: A research paper from HAL (hal.science/hal-05356643) highlights how solar-powered charging could revolutionise Myanmar's EV infrastructure — bypassing grid reliability issues entirely
- **E-bus expansion**: Licensed companies like Yar Zar Min Industry and Mandalar Min Trading & Transportation are already operating electric bus services in Yangon and Mandalay
- **Chinese investment**: BYD, MG, GAC, and other Chinese OEMs are deepening their Myanmar presence through local assembly partnerships

Myanmar's EV story is still in its early chapters — but it's a story worth watching. For the traveller willing to venture off the beaten path, it offers one of Southeast Asia's last true road trip frontiers.
