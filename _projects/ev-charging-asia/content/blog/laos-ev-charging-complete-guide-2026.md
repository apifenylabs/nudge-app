# Laos EV Charging Complete Guide 2026 — The EV Revolution in the Land of a Million Elephants

**Last updated: June 7, 2026**

Laos is quietly becoming one of Southeast Asia's most surprising EV success stories. Sandwiched between EV powerhouse Thailand and the world's largest EV market (China), this landlocked country of 7.5 million people just fired a shot heard across ASEAN: **a complete ban on petrol and diesel vehicle imports from June 1, 2026 through the end of the year.**

That's right — as of this month, you literally cannot import a new ICE (internal combustion engine) vehicle into Laos. It's one of the strongest pro-EV policies in the entire region, and it signals that Laos is betting big on electric mobility.

But can you actually drive an EV in Laos in 2026? **Yes, and surprisingly well.** With a homegrown charging network (LOCA) covering every one of the country's 18 provinces, cheap hydropower electricity, and an increasingly EV-friendly corridor from Vientiane to Luang Prabang, Laos is ready for your electric road trip.

Let's dive into everything you need to know.

## Why Laos Matters for EV Travel in 2026

Laos has long been the overlooked sibling in Southeast Asian road trips. The lack of infrastructure, the winding mountain roads, the limited accommodation — for years, it was strictly 4x4 and adventure territory. But 2026 is different:

| Metric | 2024 | 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public EV charging stations | ~40 | **120+** | 3× growth |
| Provinces with charging coverage | 12 | **18 (100%)** | Full coverage |
| Fast-chargers (50 kW+) | ~15 | **80+** | 5× growth |

| ICE vehicle imports (2026) | ~15,000 | **0** (banned) | 100% reduction |

The import ban alone is historic. Government decree No. 1235/MPI, effective June 1, 2026, suspends all import permits for petrol and diesel vehicles (passenger cars, SUVs, pickups, and motorcycles) through December 31, 2026. This is a temporary measure with long-term implications — the government has made clear it will not reverse course.

The target is audacious: **30% of all vehicles on Lao roads electric by 2030.**

## The Policy Landscape: Laos Goes All-In on EVs

### The Import Ban (Effective June 1, 2026)

The headline policy is the temporary suspension of ICE vehicle imports. Here's how it works:

- **Scope**: All passenger vehicles, SUVs, pickups, and motorcycles with internal combustion engines
- **Exemptions**: Passenger transport vehicles (buses, vans), machinery/construction equipment, trucks for goods transport, specialized vehicles (ambulances, fire trucks)
- **Duration**: June 1, 2026 to December 31, 2026
- **Renewal**: The government has indicated the ban may be extended or made permanent depending on results
- **Rationale**: Reduce fuel import expenditure (Laos imports nearly all its petroleum), leverage domestic hydropower, meet 2030 EV targets

The message is clear: if you're buying a new car in Laos in the second half of 2026, it needs a plug.

### Tax Incentives for EVs

- **Excise tax exemption**: EVs priced under $50,000 pay **0% excise tax** (compared to 10-50% for ICE vehicles depending on engine size)
- **Import duty reduction**: Reduced duties on EV components and completely knocked down (CKD) kits
- **Standardized pricing**: The Ministry of Industry and Commerce now mandates a standardized pricing structure for EVs, preventing dealers from price-gouging on the limited supply

### Why It Works: Laos Is a Hydropower Giant

Laos generates over **80% of its electricity from hydropower** — and produces far more than it consumes. The country exports billions of dollars worth of electricity to Thailand, Vietnam, and Cambodia annually. Electricity in Laos is:

- **Cheap**: Residential rates around LAK 450-710/kWh ($0.025-0.04 USD)
- **Clean**: ~80%+ renewable already
- **Domestic**: No reliance on imported fuel

For EV owners, this means charging costs are among the lowest in Southeast Asia — and the environmental case is stronger than in coal-dependent grids.

## Charging Networks in Laos

### LOCA: The National Champion

LOCA is the undisputed king of Lao EV charging. As of April 2026, they operate **78 stations** covering **all 18 provinces** — from Phongsaly in the far north to Champasak in the south.

**LOCA Network Stats (April 2026)**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Total stations | 78 |
| Provinces covered | 18/18 (100%) |
| Average spacing on major routes | ≤50 km |
| Fast-chargers (50 kW+) | ~60 of 78 stations |
| Target by end of 2026 | 100+ stations |
| Payment | LOCA app (bank transfer/card) |

**Key LOCA stations for travelers:**

- **Vientiane**: Multiple stations at shopping centres (Vientiane Center, Lao ITECC), hotels, and along Thanon Luang Prabang
- **Vang Vieng**: LOCA station at the main town entrance and at hotels
- **Luang Prabang**: Stations near the night market, at the Lao Telecom building, and several resort chargers
- **Savannakhet**: Stations on Route 9 and at central hotels
- **Pakse (Champasak)**: Station at the bus terminal and central district
- **Luang Namtha**: Northern hub station for the China border corridor
- **Oudomxay**: Station at the provincial capital
- **Phongsaly**: The northernmost station in Laos

**How to use LOCA:**
1. Download the LOCA EV app (iOS/Android)
2. Register with a phone number (Lao and international numbers work)
3. Top up via bank transfer or credit card
4. Scan the QR code on the charger to start
5. Pricing: ~LAK 1,500-2,500/kWh ($0.08-0.14 USD) depending on location and charger speed

The app has an English language option, and the LOCA team is responsive to traveler queries via their Facebook page.

An increasing number of hotels in Laos offer EV charging, especially in tourist hubs:

- **Vientiane**: Crowne Plaza Vientiane, Landmark Mekong Riverside Hotel, Lao Plaza Hotel, Lao Heritage Hotel
- **Luang Prabang**: Sofitel Luang Prabang, Rosewood Luang Prabang, Le Bel Air Resort, Luang Say Residence, MyLaoHome

Always confirm with the hotel before arrival, as some chargers may be single-point and occupied.

### Cross-Border Charging

**From Thailand:**
Thailand's PTT/EVolt stations are now common at Nong Khai (the border city across from Vientiane). You can charge at:
- **PTT Nong Khai** (Thailand side, ~5 km from Thai-Lao Friendship Bridge)
- **EA Anywhere Nong Khai** (multiple stations)
- Then cross the bridge and charge at LOCA Donkoy or BlueDot Donkoy on the Lao side

The First Thai-Lao Friendship Bridge (Nong Khai to Vientiane capital) is EV-friendly. Keep your passport and vehicle import documents ready — the Lao customs process for foreign vehicles is straightforward but requires patience (30-60 minutes).

**From China:**
The Laos-China Railway has brought massive infrastructure investment along the NR3 corridor. The Mohan-Boten border crossing now has:
- Chinese charging stations (State Grid, TELD) on the Chinese side
- LOCA station at Boten (Lao side) — 60 kW CCS2
- Stations every 80-100 km between Boten and Luang Namtha → Luang Prabang

## Key Routes for EV Road Trips in Laos

### Route 1: Vientiane → Vang Vieng → Luang Prabang (The Classic NR13 Corridor)

This is the most popular EV road trip route in Laos. The total distance is ~380 km on National Highway 13 (NR13), one of the best-maintained roads in the country.

**Segment 1: Vientiane → Vang Vieng (~150 km, 2.5 hours)**

| Stop | Distance | Charging | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vientiane | 0 km | LOCA (multiple), BlueDot Donkoy | Charge to 100% before departure |
| KM21 | ~30 km | BlueDot×PTT (60 kW) | Quick top-up option |
| Nongtaeng | ~75 km | BlueDot×PTT, LOCA | Midpoint, solid restaurant stop |
| Vang Vieng | ~150 km | LOCA town station, hotel chargers, BlueDot×PTT | Charge here for sure |

- **Road condition**: Excellent paved highway, mostly flat through the plains, slight climb into the Vang Vieng valley
- **Toll**: No tolls on NR13
- **Scenic stops**: Nam Ngum Dam viewpoint (km 70), the karst landscape approaching Vang Vieng

**Segment 2: Vang Vieng → Luang Prabang (~230 km, 4 hours)**

| Stop | Distance | Charging | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vang Vieng | 0 km | Fully charge here | Elevation climb ahead |
| Kasi | ~75 km | No public charging (hotel only) | Highest point on route (1,400m) |
| Phou Khoun | ~90 km | No public charging | Scenic viewpoint, cold weather |
| Luang Prabang | ~230 km | LOCA (2 stations), BlueDot×PTT, hotel chargers | Arrive with 20-30% |

- **Road condition**: Winding mountain road with steep climbs and descents. The section from Kasi to Phou Khoun is the most challenging — expect 15-20% higher energy consumption on the uphill
- **Warning**: No public chargers between Vang Vieng and Luang Prabang for ~230 km. You need a vehicle with at least **250 km real-world range** on this segment (or stop at a hotel with a slow charger in Kasi)
- **Elevation**: Vientiane (170m) → Vang Vieng (250m) → Phou Khoun (1,400m) → Luang Prabang (300m)

### Route 2: Vientiane → Thakhek → Savannakhet → Pakse (Southern Corridor)

This is a longer 3-4 day route through southern Laos, passing the famous Kong Lor Cave (Route 8) and through the Mekong River towns.

| Segment | Distance | Charging | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vientiane → Paksan | ~150 km | LOCA Paksan | Good morning departure |
| Paksan → Thakhek | ~175 km | LOCA Thakhek, BlueDot×PTT | Kong Lor Cave detour (40 km from Route 8) |
| Thakhek → Savannakhet | ~115 km | LOCA Savannakhet, BlueDot×PTT | Short day, explore Savannakhet |
| Savannakhet → Pakse | ~200 km | LOCA Pakse, BlueDot×PTT | Last segment |

- **Road condition**: Good on NR13, variable on secondary routes. Route 8 to Kong Lor is well-paved but narrow
- **Tolls**: None
- **Best part**: The Thakhek Loop (Route 12) offers spectacular limestone karst scenery

### Route 3: Cross-Border from Thailand (Nong Khai → Vientiane)

This is the most common entry point for foreign EVs.

| Step | Notes |
|---|---|
| 1. Charge at Nong Khai (Thailand) | Multiple PTT and EA Anywhere stations |
| 2. Cross First Thai-Lao Friendship Bridge | ~$2.50 toll. Need vehicle docs, passport, insurance |
| 3. Lao customs/immigration | Allow 30-60 min. Temporary import permit issued |
| 4. CHARGE at Donkoy (BlueDot×PTT or LOCA) | Immediately after immigration. Don't skip this. |
| 5. Proceed to Vientiane city | ~20 km from bridge |

**Important**: You need **Lao temporary vehicle import** documentation. Most rental EVs from Thailand (like those from EVRent Thailand) handle this for you, but if self-driving, prepare:
- Vehicle registration book (original)
- Passport (valid 6+ months)
- Driver's license (international driving permit recommended)
- Vehicle insurance covering Laos (cross-border coverage from Thai insurers)
- Temporary import permit cost: ~LAK 50,000-100,000 ($3-6 USD)

### Route 4: Luang Prabang → Oudomxay → Luang Namtha → Boten (China Border)

This is the northern corridor tracking the Laos-China Railway and NR3.

| Segment | Distance | Charging | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Luang Prabang | 0 km | Fully charge | |
| Oudomxay | ~200 km | LOCA Oudomxay | Mountain roads |
| Luang Namtha | ~120 km | LOCA Luang Namtha, BlueDot×PTT Houayxay | |
| Boten (China border) | ~60 km | LOCA Boten | Cross into Mohan (China) |

- **Road condition**: NR3 is Chinese-funded and well-maintained. High elevation passes (1,000-1,500m)
- **Range caution**: Similar to Vang Vieng-Luang Prabang, charge fully at each opportunity

## Costs: What to Budget

### Charging Costs

| Charger Type | Cost per kWh | Cost per 100 km (a typical EV) |
|---|---|---|
| LOCA fast charger | LAK 1,500-2,500/kWh | LAK 25,000-42,000 ($1.40-2.40) |
| BlueDot×PTT fast charger | LAK 2,000/kWh | LAK 34,000 ($1.90) |
| Hotel slow charger | LAK 0-1,000/kWh (often free for guests) | LAK 0-17,000 ($0-0.95) |
| Home charging (if staying) | LAK 450-710/kWh | LAK 7,600-12,000 ($0.42-0.67) |

Compare this to petrol: a similar ICE vehicle would cost LAK 400,000-500,000 ($22-28) per 100 km in Laos. **EV charging is roughly 10-20× cheaper than petrol.**

### Toll Roads

There are very few toll roads in Laos. On the major routes you'll use (NR13, NR3, NR9), there are no tolls. The only tolls to expect are:
- **Thai-Lao Friendship Bridge crossing**: ~$1.50-2.00 per crossing
- **Urban expressways**: Very few, mostly in Vientiene outskirts
- **Cash only**: Most tolls are cash-only in LAK or Thai baht (at border crossings)

### Accommodation

Hotels with EV charging command a slight premium, but less than in Thailand:
- Budget: $15-25/night (no charging, but LOCA stations are nearby)
- Mid-range with charging: $30-60/night
- Luxury resorts with charging: $80-200+/night

## Practical Tips for Driving an EV in Laos

### The Language Factor

Lao is the official language. English is spoken at hotels, tourist sites, and some LOCA stations, but it's limited at rural gas stations and smaller towns. **Download Google Translate (Lao language pack)** before you go. LOCA's app has English support.

### Which EVs Work Best

| Vehicle | Real-world range (Laos conditions) | Suitability |
|---|---|---|
| BYD ATTO 3 | 350-380 km | Excellent — abundant in Laos |
| BYD Dolphin | 300-330 km | Good for city driving |
| MG ZS EV | 280-310 km | Good, popular |
| Neta V | 250-280 km | Adequate with careful planning |
| Tesla Model 3/Y | 400-450 km | Excellent range but no Tesla service center in Laos |
| Ora Good Cat | 300-330 km | Good, increasingly common |
| Toyota bZ4X | 280-310 km | Sold through Toyota Laos |

**Minimum recommended range**: 250 km real-world. The 230 km Vang Vieng → Luang Prabang segment with no midpoint charger is the limiting factor for smaller-range EVs.

### Charging App You Need

1. **LOCA EV** — The #1 app. Covers all 18 provinces
2. **BlueDot** — For the PTT partnership stations
3. **Google Maps** — LOCA stations are increasingly mapped. Search "LOCA EV" or "ที่ชาร์จรถไฟฟ้า"
4. **PlugShare** — Growing coverage in Laos. User-reported stations and hotel chargers

### When to Go

- **Best**: November to February (dry season, cool temperatures — better for EV range)
- **Good**: March to May (dry season, hot — AC use reduces range by 10-15%)
- **Challenging**: June to October (rainy season — roads are fine on NR13, but landslides occasionally close mountain sections)
- **Festivals**: Pi Mai (Lao New Year, April 14-16) is peak season. Book hotels and charging well in advance

### Safety & Road Conditions

- Laos drives on the **right** side of the road (unlike Thailand!)
- NR13 between Vang Vieng and Luang Prabang is winding and narrow in sections — expect 40-60 km/h average speed
- Overtaking on mountain roads is risky. Be patient
- **Carry cash** (LAK) — most rural gas stations and toll booths don't take cards
- 4G/LTE coverage is good along NR13 and in all provincial capitals. Remote areas may have no signal
- LOCA customer support hotline: available via the app for charger issues

### Charging Etiquette

- Don't ICE (Internal Combustion Engine) parking at charging spots — though with the import ban, ICE vehicles may become rarer
- Lao drivers tend to park at chargers for the full charge cycle. Slow chargers at hotels are often occupied overnight
- Report broken chargers in the LOCA app or on PlugShare
