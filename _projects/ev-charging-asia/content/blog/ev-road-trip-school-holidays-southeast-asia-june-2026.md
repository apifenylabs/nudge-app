# Best EV Road Trips for June 2026 School Holidays: 5 Family Routes in Southeast Asia

The **June school holidays** (typically early June through mid-July across most of Southeast Asia) are a prime window for family EV road trips. Monsoon season is just starting — meaning crowds are thinner, accommodation is cheaper, and the landscapes are lush.

We've mapped five family-friendly EV routes with verified charging infrastructure so you can plan without range anxiety.

## 1. Kuala Lumpur → Penang → Perhentian Islands (Malaysia)

**Distance**: ~650 km (3–4 days driving with stops)
**Best for**: Beach families, snorkelling, national park

This is Malaysia's most established EV road trip corridor. The North-South Expressway (PLUS) has Gentari and ChargeSini 150 kW DC fast chargers at almost every rest stop.

**Charging stops**:
- KL → Tapah R&R (160 km) — Gentari 150 kW
- Tapah → Penang (250 km) — Juru Auto-City DC charger
- Penang → Besut (320 km) — charging at Perhentian Island jetty area (AC only, charge overnight)

**Family highlights**:
- Batu Ferringhi night market in Penang
- Perhentian Kecil snorkelling (turtles, reef sharks — June visibility is 15–20 m)
- Taman Negara (National Park) side trip from Kuala Tembeling

**EV tip**: The Besut–Perhentian ferry doesn't take cars. Park at the jetty car park (covered, with basic AC charging) and take the speedboat.

## 2. Bangkok → Hua Hin → Khao Yai Circuit (Thailand)

**Loop distance**: ~500 km
**Best for**: Mix of beaches, mountains, and wildlife

This circular route hits three of Thailand's most family-friendly destinations with excellent EA Anywhere charging coverage.

**Charging stops**:
- Bangkok → Hua Hin (200 km) — EA Anywhere 120 kW at Cha-Am PTT station
- Hua Hin → Khao Yai (250 km) — PTT EV Station PluZ at Saraburi
- Khao Yai → Bangkok (170 km) — plenty of options on Highway 1

**Family highlights**:
- Hua Hin beach and Cicada Night Market (weekends only)
- Khao Yai National Park night safari (wild elephants, deer, hornbills)
- PB Valley Winery (Khao Yai) — yes, they have EV charging

**EV tip**: Book accommodation with wall charging — many Khao Yai resorts offer free overnight AC charging for guests.

## 3. Denpasar → Ubud → Amed East Bali Circuit (Indonesia)

**Loop distance**: ~200 km (2–3 days)
**Best for**: Culture, rice terraces, snorkelling, and scenic mountain driving

Bali's compact size makes it ideal for EVs, and SPKLU (PLN) now has decent coverage on main routes.

**Charging stops**:
- Denpasar airport → Ubud (40 km) — SPKLU 50 kW at Gianyar
- Ubud → Amed (80 km) — SPKLU at Candidasa (50 kW)
- Amed → Denpasar (100 km) — SPKLU 50 kW at Klungkung

**Family highlights**:
- Ubud Monkey Forest and Tegallalang Rice Terraces
- Tirta Gangga Water Palace (kids love the pools)
- Amed snorkelling — USS Liberty shipwreck at Tulamben is 20 min away
- Less crowded than Seminyak/Canggu during school holidays

**EV tip**: Amed has only SPKLU AC charging (7 kW). Charge fully in Ubud before heading east, and confirm your accommodation has a Type 2 outlet.

## 4. Ho Chi Minh City → Da Lat Highland Escape (Vietnam)

**Distance**: ~300 km (4–5 hours drive)
**Best for**: Cool climate, pine forests, waterfalls, strawberry picking

Da Lat at 1,500 m elevation is a favourite June escape from Saigon's heat. VinFast's 2025–2026 charger rollout makes this a practical EV route for the first time.

**Charging stops**:
- HCMC → Binh Phuoc (130 km) — VinFast 150 kW
- Binh Phuoc → Bao Loc (120 km) — VinFast 150 kW at petrol station
- Bao Loc → Da Lat (60 km) — VinFast 60 kW at the entrance to Da Lat

**Family highlights**:
- Bao Loc waterfall viewpoint
- Da Lat Flower Garden
- Crazy House (Hang Nga) — bizarre, surreal, kids love it
- Strawberry picking at farms on the outskirts

**EV tip**: The climb from Bao Loc to Da Lat uses significant battery — expect 15–20% range loss on the ascent. Regen braking pays it back on the way down.

## 5. Manila → Subic → Baguio (Philippines)

**Distance**: ~280 km (with Baguio extension)
**Best for**: Beach + mountain combo, historical sites

The NLEX (North Luzon Expressway) and SCTEX (Subic-Clark-Tarlac Expressway) corridor is the Philippines' most EV-friendly long-distance route, with eVantage and GreenFuel 120 kW chargers at multiple stops.

**Charging stops**:
- Manila → Dau exit (90 km) — eVantage 120 kW at SM Clark
- Dau → Subic (50 km) — GreenFuel 60 kW at Subic Bay Freeport
- Subic → Baguio (140 km) — GreenFuel 120 kW at Tarlac

**Family highlights**:
- Subic Bay — Ocean Adventure, tree-top challenge, beach
- Baguio — Burnham Park, Mines View, strawberry shortcake at the public market
- Kennon Road — the scenic route to Baguio (watch for landslides in rain)

**EV tip**: Baguio's cold June climate (18–22°C) is actually optimal for EV battery range. Going uphill costs 15–20% range, but the cool air means no AC overhead.
