# Chiang Mai EV Charging Guide 2026: Complete Guide

Chiang Mai has emerged as Northern Thailand's leading city for EV adoption. With about **1.3 million residents** and over **2 million international visitors annually**, the cultural capital of the north now boasts reliable charging infrastructure across the city center, major shopping destinations, and luxury hotels. While the surrounding mountains require more planning, navigating Chiang Mai itself in an EV is refreshingly straightforward.

This guide covers every charger, network, cost, and insider trick you need to drive electric in Chiang Mai.

## Key Takeaways

- **100+ public charging points** across Chiang Mai city, concentrated at shopping malls and hotels
- **Central Festival and MAYA Lifestyle Mall** are the two primary fast-charging hubs, both with 50-150 kW CCS2
- **Cost**: ฿6-8/kWh ($0.17-0.22) at public DC chargers — cheaper than Bangkok malls because fewer tourist markups
- **Hotel charging is widespread** — most 4-5 star properties now offer complimentary or paid EV charging
- **CCS2 is the standard** — compatible with all modern EVs sold in Thailand
- **Plan ahead going north** — charging becomes sparse beyond the city limits toward Pai and Mae Hong Son
- **EV-friendly rating**: 3/5 — solid for city driving, challenging for mountain exploration

## Best Charging Locations by Area

### Old City (Mueang Chiang Mai)
The walled Old City has limited public fast-charging inside the moat itself, but several excellent options are a short drive from almost anywhere in the center:

- **MAYA Lifestyle Mall** (Nimman area, 5 min from Old City) — 8 × 50-150 kW EA Anywhere CCS2 chargers, plus 4 × 22 kW AC. BYD and MG also have branded bays here. **Most reliable charging hub in Chiang Mai.**
- **Tha Phae Gate area** — Limited on-street charging. Best option: Charge+ AC units at select cafes near Tha Phae Road.
- **Anantara Chiang Mai** — 2 dedicated EV bays with 22 kW AC charging for guests and restaurant visitors.
- **137 Pillars House** — 22 kW AC charging point for hotel guests.

### Nimmanhaemin (Nimman)
Chiang Mai's trendiest district has the best urban charging coverage:

- **MAYA Lifestyle Mall** — As above, the premier charging destination in the Nimman area.
- **Think Park / One Nimman** — 4 × 22 kW AC chargers by Charge+. Free for first 2 hours with purchase.
- **Nimman Hotel Zone** — Several boutique hotels (Hotel Indigo, Akyra Manor) offer guest EV charging.

### Airport & Superhighway
- **CentralPlaza Chiang Mai Airport** — 6 × 50-350 kW PTT EV Station PluZ CCS2 chargers. The fastest DC charging in the city.
- **PTT Station (Superhighway)** — 4 × 120 kW EA Anywhere chargers at the PTT on the Superhighway (Highway 11 ring road).
- **CH Airport Parking** — 4 × 22 kW AC charging bays in the airport long-stay car park.

### Outskirts & Surrounding Area
- **Promenada Resort Mall** (east of city) — 4 × 60 kW MG Charge CCS2 chargers.
- **CentralPlaza Chiang Mai Airport** (south) — Already covered above, but worth noting as the best charging option if entering the city from the south.
- **Lampang** (80 km south) — EA Anywhere 50 kW charger at the PTT on Highway 11, useful as the last fast-charging option before Chiang Mai from the south.
- **Doi Suthep / Doi Pui** — No public EV charging at the temples. Charge fully before ascending. The 16 km winding road up the mountain will consume about 15-20% of your battery.

## Must-Have Apps for Chiang Mai

1. **EA Anywhere** — The most useful for Chiang Mai. Covers MAYA, Central Festival, and PTT highway stations. Shows real-time availability.
2. **PTT Blue** — For the airport CentralPlaza chargers and PTT highway stations. Also functions as a fuel loyalty app.
3. **MG Charge** — Useful if you're driving an MG (BYD owners can use via roaming). Covers several mall locations.
4. **Charge+** — Good for AC charging at cafes, restaurants, and boutique hotels. Growing network in Nimman.
5. **Google Maps** — Filter by "EV charging" to find stations. Charging data is reasonably up-to-date for Chiang Mai as of 2026.

## Road Trip Prep: Charging Before Heading Out

Chiang Mai is a gateway to some of Thailand's most scenic road trips. Here's what to know before you go:

### Chiang Mai to Pai (150 km, 3 hours via Highway 1095)
**Warning:** This is one of Thailand's most challenging drives — 762 curves through mountain terrain. There is **no public DC fast charging** between Chiang Mai and Pai. Charge to 100% before leaving, and expect to use 40-50% of your battery for the journey. In Pai, some guesthouses offer 7-22 kW AC charging but availability is unreliable. A full charge back to Chiang Mai is essential before returning.

### Chiang Mai to Chiang Rai (180 km, 3 hours via Highway 118)
Better than the Pai road. The stretch has **one EA Anywhere charger** at a PTT station in **Mae Suai** (about 90 km from Chiang Mai). An EV with 350+ km range can do the round trip without charging, but topping up at Mae Suai gives comfortable margin. Chiang Rai has a few DC chargers at CentralPlaza Chiang Rai.

### Chiang Mai to Mae Hong Son (300 km, 6.5 hours)
The ultimate northern Thai road trip — and the most challenging for EVs. There are **no public DC fast chargers** on this route. Requires careful planning with hotel AC charging at overnight stops. For most EV owners, this trip is best done as a 2-3 day journey with pre-booked accommodation that offers charging.

## Tips for EV Driving in Chiang Mai

1. **Charge at Central Festival or MAYA before leaving the city** — These are the only reliable fast-charging hubs and can have queues during peak hours (5-7 PM).
2. **The Airport CentralPlaza has the fastest chargers** — PTT EV Station PluZ at CentralPlaza Airport delivers up to 350 kW. If your EV supports 200+ kW charging, this is the place.
3. **Avoid driving to Doi Inthanon without a full charge** — Thailand's highest peak (2,565 m) is a 60 km drive from the city with no charging at the summit. The ascent will drain 25-35% of your battery. Charge fully in Chom Thong or at the base.
4. **Sunday Walking Street is worth planning around** — The Old City is closed to traffic 4 PM-midnight on Sundays. Park at MAYA Mall (EV charge there) and walk or songthaew in.
5. **Rainy season (June-October)** — AC charging works fine in rain, but some outdoor DC chargers may be occupied by non-EV parkers seeking covered parking during storms.
6. **Burning season (February-April)** — Reduced visibility on mountain routes. Consider sticking to city-based EV exploration during these months.
