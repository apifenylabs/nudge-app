# South Korea EV Charging Guide 2026 — Networks, Highway Stops & Complete Route Planning

South Korea has one of the densest EV charging networks in Asia — you're never more than 30 km from a fast charger anywhere on the mainland. With the Korean government aggressively subsidizing EVs and charging infrastructure, 2026 is the best year yet for an EV road trip through Korea.

## The EV Charging Landscape in South Korea (2026)

South Korea's charging infrastructure has reached near-ubiquity:

- **240,000+** public charging points
- **45,000+** DC fast charging stations
- **Every expressway rest stop** has DC fast charging (100-350 kW)
- **CCS1** is the standard (not CCS2 — Korea and the US share CCS1)
- **CHAdeMO** still available at older stations for Nissan Leaf imports

### Major Charging Networks

| Network | Stations | Connectors | Payment |
|---------|----------|------------|---------|
| **EV Infra** (Government) | 90,000+ | CCS1, CHAdeMO | App / RFID |
| **KEPCO** (Korea Electric Power) | 45,000+ | CCS1 | App / Card |
| **SK Signet** | 30,000+ | CCS1, 350 kW | App |
| **LS E-Link** | 25,000+ | CCS1 | App / RFID |
| **Hyundai E-Pit** | 12,000+ | CCS1 (350 kW) | App / Card |
| **Kia Charge** | 8,000+ | CCS1 | App (Roaming) |

### The 3 Must-Have Apps

1. **EV Infra** — The biggest network, covers government-funded chargers. Free station registration, easy payment.
2. **Hyundai E-Pit** — Best for fast highway charging (350 kW). Membership gets you priority at busy stations.
3. **Kia Charge** — Roaming access to multiple networks through one app. Best for route planning.

Many stations also accept the **T-money** card (Korea's transit card) for tap-and-charge convenience.

## Expressway Charging (Korea's Superpower)

Korea's expressway rest areas (휴게소) are EV heaven. Unlike most countries where highway charging is an afterthought, **every single expressway rest stop in Korea has at least 4 DC fast chargers**, with busier ones having 8-12.

### Key Expressway Corridors

**Gyeongbu Expressway (Seoul → Busan, ~400 km)**
- Route: Seoul → Suwon → Cheonan → Daejeon → Daegu → Busan
- Charging: Every rest stop has 100-350 kW chargers
- Tip: Use Hyundai E-Pit stations for 350 kW charging — 18-minute full charge in compatible cars

**Honam Expressway (Seoul → Gwangju → Mokpo, ~330 km)**
- Route: Seoul → Cheonan → Iksan → Gwangju → Mokpo
- Charging: Excellent coverage, rest stops every 20-25 km
- Tip: The Iksan rest stop has a dedicated 12-bay EV charging plaza

**Yeongdong Expressway (Seoul → Gangneung, ~230 km)**
- Route: Seoul → Wonju → Pyeongchang → Gangneung (East Sea)
- Charging: Well-covered, chargers at every rest stop
- Tip: Popular ski route — winter range is reduced 25-30%

### Seoul Area Coverage

Seoul itself has excellent coverage:

- **Gangnam**: Chargers at COEX, Hyundai Department Store, and most office buildings
- **Hongdae / Mapo**: Public chargers in 30+ parking lots
- **Jongno**: Government offices have public chargers available 24/7
- **Apartments**: New buildings (post-2023) are required to have EV charging in 20% of parking spaces

## Best EV Road Trips from Seoul

### 1. Seoul → Busan (The Classic)
**Distance**: ~400 km  
**Charging**: 6+ rest stops with 350 kW chargers  
**Duration**: ~4.5 hours driving  
**Best for**: Korea's most famous road trip. Do it in a Hyundai Ioniq 6 or Kia EV6 — built for this.
**Stop**: Daejeon rest area for their famous hotteok (Korean pancakes) while you charge.

### 2. Seoul → Sokcho (East Coast)
**Distance**: ~200 km  
**Charging**: 3 rest stops, plus chargers at Sokcho   
**Duration**: ~2.5 hours  
**Best for**: Beach destination, Seoraksan National Park, fresh seafood
**Tip**: Use regen braking on the downhill approach to Sokcho to recover range.

### 3. Seoul → Jeonju → Suncheon (Southern Flavors)
**Distance**: ~400 km round trip  
**Charging**: Excellent coverage on Honam Expressway  
**Duration**: 3-day weekend  
**Best for**: Korean food pilgrimage. Jeonju is Korea's food capital.
**Tip**: Jeonju Hanok Village has several AC chargers — park and explore while charging.

### 4. Busan → Ulsan → Pohang → Gyeongju (Southeastern Loop)
**Distance**: ~250 km loop  
**Charging**: Every coastal town has public chargers  
**Best for**: Historical sites (Gyeongju), industrial marvel (Hyundai's EV factory in Ulsan), coastal scenery
**Tip**: Gyeongju's Bulguksa Temple area has free AC charging at the tourist parking lot.

## Charging Costs in Korea

Korea's charging costs vary by network:

- **EV Infra (Government)**: ~₩250/kWh ($0.18 USD) — cheapest
- **Hyundai E-Pit**: ~₩350/kWh ($0.25 USD) — fast but pricier
- **KEPCO**: ~₩280/kWh ($0.20 USD)
- **AC slow chargers**: Often free at hotels and malls

**Real-world cost**: A full charge for a Hyundai Ioniq 6 (77 kWh) costs approximately **₩19,250 ($14 USD)** at an EV Infra station.

Foreign drivers: You can pay with foreign credit cards at most stations (especially Hyundai E-Pit and KEPCO). T-money cards work everywhere.

## Practical Tips for Korea

### Language
- All major EV apps have English versions
- Highway rest stops have staff who can help (some English)
- Download Naver Maps or Kakao Maps — they show real-time charger status

### Winter Range (Important!)
Korean winters are cold. Expect:
- **20-30% range loss** in January (Seoul averages -5°C)
- **Use departure scheduling**: Pre-heat the cabin while plugged in
- **Find indoor chargers** in winter — Seoul has several underground charging plazas

### T-money for Charging
Tap your T-money card to start charging at government stations. Works like the subway — just tap, charge, tap again to stop.

### Hotel Charging
Most mid-range and luxury hotels offer AC charging. **Best chains for EV drivers**:
- **Lotte Hotels** — complimentary AC charging at most properties
- **Shinsegae Chosun** — DC fast chargers in Busan and Seoul
- **Holiday Inn** — growing network of AC chargers
- **Hanwha Resorts** — EV charging at all ski and beach properties

## Korea's EV Future (2026-2027)

- **Target**: 500,000 public chargers by 2027
- **Ultra-fast**: 800V 350 kW becoming standard at highway stops
- **V2G pilot**: Jeju Island testing bidirectional charging across 500 households
- **Wireless charging**: Pilot on Gwangju bus route — stationary inductive charging
- **Used EV export**: Korea becoming a major used EV exporter to Southeast Asia
