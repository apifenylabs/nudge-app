# Bangkok to Chiang Mai EV Road Trip Guide: Charging Stations, Costs & Tips

Driving from Bangkok to Chiang Mai in an electric vehicle is no longer a pipe dream. With Thailand's EV infrastructure expanding rapidly — over 10,000 public charging points nationwide as of early 2026 — the 695 km journey up Highway 1 (Phahonyothin Road) is now one of Southeast Asia's most practical and scenic EV road trips.

This guide covers every charging stop, the apps you need, estimated costs, and insider tips for a smooth journey from the capital to the "Rose of the North."

## Key Takeaways

- The 695 km Bangkok–Chiang Mai route can be completed with just **2–3 charging stops** in a modern EV with 400+ km range
- Total charging cost is approximately **THB 500–900** (USD $14–25), versus THB 1,800–2,500 for petrol
- PEA Volta, EA Anywhere, and MG Charge are the dominant networks along Highway 1, offering **50 kW to 360 kW DC fast chargers**
- The most strategic mid-point stop is **Nakhon Sawan** (240 km from Bangkok), followed by **Phitsanulok** or **Kamphaeng Phet** for northern leg
- Thailand's EV charging coverage is projected to reach 90% of Highway 1 by end of 2026

## Route Overview: Bangkok to Chiang Mai

The journey follows AH2/Route 1 north through the central plains, then climbs into the foothills of northern Thailand. The terrain is mostly flat for the first 400 km, with gentle elevation gain after Phitsanulok.

## Charging Networks on the Route

### 1. PEA Volta
Thailand's state-enterprise EV charging network is the most reliable along Highway 1. Their Nakhon Sawan station offers 4 CCS2 connectors at 120 kW each. Payment via the PEA Volta app or QR code at the station.

**Cost:** THB 6.50–8.00 per kWh
**App:** PEA Volta (iOS/Android)

### 2. EA Anywhere
Energy Absolute's network has expanded aggressively. Their Phitsanulok station is a flagship site with 6 stalls, including some 150 kW units. The EA Anywhere app supports advance reservation.

**Cost:** THB 7.00 per kWh (EA members get 10% discount)
**App:** EA Anywhere (iOS/Android)

### 3. MG Charge
MG's network covers many provincial towns. The Lampang station is conveniently located near the city center with slower but reliable 60–90 kW chargers — ideal for a meal break.

**Cost:** THB 6.00 per kWh (free for MG owners during first year)
**App:** MG Charge (iOS/Android)

### 4. Shell Recharge (new in 2026)
Shell has opened two ultra-fast (200 kW+) stations along Route 1 near Ayutthaya and Tak. While slightly off the main route, they offer the fastest charging speeds.

**Cost:** THB 8.50 per kWh
**App:** Shell Recharge (iOS/Android)

## Essential Apps for the Trip

1. **PEA Volta** — For locating and activating PEA chargers along Highway 1
2. **EA Anywhere** — Pre-book charging slots at Phitsanulok and other major stations
3. **MG Charge** — Provincial coverage including Lampang and Tak
4. **ChargeV** — A Thai aggregator app showing all networks on one map with real-time availability
5. **Google Maps** — With EV routing enabled (now supports Thailand), it can suggest charging stops based on your battery level
6. **LINE MAN / Grab** — For food delivery to the charging station while you wait

## Where to Stop for Meals While Charging

The beauty of Thailand's Highway 1 is the abundance of excellent roadside food. Here are the best spots near charging stations:

- **Nakhon Sawan:** Try the famous *Khao Soi* (a different take from Chiang Mai's version!) at Pa Nuan restaurant, 5 minutes from the PEA Volta station
- **Phitsanulok:** The EA Anywhere station is near the city's night market area. Try *Khao Tom Pla Khao* (herbal fish soup) while your car charges
- **Lampang:** MG Charge is near the famous chicken rice spots on Thip Chang Market Road

## Seasonal Considerations

### Cool Season (Nov–Feb) — BEST TIME
Temperatures along the route range 15–30°C. Expect your EV's real-world range to be closest to the WLTP rating. Clear skies make for stunning views approaching Chiang Mai, especially the Doi Khun Tan mountain pass.

### Hot Season (Mar–May)
Temperatures can reach 40°C+ in the central plains. Running the A/C at full blast may reduce range by 10–15%. Plan an extra 15-minute charging stop. Try departing before 7 AM to avoid peak heat.

### Rainy Season (Jun–Oct)
Expect heavy downpours, especially in the late afternoon. Wet roads reduce efficiency slightly. Flooding near Ayutthaya and Sing Buri is possible — check route conditions on the Thailand Highway Department's app. PEA Volta stations are well-sheltered.

## Expert Tips for the Drive

1. **Charge before leaving Bangkok** at EV chargers in the PTT gas stations along Vibhavadi Rangsit Road or at Central Ladprao shopping mall
2. **Avoid charging at Ayutthaya** (50 km out) — the chargers there are often busy with day-trippers
3. **The climb into Chiang Mai** from Lampang (the "Khun Tan" range) can reduce range by 8–10% on the ascent but you'll recoup some on regenerative braking on the descent
4. **Book accommodation with charging** in Chiang Mai — many hotels in the Nimman and Old City areas now offer overnight AC charging
5. **Join the "EV Thailand" Facebook group** for real-time reports on charger availability
6. **Carry a Type 2 to CCS2 adapter** for AC destination chargers at hotels

## What to Do in Chiang Mai on Arrival

Once you arrive with a depleted battery (or some charge if you stopped at Lampang), you'll find excellent charging options in the city:

- **Central Festival Chiang Mai:** PEA Volta with 6 DC fast chargers (120 kW) in the parking garage
- **Maya Lifestyle Mall:** EA Anywhere with 4 chargers (150 kW)
- **Nimman area hotels:** Many boutique hotels offer complimentary 7 kW AC charging for guests
- **PTT gas stations (outer ring):** PEA Volta units at several locations

While charging, explore the Old City's temples, hit the Sunday Night Walking Street, or plan a day trip to Doi Inthanon National Park (Thailand's highest peak) — just be aware that requires careful range planning for the mountainous terrain.

## Bottom Line: Is the Bangkok–Chiang Mai EV Road Trip Worth It?

Absolutely. With the current charging infrastructure in 2026, the route is comfortable for any EV with 300+ km real-world range. You'll save 75–80% on fuel costs compared to petrol, enjoy newer and more comfortable cars (many EVs have excellent highway-assist features perfect for Thailand's long straight highways), and arrive in Chiang Mai relaxed rather than exhausted.

The key is planning your charging stops in advance — don't rely on finding a charger at every PTT station. Use the ChargeV aggregator app, bookmark your stops, and the journey becomes as enjoyable as the destination.
