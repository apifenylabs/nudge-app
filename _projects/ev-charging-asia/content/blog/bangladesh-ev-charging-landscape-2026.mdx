# EV Charging in Bangladesh: Current Landscape, Key Players, and Future Outlook 2026

**Last updated: May 24, 2026**

Bangladesh is one of South Asia's most surprising EV stories. While most coverage focuses on India and China, this delta nation of 170 million people is quietly building an electric vehicle ecosystem that's unlike anywhere else in Asia. The key difference? Bangladesh's EV revolution isn't starting with cars — it's being driven by millions of electric 3-wheelers.

From the chaotic streets of Dhaka to the port city of Chattogram, from the tea gardens of Sylhet to the Cox's Bazar beach road, electric mobility is reshaping how Bangladesh moves. But if you're an EV owner planning to drive in Bangladesh, or a traveller wondering whether you can charge your EV there, the reality is nuanced: booming 3-wheeler adoption, nascent car EV infrastructure, and a government that's finally getting serious about charging networks.

In this guide, we cover:

- Bangladesh's unique 3-wheeler EV revolution
- The car EV market and what you can drive
- Charging infrastructure in Dhaka, Chattogram, and the highway
- Government policy and what it means for EV owners
- Challenges: grid reliability, connectors, and coverage gaps
- The future: DC fast charging, solar chargers, and ADB projects
- Practical tips for the rare EV traveller in Bangladesh

## The 3-Wheeler Revolution: Bangladesh's EV Backbone

**Easy bikes, auto-rickshaws, and Nasir Glass**

If you walk through any Bangladeshi city, you'll hear them before you see them: the distinctive whir of an electric 3-wheeler. These are **easy bikes** (electric rickshaw-vans) and **battery-powered auto-rickshaws**, and they are the backbone of Bangladesh's EV revolution.

**Key numbers:**

| Metric | Figure |
|--------|:------:|
| Electric 3-wheelers on the road (2026 est.) | 100,000+ |
| Nasir Glass (NGL) EV production capacity | 50,000+ units/year |
| Battery swapping stations nationwide | 500+ |
| Average swap time | 2-5 minutes |
| Cost per swap | ৳50-120 (~$0.45-1.10) |

The most remarkable story is **Nasir Glass** — a glass manufacturer that diversified into EV production. The company's NGL brand produces thousands of electric 3-wheelers each year from its factory in Dhaka's Hemayetpur area. These vehicles have become the default last-mile transport option in cities across Bangladesh, replacing CNG-run auto-rickshaws with zero-emission alternatives.

**How 3-wheelers charge: battery swapping, not plug-in**

Unlike car EVs, Bangladesh's 3-wheelers use **battery swapping** almost exclusively. Riders pull into a swap station, hand over their depleted lithium-ion battery, and get a fully charged one in minutes. This model works brilliantly in Bangladesh because:

- **No grid dependency at the point of use** — swap stations can charge batteries during off-peak hours
- **Zero downtime** — no waiting 4-8 hours for a slow charge
- **Lower upfront cost** — riders don't buy the battery, just the vehicle
- **Swappable standards** — most easy bikes use compatible 60V or 72V battery packs

Companies like **BD EV Charging Co.**, **Solargao**, and local startups have set up swap stations across Dhaka, Chattogram, Rajshahi, and Khulna. The system is so efficient that some estimates suggest battery swapping covers over 90% of 3-wheeler energy needs in Bangladesh.

> **Side note for travellers:** If you see a row of easy bikes lined up at a small roadside stall with a metal cabinet and a sign in Bengali — that's a battery swap station. They're often unrecognisable to foreign visitors, but they outnumber petrol pumps in many areas.

## The Car EV Market: Small but Growing

While 3-wheelers dominate, car EV adoption in Bangladesh is very much in its infancy. We estimate that as of May 2026, there are **roughly 500–1,000 electric cars** registered in the entire country — a tiny fraction of the 4+ million vehicles on Bangladesh's roads.

**What EVs are available in Bangladesh?**

Bangladesh's car EV market is driven almost entirely by imports, and the options are limited:

| Model | How it arrives | Est. Price (BDT) | Notes |
|-------|---------------|:----------------:|-------|
| BYD Atto 3 | Official import (BYD Bangladesh) | ৳45-55 lakh ($37K-45K) | Most popular new EV option |
| BYD Dolphin | Official import | ৳35-42 lakh ($29K-35K) | Compact city car |
| MG ZS EV | Official import (MG Bangladesh) | ৳50-60 lakh ($41K-49K) | Decent dealer network |
| Nissan Leaf (used) | Japanese reconditioned imports | ৳20-30 lakh ($16K-25K) | Most common affordable option |
| Tesla Model 3/Y | Grey market from UAE/Singapore | ৳1-2 crore ($82K-165K) | Very rare, no official support |

**Official distributors:** BYD Bangladesh (operated by CG Corporation) and MG Bangladesh (by Navana Group) are the two major players with showrooms in Dhaka. Both offer warranty and limited after-sales support for EVs.

**Japanese reconditioned imports** are a major channel — Bangladesh is one of the world's largest markets for used Japanese cars, and the Nissan Leaf (especially 2018-2022 models) is the most commonly imported EV. You'll find them at dealerships in Dhaka's Motijheel and Tejgaon areas.

**Tesla and other premium EVs** arrive through grey-market importers in Uttara and Gulshan, but with no Supercharger network and no official Tesla presence, they're more status symbols than practical daily drivers.

### Dhaka-Chattogram Highway Charging

The 240 km Dhaka-Chattogram highway (N1) is the most important road in Bangladesh, carrying the bulk of the country's freight and passenger traffic. EV charging along this route is almost non-existent, but there is one notable waypoint:

**Comilla Waypoint (BD EVC)** — Approximately halfway between Dhaka and Chattogram, near the Comilla Cantonment, there is a single AC Type 2 charger installed by BD EV Charging Co. It's the only public charger on this entire 240 km stretch.

**Charging strategy on the Dhaka-Chattogram highway:**

1. **Start full** — Make sure you leave Dhaka with a full charge
2. **Top up at Comilla** if needed — the midpoint charger is your only option
3. **Destination charging** — Book hotels with chargers in Chattogram
4. **Backup plan** — Carry a Type 2 to Type 2 cable and ask at roadside restaurants

## Government Policy: The EV Policy 2024-2030

Bangladesh's government has recognised that EVs are the future, and after years of slow movement, policy is now accelerating.

### Bangladesh EV Policy 2024-2030

Launched in 2024, this is the country's first comprehensive EV policy framework:

| Target | Timeline | Status |
|--------|:--------:|:------:|
| 30% of all registered vehicles to be EVs | By 2030 | On track for 3-wheelers, behind for cars |
| EV charging stations in all 64 districts | By 2028 | ~15 districts covered in 2026 |
| DC fast charger network on major highways | By 2027 | Under development |
| Mandatory EV charging in new housing/retail | By 2026 | Partial implementation |

### Import Duty Reductions

The single most impactful policy change has been the reduction of import duties on CBU (Completely Built Up) EVs:

| Component | Old Duty | New Duty (2025) |
|-----------|:--------:|:---------------:|
| CBU EV import duty | 89% | 26% |
| EV parts/components | 25% | 5-10% |
| Charging equipment | 15-25% | 5% (duty-free for select items) |
| Lithium-ion battery imports | 25% | 10% |

This duty reduction has slashed EV prices by 30-40% virtually overnight. A BYD Atto 3 that would have cost ৳75 lakh ($61K) in 2023 now costs around ৳50 lakh ($41K) — still expensive by Bangladeshi standards, but increasingly competitive with petrol SUVs in the same segment.

### Other Policy Measures

- **Tax holiday for charging station operators** — 5-year corporate tax exemption for new charging businesses
- **Green energy mandates** — New charging stations must source at least 30% of electricity from renewable sources
- **Public sector EV targets** — Government ministries to convert 25% of their fleets to EVs by 2027
- **SUSTAINABLE AND RENEWABLE ENERGY DEVELOPMENT AUTHORITY (SREDA)** — Leading the infrastructure rollout

## Challenges

Bangladesh's EV journey faces significant hurdles that make it different from more mature markets like Thailand or Malaysia.

### 1. Grid Reliability

**Load shedding is a reality.** Bangladesh's power grid struggles during the summer months (March-June), when temperatures hit 38-42°C and air conditioner demand spikes. Load shedding (planned power cuts) can last 2-4 hours a day in urban areas and longer in rural regions.

This creates a chicken-and-egg problem for charging: you can't build many chargers if the grid can't support them, and you can't justify grid upgrades without charger demand.

**Adaptation:** Many charging stations now include battery storage — charging their own batteries overnight during off-peak hours and dispensing energy during the day. Some use solar panels to supplement grid supply.

### 2. Import Dependency

Bangladesh has no domestic EV car manufacturing. Every EV on the road is imported — either as a CBU from China/Japan or through the grey market. This means:
- **Parts availability** is poor — wait times for replacement parts can be weeks
- **Service expertise** is limited to a handful of mechanics trained on specific models
- **No warranty coverage** for grey-market vehicles

### 3. Connector Chaos

Bangladesh has not adopted a single charging standard. You'll find:

| Connector Type | Used By | Prevalence |
|:--------------:|---------|:----------:|
| **Type 2 (Mennekes)** | BD EVCC, PowerPac, most hotels | Most common |
| **GB/T** | Some PowerPac units, Chinese imports | Growing |
| **CCS-2** | Virtually none | Planned |
| **CHAdaMO** | None | Not used |

**Reality:** There is **no CCS or CHAdeMO DC fast charging** publicly available anywhere in Bangladesh as of May 2026. All public charging for car EVs is Level 2 AC (3.7-22 kW), meaning a full charge from empty takes 4-8 hours even on a fast AC unit.

### 4. Geographic Concentration

Almost all charging infrastructure is concentrated in **Dhaka and Chattogram**. The rest of Bangladesh — including Sylhet, Rajshahi, Khulna, Cox's Bazar, and Barisal — has minimal to no public EV charging for cars. This makes intercity EV travel effectively impossible without careful hotel booking.

## Future Outlook

Despite the challenges, the next 2-3 years look transformative for Bangladesh's EV ecosystem.

### DC Fast Charging is Coming

The government's EV Policy 2024-2030 explicitly targets DC fast charger deployment. **SREDA** (Sustainable and Renewable Energy Development Authority) is leading a project to install the first CCS-2 DC fast chargers in Bangladesh, with an initial batch of 10 units expected by late 2026:

| Location | Planned DC Charger | Timeline |
|----------|:-------------------:|:--------:|
| Dhaka (Kawran Bazar) | 60-120 kW CCS-2 | Q3 2026 |
| Dhaka-Chattogram Highway (Comilla) | 60 kW CCS-2 | Q4 2026 |
| Chattogram (GEC Circle) | 60 kW CCS-2 | Q4 2026 |
| Sylhet City | 60 kW CCS-2 | Q1 2027 |
| Dhaka-Mymensingh Highway | 60 kW CCS-2 | 2027 |

### Solar-Powered Charging

Bangladesh is already a solar energy success story — over 6 million solar home systems have been installed in rural areas, making it one of the world's largest off-grid solar markets. This expertise is being applied to EV charging:

- **Olomax Energy** and **Solarland** are piloting solar-powered charging stations with battery storage
- These stations can operate independent of the grid — a game-changer for rural charging
- The **Dhaka-Chattogram highway** has been identified as a priority corridor for solar EV charging

### ADB-Funded Projects

The **Asian Development Bank (ADB)** has approved a $100 million project for sustainable transport in Bangladesh, which includes:
- Deployment of 50+ public EV charging stations in 5 divisional cities
- Technical assistance for grid integration of EV charging load
- Capacity building for the Bangladesh Road Transport Authority (BRTA) on EV registration and standards

### Dhaka-Chattogram Expressway

The proposed **Dhaka-Chattogram Expressway** (a 220 km, 4-lane controlled-access highway) will include EV charging in its design specifications. If built as planned, it would be the first highway in Bangladesh with integrated fast charging — similar to the expressway model in Cambodia and the Trans-Java Toll Road in Indonesia.

### What's Realistic for the Average EV Owner?

**By 2027:** Dhaka should have 50-80 public chargers (still mostly AC), with DC fast charging available in 3-4 locations. Chattogram and Sylhet will have limited but functional charging options.

**By 2028:** The Dhaka-Chattogram corridor should support EV travel with 3-4 charging stops. DC fast charging may reach 60 kW in major cities.

**By 2030:** The government's target of 30% EVs (dominated by 3-wheelers) is achievable. Car EVs may reach 10,000-15,000 units — still tiny but usable for city driving.

> **Reality check:** Bangladesh is roughly where Thailand was in 2019-2020 in terms of car EV charging infrastructure. The difference is that Bangladesh is leapfrogging directly to CCS-2 and solar integration, skipping some of the early fragmentation that other markets experienced.

### What to Carry

For EV travel in Bangladesh, pack:

| Item | Why |
|------|-----|
| **Type 2 to Type 2 cable (7.5m+)** | Essential for all hotel AC chargers |
| **Type 2 to Type 1 adapter** | If driving an older Leaf with Type 1 inlet |
| **Portable EVSE (Schuko plug)** | Emergency charging from any 16A wall socket |
| **Universal power adapter** | Bangladesh uses Type D and Type G sockets (230V, 50Hz) |
| **Extension cord (heavy duty)** | Some chargers are awkwardly placed |
| **Offline maps (Google Maps)** | Data coverage is good in cities, patchy on highways |
| **Local SIM** | Grameenphone or Robi — essential for charging app registration |
| **Paper map** | Yes, really — GPS routing can be unreliable outside cities |

### Emergency Backup Plan

Given the sparse charging infrastructure, every EV traveller in Bangladesh needs a backup plan:

1. **Know your hotel charging status** before departure
2. **Check PlugShare** for the latest check-ins — the community is small but active
3. **Carry a Type 2 cable** — many hotel chargers exist but users must provide their own cable
4. **Learn the Bengali words for "electric car charging"** — ইলেকট্রিক গাড়ির চার্জিং (pronounced "electric garir charging") can help you ask for help at hotels and petrol stations
5. **Join the Bangladesh EV Owners Facebook group** — a small but helpful community that shares real-time charger updates
6. **Don't let your battery go below 20%** without knowing your next charging option — charging stations are too sparse to risk it
