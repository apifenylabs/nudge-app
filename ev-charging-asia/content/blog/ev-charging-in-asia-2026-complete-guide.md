# EV Charging Apps and Payment Guide for Asia — Which Ones You Actually Need

One of the biggest hassles of EV road tripping across Asia is the apps. Every country has different networks, each with its own app, payment system, and account setup. Here's what you actually need to download before you hit the road.

## Key Takeaways
- Every Asian country needs a different combination of charging apps — there's no single universal solution
- Japan requires an e-Mobility Power RFID card, best ordered before you arrive
- Singapore is the simplest market — most chargers support tap-and-go credit cards
- Thailand needs both EA Anywhere and PTT EV Station PluZ apps with pre-loaded balance
- China requires WeChat or Alipay for payment — foreign credit cards rarely work

## Japan

**Must-have:** e-Mobility Power app or RFID card. This is the most widely accepted network covering CHAdeMO and CCS2. Many stations also accept credit cards directly.

**Secondary:** ENECHANGE app for newer stations.

**Payment:** Credit card or app-based. Some older stations require prepaid cards.

**Tip:** Get the e-Mobility Power RFID card mailed to you before arriving — it works without a smartphone, which is crucial in areas with poor mobile data.

## Singapore

**Must-have:** SP Group app for the largest network. Charge+ app for their growing fleet.

**Payment:** Credit card works at most SP Group chargers. Some require the app for activation.

**Note:** Singapore has the simplest payment system in Asia for EV charging. Most chargers support tap-and-go credit card — refreshingly straightforward.

## Thailand

**Must-have:** EA Anywhere app (Energy Absolute) — covers Siam Paragon, CentralWorld, ICONSIAM, and most highway chargers.

**Secondary:** PTT EV Station PluZ app — crucial for highway stops at PTT gas stations.

**Payment:** App top-up with credit card. Some accept QR payment via PromptPay.

**Tip:** Both apps need an account with pre-loaded balance. Register and top up before your road trip — don't wait until you're at the charger.

## Malaysia

**Must-have:** Gentari Go app — covers KLCC, Mid Valley, and most highway R&R stops. ChargeEV app for Pavilion and Penang.

**Payment:** Credit card in-app. Some Gentari chargers now support direct credit card tap.

**Good news:** Cross-border roaming with Singapore networks is being tested, which will make Singapore-Malaysia road trips much smoother.

## China

**Must-have:** WeChat or Alipay — these aren't charging apps but you need them for payment. Most chargers activate via WeChat mini-programs or Alipay.

**Specific apps:** State Grid EV app (国家电网), NIO Power app, BYD app.

**Payment:** WeChat Pay or Alipay exclusively. Foreign credit cards rarely work.

**Note:** You'll need a Chinese phone number to register. Consider getting a temporary Chinese SIM before your trip.

## General Tips

1. Download and register all apps at home with WiFi — don't wait until you're at the charger
2. Carry a backup credit card that works internationally
3. Some apps need SMS verification — ensure your phone can receive SMS abroad
4. Check our station pages for which payment methods each station accepts
5. Keep screenshots of QR codes for offline access

Asia's EV payment landscape is still fragmented, but it's getting better every year. The key is preparation: download the apps, create accounts, and load balance before you hit the road. A little planning goes a long way toward a stress-free EV road trip.
