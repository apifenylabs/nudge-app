# Tokyo with Kids 2026 — Family Itinerary, Hotels, Attractions & Food Guide

Tokyo can feel overwhelming for first-time family visitors. It's massive, densely packed, and the cultural differences can be intimidating. But Tokyo is also one of the most kid-friendly cities on the planet — incredibly safe, impeccably clean, and filled with whimsical attractions that children adore.

This guide covers the best family attractions, hotels, food strategies, day trips, and a sample 5-day itinerary that we've tested with our own kids.

---

## Kid-Friendly Attractions in Tokyo

### 1. Tokyo Disneyland & DisneySea

**Best for:** All ages (but especially 3–12)
**Price:** ~$55/adult, ~$40/kid (buy on Klook in advance)
**Time needed:** 1–2 full days

Tokyo Disneyland is the most-visited theme park in the world for a reason. It's impeccably maintained, has shorter lines than its US counterparts, and offers unique Japanese-only attractions. DisneySea is unique to Tokyo and more adventurous.

**Family Tip:** Buy tickets on [Klook](https://www.klook.com) at least 2 weeks in advance — weekend tickets sell out. Go on a Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday for shortest queues.

### 2. Sanrio Puroland (Hello Kitty Theme Park)

**Best for:** Ages 2–8 (especially Hello Kitty fans)
**Price:** ~$25/adult, ~$20/kid
**Time needed:** 3–4 hours

An indoor theme park entirely dedicated to Sanrio characters. Indoor parades, gentle rides, character meet-and-greets, and Hello Kitty's house. Everything is adorable and toddler-friendly.

**Family Tip:** Located in Tama Center, 30 minutes from Shinjuku on the Keio Line. Go early (opens 10 AM).

### 3. TeamLab Planets Tokyo

**Best for:** All ages (2+)
**Price:** ~$25/adult, ~$15/kid (under 3 free)
**Time needed:** 1.5–2 hours

An immersive digital art experience where kids wade through water, walk through projected flower fields, and interact with light installations. It's Instagram-worthy for parents and magical for kids.

**Family Tip:** Book timed-entry tickets in advance. Go at opening (9 AM) or late afternoon to avoid crowds. Kids will get wet — bring a change of clothes.

### 4. Ueno Zoo & Ueno Park

**Best for:** All ages
**Price:** ~$5/adult (incredible value), kids under 12 FREE
**Time needed:** 2–3 hours

Japan's oldest zoo, home to giant pandas (Ling Ling and Shuan Shuan), a petting zoo, and a monorail through the park. Ueno Park also has a massive playground, paddle boats on the pond, and street performers on weekends.

**Family Tip:** Combine with the National Museum of Nature and Science (next door) and the Ameya-Yokocho market street for lunch.

### 5. Odaiba — Family Entertainment Island

**Best for:** All ages
**Price:** Variable (some free, some paid attractions)
**Time needed:** Full day

Odaiba is a man-made island packed with family attractions:
- **Joypolis** — Indoor SEGA-themed amusement park ($30/adult)
- **Miraikan** — National Museum of Emerging Science ($10/adult)
- **Palette Town** — Ferris wheel with rainbow lights ($10/adult)
- **Legoland Discovery Center** ($20/kid)
- **Daiba Park** — Free playground with Tokyo Bay views

**Family Tip:** Take the Yurikamome driverless train from Shimbashi — kids love riding at the front.

### 6. Ghibli Museum

**Best for:** Ages 4+ (Ghibli fans)
**Price:** ~$10/adult, ~$5/kid
**Time needed:** 2–3 hours

A whimsical museum dedicated to Studio Ghibli (Spirited Away, My Neighbor Totoro, Howl's Moving Castle). No photography allowed inside, but the rooftop garden with the robot soldier from Castle in the Sky is unforgettable.

**Family Tip:** Tickets go on sale on the 10th of each month for the following month. They sell out within hours. Buy through a proxy service or schedule your trip around availability.

## Best Family Hotels in Tokyo

### Luxury Pick
**Keio Plaza Hotel Tokyo** — $250–500/night, Shinjuku. Family rooms sleep 4, multiple pools, direct airport limousine bus. Great location near Shinjuku Gyoen park.

### Mid-Range Favorites
**Hotel Gracery Shinjuku** — $150–300/night, Shinjuku (Godzilla building!). Fun for kids who love Godzilla. The giant Godzilla head on the roof is visible from rooms. Family rooms available.

**Shinjuku Granbell Hotel** — $120–250/night, Shinjuku. Modern, clean, family rooms with bunk beds. Walking distance to Omoide Yokocho (memory lane) for family-friendly street food.

**Mitsui Garden Hotel Ginza Premier** — $180–350/night, Ginza. Spacious rooms, great for families wanting a quieter area. Near Hama-rikyu Gardens and Tsukiji Outer Market.

### Budget Options
**APA Hotels** — $80–150/night, various locations. Compact but clean. Great for families on a tight budget. Look for locations in Ueno or Asakusa.

**Tokyu Stay** — $100–180/night, various locations. Rooms with kitchenettes — perfect for families with picky eaters who want to prepare simple meals.

### Best for Toddlers
**Tokyo Disneyland Hotels** — $300–600/night. If you're doing Disney, stay on property. Monorail connection, early park entry, character-themed rooms.

## Sample 5-Day Tokyo Family Itinerary

### Day 1: Shinjuku & Harajuku
- **Morning:** Arrive, check in. Lunch at a family restaurant (Gusto or Jonathan's — kid-friendly menus)
- **Afternoon:** Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden (sprawling park, playground, ice cream)
- **Evening:** Harajuku's Takeshita Street — crepes, character shops, street performers

### Day 2: Disney Day
- **Full day:** Tokyo Disneyland or DisneySea
- **Dinner:** Ikspiari (Disney-owned shopping complex) — more dining options than the park

### Day 3: Asakusa, Ueno & Akihabara
- **Morning:** Senso-ji Temple in Asakusa + Nakamise Street (souvenir shopping, street snacks)
- **Afternoon:** Ueno Zoo + Park playground
- **Evening:** Akihabara — arcades, gacha machines, Anime cafes (take a 20-minute break at a cat café)

### Day 4: Odaiba
- **Full day:** Yurikamome train → Miraikan → Palette Town → Legoland → Daiba Park
- **Evening:** Rainbow-colored Ferris wheel at sunset

### Day 5: TeamLab & Departure
- **Morning:** TeamLab Planets
- **Afternoon:** Last-minute shopping & airport transfer

## Food Tips for Kids in Tokyo

Tokyo has more Michelin stars than any other city, but kids need kid-friendly food. Here's what works:

- **Convenience stores (FamilyMart, 7-Eleven, Lawson)** — Onigiri (rice balls), egg sandwiches, fruit packs, yogurt. Perfect for picky eaters. $3–5 per meal
- **Family restaurants** — Gusto, Jonathan's, Denny's Japan. Menus with pictures, kid's sets with toys, high chairs. $8–15 per person
- **Tonkatsu (fried pork cutlet)** — Breaded and fried — kids love it. Go to Maisen in Harajuku
- **Udon/soba** — Simple noodles in broth. Easy for kids. Try Udon Shin in Shinjuku
- **Ikebukuro's Sunshine City food court** — Huge selection, something for everyone

**What NOT to do:** Don't expect kids to love sushi (raw fish) or natto (fermented soybeans). Don't walk and eat in Japan. Stand at the shop to eat, then continue walking.

## Family Tips for Tokyo

- **Get a Suica or Pasmo card** — Tap on/off trains, buses, and convenience stores. Kids 6–11 get half-price
- **Stroller tip** — Bring a compact stroller. Tokyo subways are crowded and some stations have limited elevators
- **Nursing rooms** — Major department stores (Isetan, Takashimaya) have excellent baby rooms with changing tables, microwaves, and nursing chairs
- **WiFi** — Rent a pocket WiFi at the airport ($3–5/day). Most hotels offer free mobile phones too
- **Taxis are expensive** — Use the excellent train system. A family day pass is $10–15 total
- **Book Disney tickets early** — They sell out weeks ahead. [Klook](https://www.klook.com) is the best third-party option

## The Verdict

Tokyo is surprisingly easy with kids. The city is safe, clean, and filled with attractions that delight children. Budget $300–500/day for a family of four (mid-range hotel, mix of attraction and free activities, convenience store meals plus one restaurant meal). Stay in Shinjuku or Ueno for the best balance of convenience and value.
